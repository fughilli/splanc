# Splanc Mini engineering revision

USB-PD-only Mini is implemented as a distinct target in the shared hardware
project. Battery's `default` and `factory_2s` entries are retained. The preserved
battery-capable checkpoint is `8c68c24` on `splanc-manufacturing`; Mini development
is on `splanc-mini`. No remote branch has been published by this work.

## Circuit and enclosure

The circuit has 136 fitted components. USB-C feeds the TPS25730D protected sink
path, which directly feeds the TPS552882 5 V converter. The existing converter
is retained for input-voltage flexibility. The 3.3 V regulator, two switched and
monitored LED ports, ESP32-C6, ICM-42670-P, MMC5603NJ, BMP580, and 1.8 V T5848
microphone chain are populated. Battery charger, gauge, source selector, XT30,
thermistor interfaces, and battery assembly straps are absent.

The former charge indicator is now an active-low status LED on GPIO10.
GPIO11 and GPIO15 are unused on Mini. Firmware must use the Mini pin map,
not the Battery source-selector/charger definitions. MINI-EOL-V1 exposes
20 bottom-side test pads, including four ground contacts.
It is incompatible with the legacy header. Fixture power enters USB-C and
test loads use the LED connectors. A new 10 kΩ GPIO8 pullup makes ROM download
mode deterministic when the fixture asserts BOOT_N.

The compact outline is 70 × 55 mm, four layers: 30.3% less area than the
85 × 65 mm study. Four 2.7 mm nonplated M2.5 mounts have all-layer copper
keepouts. The two LED ports repeat the same
13-part placement at 11 mm pitch along the right edge. Side-actuated buttons,
the two-indicator light-pipe row, antenna clearance, and microphone acoustic
opening remain. The Mini enclosure must omit the battery connector openings.
Dimensions are provisional until physical enclosure and thermal validation.

## Build and reproduce

The physical/electrical interface is specified in
`docs/hardware/splanc-mini-eol-interface.md`; its shared JSON lives in
`hardware/interfaces/mini-eol-v1.json`. Both footprints and the passive carrier
are generated by `generate_mini_eol.py` and `make_mini_eol_contact_board.py`.
The active tester, its protection/interlocks and fixture mechanics remain
engineering work; the old tester must not be wired straight to these pads.

Run from the repository root. `KICAD_PYTHON` denotes a Python interpreter with
KiCad's `pcbnew` module; normal `python3` needs the PnR Python dependencies.

```sh
python3 hardware/tools/generate_mini_eol.py --check
cd hardware/splanc_dev
ato build -b mini --no-standardize-designators
cd ../..
"$KICAD_PYTHON" hardware/tools/restore_splanc_thermal_pads.py hardware/splanc_dev/elec/layout/mini/mini.kicad_pcb
PYTHONPATH=hardware/pnr "$KICAD_PYTHON" -m pnr.ingest hardware/splanc_dev/elec/layout/mini/mini.kicad_pcb --dump-json /tmp/mini-graph.json
python3 hardware/tools/place_splanc_mini.py /tmp/mini-graph.json --output-dir /tmp/mini-placement
PYTHONPATH=hardware/pnr "$KICAD_PYTHON" -m pnr.writeback hardware/splanc_dev/elec/layout/mini/mini.kicad_pcb /tmp/mini-placement/mini-placed.json --out /tmp/mini-placement/mini.kicad_pcb --rules /tmp/mini-placement/mini-rules.json
"$KICAD_PYTHON" hardware/tools/check_splanc_power.py /tmp/mini-placement/mini.kicad_pcb --mini --require-layout
"$KICAD_PYTHON" hardware/tools/make_mini_eol_contact_board.py --output /tmp/mini-fixture/mini-eol-carrier.kicad_pcb
"$KICAD_PYTHON" hardware/tools/check_mini_eol.py /tmp/mini-placement/mini.kicad_pcb /tmp/mini-fixture/mini-eol-carrier.kicad_pcb --placement /tmp/mini-placement/mini-placed.json --output /tmp/mini-interface-checks.json
```

The pad repair is necessary with the pinned atopile 0.15.8: repeated pad numbers
can cause the updater to replace an exposed solder land with its thermal via's
geometry; the microphone also has repeated-number segmented ground lands
and coincident graphics on different layers. The repair restores source pad
geometry while retaining nets and
placement. Circuit checks verify both the vias and exposed lands. Run it after
each rebuild, before placement or export. Atopile's automatic datasheet download
also rejects some supplier URLs containing query parameters; this does not make
the generated netlist fail, but the build logs contain those download errors.

Bazel source/route targets are `//hardware/splanc_dev:splanc_mini` and
`//hardware/splanc_dev:splanc_mini.fab`. The latter retains routing/quality gates;
it is not a claim that a routed fabrication bundle has passed. The review-copy
workflow above is the verified placement workflow.

## Mini firmware

Build `//firmware/player_app:splanc_mini` or
`//firmware/player_app:splanc_mini_flashbundle`. These select the Mini pin map
without compiling Battery's GPIO initialization. The existing wireless player
and two RMT LED outputs remain the application base.

The Mini power task uses I2C at 100 kHz. It reads the length-prefixed active PDO
and RDO twice and rejects changing/inconsistent snapshots, unsupported supply
types, absent contracts, or invalid current fields. It budgets from the accepted
operating current, rather than a source's advertised maximum. The engineering
allocation assumes 85% conversion efficiency, reserves 3 W for control, and
caps LED power at 20 W. These assumptions require measurement.

Both INA226 monitors must identify correctly and remain in their default
continuous conversion configuration. Bus and signed shunt voltage readings give
rail voltage and current without relying on an unprogrammed current-calibration
register. Acceptable rail readings are 4.75–5.25 V, with software ceilings of
2 A per port and the PD-derived aggregate budget. The hardware eFuses remain
responsible for fast local fault protection; software polling is not a
real-time substitute for them.

For bench bring-up, outputs start off. After three healthy samples, a press of
USER1 toggles the outputs. A fault, invalid reading, contract change, or frame
idle-current overload disarms them; another press is required after recovery.
The status LED lights while armed. This is an explicit engineering control,
not the final consumer button behavior.

Before each transmitted frame, the limiter scales both ports together to
preserve relative color/brightness, respecting individual and aggregate budgets.
Its provisional WS2812 model assumes 1 mA idle per pixel and 20 mA per color at
full code. If idle current alone exceeds the budget, outputs are disarmed.
Qualify the actual strip before relying on this model. Host tests exercise
contract parsing, rejected contracts, rail/fault limits and frame limiting.

Sensor and microphone application drivers, sensor-reactive demos, consumer power
status UI, and measured power-source compatibility remain unfinished. The
populated sensor circuits and their pin maps are not evidence of working sensor
firmware or a completed Kickstarter demonstration.

## Cost and verification scope

The generated Mini BOM repriced at quantity 1,000 is **$24.00533 per board**.
Buying for 1,030 assemblies and rounding supplier multiples gives **$24,728.68**.
Every retained line is covered by the 9 September 2026 sourcing snapshot; some
distributor listings are cached and all allocations need reconfirmation.
Prices exclude PCB, assembly, test, enclosure, power source/cable, shipping and
taxes. The CSV/JSON sourcing ledger accompanies the engineering review files.

The compact placed board has zero KiCad DRC violations and **422 unconnected
items**. Circuit and mechanical-array checks pass. This is an unrouted review
board, not a manufacturing release. Routing remains deferred as requested.
The mating-interface audit passes 110 checks against both physical PCBs and
the placement graph; 82 circuit/mechanical checks pass. The fixture carrier
has zero placement DRC violations and 23 unrouted connections.

Converter compensation, inrush, actual thermal/current margins, EMI/ESD behavior,
source transitions, assembled sensor operation, and enclosure fit need validation.
Do not use the placement render as a finished Mini product photograph.
