# Ground-via review: buttons and U19

2026-09-11. Read-only diagnosis of `keyhole-u18-53/candidate.kicad_pcb`; no best-board edits. It remains 53 opens, 11 dangling tracks, one dangling via, no other native DRC violations.

## What is actually present

There are 170 router-level PCB_VIA objects on lv. This excludes drilled pads embedded in footprints. All 19 distinct ground vias connected by a single F.Cu track to the four buttons' lv pads already exist in manual22.

Button mounting-pad counts from exact pad/track/via shape contact (pad 3 is the electrical ground terminal; pads 4/5 are the mounting pads):

| Reference | Pad 4 | Pad 5 |
|---|---:|---:|
| SW1 | 3 | 2 |
| SW2 | 2 | 2 |
| SW3 | 1 | 2 |
| SW4 | 2 | 2 |

Thus six mounting pads have two direct via connections, one has three, one has one. SW1's electrical-ground-terminal connection shares one of the pad-4 via paths; summing pad counts would double-count that via. The vias are outside the SMD anchors; they are not the mounting pads themselves.

## Provenance and algorithmic cause

Coordinate comparison, rather than only UUID comparison (imports rewrite identifiers), shows one mounting-pad fanout in the plane-first board and an additional nearby fanout in native1-routed on several pads. For example SW2.4 has its plane-first via at (66.9681,83.1204) mm and another at (66.208985,83.732259) mm appearing in native1-routed. Both survive manual22 and the current best board. SW1's third pad-4 path appears in manual1, when the separate ground escape for SW1.3 was added.

`hardware/pnr/pnr/writeback.py:_dogbone_fanout_net` iterates eligible SMD pads and directly searches for a legal dogbone+via. It does not first search for a surface connection to an existing ground island. The surrounding `apply_planes` code now reuses existing zones and skips another fanout pass for them, but that guard does not remove historic duplicate routes or optimize a newly generated fanout by connected surface components. `work/mini-routing/fanout.py` is also via-first for native-reported open SMD lv pads; it does not compare a surface-only connection first.

The saved history establishes when the extra geometry appeared. It does not, by itself, prove the precise internal FreeRouting objective or its interpretation of the ground plane. Ground stitching can be intentional, but these repeated pad fanouts were not generated by an explicit, reviewed stitching-density/return-path specification.

## U19 distinction and tested alternative

U19 contains four 0.2 mm drilled pads numbered 39 in its exposed-ground-pad footprint, plus the SMD exposed pad. These are footprint geometry, not redundant router-added vias, and must not be swept up by a general via-removal pass.

External fanouts near U19.26 and U19.35 already exist in plane-first; U19.2's external via appears in manual1. A diagnostic F.Cu-only route from U19.2 at (45.1,70.4) to U19.39 at (47.66,71.0) succeeds with existing 0.2 mm track width and conservative clearance. Native before/after: 53 opens, 11 dangling tracks, one dangling via, no added violations, prior pad connections preserved. This proves a geometrically legal surface connection for this example. It does not prove every nearby via unnecessary or establish electrical return-path quality.

Diagnostic: `artifacts/u19-ground-surface-diagnostic-01/region-001/`, with fixture, PCB/project and DRC. `accepted:false` is expected: the ground pads were already connected through the plane, so adding a surface branch alone does not reduce opens. Do not use this diagnostic board as a newer best checkpoint.

Inventory/provenance: `artifacts/lv-via-audit-01/lv-audit.json` and `lv-history.json`. The native1/native2/native3 private routing boards use renamed net names, so their zero matches under literal lv are not zero physical ground-via counts; rely on plane-first/native1-routed/manual snapshots for this comparison.

## Next engineering change

Plan ground fanout around actual connected copper components, not independent pads. Attempt short, legal surface reuse first; prefer a shared, nearby plane connection when electrically appropriate. Before inserting a via, refresh connectivity so another pad in the same component does not trigger duplicate work. After routing, perform a native-gated cleanup that preserves required pad connectivity, thermal footprint holes, power/return capacity and intentional stitching. Judge cleanup by reduced vias/length at unchanged opens, separately from connectivity-improvement acceptance. A via-count penalty alone is insufficient, and blanket deletion of lv vias is inappropriate.

The image review corroborates the paired/triple button fanouts and the U19 exposed-pad structure. Other current layer findings and the full PDF are recorded in the round's review ledger. No publication or manufacturing action occurred.
