FX engine performance study (ESP32-C6)¶

Scope. How fast is the effects engine on the ESP32-C6 (RV32IMAC, no FPU, 160 MHz) compared with what the chip can do, how well do the authoring agents use it, how good is the offline estimator, and what should change. Every number below was measured on a HITL C6 unless it says “estimated”. The bench, the agent-eval harness and the charts are in this PR; how to rerun them is at the end.

Short answers.

  1. Native ceiling. A native Rust version of an effect that uses the same float maths is 2–5× faster than the VM. A fixed-point native rewrite is 7–70× faster. At 2048 LEDs, 9 of the 13 study effects fit 30 fps natively (all under 20 ms); through the VM none of them does. Where the VM’s cycles go, in order: the operand-stack interpreter (about 54 cycles of dispatch per opcode, plus stack traffic, which together is 40–75% of a heavy effect), a fixed 1,790 cycles per LED for each VM invocation, soft-float math, and the per-LED framing. The JIT saves 0–8%.

  2. Agent authoring. The agents write expensive effects. The MCP skill path never uses fixed point (0 of 30 programs). Only 3 of 30 skill programs and 10 of 30 in-app programs fit 33 ms at 500 LEDs. Your 45-effect library needs a median of 49 ms on the 500-LED sign and 31 ms on the 250-LED lamp. Per LED, the sign effects are no more expensive than the lamp effects. The sign is slower because it has twice the LEDs, and that pushes 24 of its 35 deployments past the 33 ms frame. A 24-line prompt addendum cut the in-app agent’s cost by 30% (paired, same 15 prompts). Hand-optimising the 4 worst library effects gave only 1.35–1.65× under the VM (up to 2.2× with the JIT), because dispatch dominates once the floats are gone. 9 of the 90 generated programs hang the device: a compiler bug makes clamp(vec3, 0.0, 1.0) underflow the VM stack. The fix is a separate PR.

  3. Estimator. The estimate the MCP agent uses (main’s default table) predicts 5% of the measured frame time in the median. For agent programs it predicts 0.6%, and the worst case is 0.3% (330× low). This is the “3 ms vs 132 ms”. Two bugs cause it: the cost table prices ops at 2–14 cycles when they cost 54–250, and the walker stops at the first opcode numbered 70 or above (every fixed-point op and superinstruction). PR #328 fixes both. Its median is 1.00, with p10 0.59 and a worst case of 0.29 (branchy if ladders, SampleTex). The dashboard’s “negM ≈ 1.57 M” is cycles for one whole frame of 128 LEDs (update + shade + firmware framing), from a program that negates a local 32 times per LED. That is 12.3 k cycles per LED, or ≈310 cycles per a = -a statement above the empty-program floor. The panels now say so.

  4. Architecture. The cheapest large win is VM invocation overhead. Prototype P2 is bit-exact and changes about 60 lines: an integer RGB epilogue and an out-of-line LoadCtx. It cuts 1,790 → 1,031 cycles per LED: −41% for a solid fill, −16% for rain, −6% median on your library. PR #328’s memoization is orthogonal (−15% median). Moving the interpreter to IRAM gained <1%. A faster soft-float fadd/fmul gained nothing (the C6 ROM versions take ~55 cycles). The big remaining lever is AOT native code, worth 3–20× on top. It is feasible as signed, sandboxed, position-independent code, building on the existing JIT’s W^X region and the OTA signing.

Method¶

The bench image (//firmware/fx_perf_bench:fx_perf_bench_flashbundle) is a C6 firmware with no radios. It boots, runs every measurement once, logs fxpb … lines, and keeps re-dumping its RAM log so a late or resetting serial reader still gets all of it. It links:

  • the real fx_vm (the firmware’s crate, -c opt, riscv32imac, opt-level=3), with Led framing reproduced from ffi.rs fx_shade_one. That framing is the per-LED soft-float uv projection when the program reads led.uv, and the Q16 mirrors when it reads fixed context;

  • the real JIT install path, copied from ffi.rs fx_build_jit: the same 2 KB PMP W^X region, the same limits (8 blocks, 64 constants, 1 KB of code), and the same JitCall patching;

  • hand-written native twins of 13 study effects (src/native.rs), compiled the same way. f32 twins do the VM’s maths operation for operation through the same fx_vm helpers. They are bit-identical to the VM on every LED (host test native_vs_vm_test, 500 and 2048 LEDs, 3 frames), so VM − f32 is the interpretation cost alone. q16 twins are fixed-point rewrites with the same look; their max error is 1–2/255 per channel;

  • micro-benchmarks (src/micro.rs): soft-float primitives, the VM’s maths helpers, and hand-assembled .fxb programs that isolate dispatch, stack traffic and single opcodes.

Fixtures (src/fixture.rs, synthesized):

  • a 3-D helix: 10 turns, r = 8 cm, 30 cm tall, +Y up, 8 chained topology segments. Used at 500 and 2048 LEDs, and at 250 LEDs to stand in for the cylindrical PixieLamp;

  • a planar two-row letter sign: “CAMP / THERAPY” in a crude 29-stroke font, about 1 m × 0.45 m, one topology segment per stroke, 500 LEDs. It stands in for FugWidget. The real sign map wasn’t available, so its stroke numbering is ours;

  • fx_bench’s linear map.

Frames start at t = 10 s at 30 fps. Each number is the cheapest of 3 frames (interrupts only add cycles, as in fx_bench). Uniforms are set to their defaults.

The terms, measured:

  • Dispatch: ~54 cycles per opcode, measured with 200 Pop 0 per LED. That covers the instruction-budget decrement, the counter and stack high-water updates, the deadline poll mask, Op::from_u8, and the jump-table match. A push+pop averages 83 cycles per op and a load/store-local 88. A vecN opcode adds ~10 cycles per extra lane of stack traffic. Effects spend 59–67% of their opcodes on plumbing (load, store, push const, swizzle; per tools/fx_agent_eval/analyze.py). So ops × 54 is a hard floor: about 9.7 k of particles’ 19.7 k interpretation cycles. Most of the rest is operand-stack moves, operand decoding and bounds checks.

  • VM invocation: 1,790 cycles per LED, even for return vec3(0). Two causes, both visible in the RV32 disassembly of run(). First, LLVM hoists LoadCtx’s loop-invariant soft-float (1.0 − led.s, int→f32 of idx/seg/frame, …) into run()’s prologue, so every invocation pays ~5 soft-float calls whether or not the program reads those inputs. Second, the clamp01(x) * 255 as u8 epilogue is 3 × (2 compares + mul + f32→int). Prototype P2 below removes ~760 cycles of it.

  • Soft-float primitives are not the problem. Measured with precomputed inputs, the C6 ROM’s libgcc __addsf3 takes 57 cycles, __mulsf3 53, __divsf3 166, a compare 25, f32→i32 ~110 and i32→f32 87. A hand-written, bit-exact RV32 fast path (src/fastfloat.rs, checked against hardware floats on 4 M random pairs) took 71 and 51, no better. The VM’s maths helpers are where float costs pile up: sinf 482 (a f32→i64 + u32→f32 round trip to build the LUT phase), floorf 217, fractf 269, sqrtf 566, atan2f 900, expf 845, logf 684, powf 1,696, hsv2rgb 1,371. The fixed-point equivalents: sin_fix 86, atan2_fix 258, sqrt_fix 574 (a bit-by-bit sqrt), the integer hash1 109. Including dispatch, an UnMath sin opcode costs ~580 cycles and a SinFix ~146.

  • Per-LED framing. The soft-float uv projection (led.uv) costs ~330 cycles per LED. The Q16 context mirrors cost 1,100–1,500 cycles per LED whenever a program reads any fixed context. They are recomputed per LED per frame in fx_shade_one (q16_16(x) = soft mul + f32→i32 × 7), although the struct comment says “at map-load”. That is 20% of the hand-optimised rain. Caching them at map load is pure win.

  • The JIT compiled blocks for 31 of the 45 library effects but saved a median of only 1.7% (best 10.8%). It never engaged for 4 of the 13 study effects (consts>64 for rain, which has 73 constants; no candidate block for the float effects). Its IR covers only straight-line PushConst / Load/Store/TeeLocal / AddI SubI MulI NegI MulFix Pop (fx_jit op_to_ir). It does better on fixed-point code: the hand-optimised rainforest gets 2.2× with the JIT against 1.5× without, and hits the 8-block cap.

VM maths accuracy (a correctness finding). fx_vm::logf adds a polynomial that approximates log₂(m) − 1 to e·ln 2. It is off by up to ~0.13 nats: logf(1.5) = 0.276 against ln 1.5 = 0.405. So powf(x, 8) (= exp(8·log x)) is off by up to ~2.8×. sparkle’s pow(…, 8.0) renders 12/255 where the exact value is 102/255. A fix ((e + p(m)) · ln 2 with a log₂ poly) changes every pow/log effect’s look, so it needs its own PR and owner sign-off.

Native ms/frame at 2048 LEDs. Through the VM, only solid fits 33 ms at 2048 LEDs (29 ms). Natively, 9 of the 13 study effects fit, all in under 20 ms, including the 8-agent particles at 16 ms. The four that don’t are noise (40 ms even in fixed point: 8 lattice hashes per LED) and texture, flood and rain_naive, which have only f32 twins.

Static analysis (analyze.py: disassembly-level counts, a variance analysis and perf-lint rules; out/summary.md):

  • The skill path never uses fixed point (0 of 30 programs, against 24 of 30 for the app path). It averages 2.6–5.1 float transcendentals per LED and ~38 float ops per LED. Its docs tell the model “sin/cos are cheap LUTs; hash is an integer mix (cheap)”. For float arguments sin costs ~580 cycles. All the skill’s example effects are float.

  • Where a cheaper equivalent exists, the agents mostly don’t pick it:

    • float sin/cos in shade: 56 hits in 20 programs;

    • per-LED-invariant atan2 / hash(led.idx): 38 hits in 18;

    • frame-invariant maths left in shade: 36 hits in 28;

    • a float literal silently promoting fixed maths back to soft-float (fixed x; x * 12.5): 29 hits in 8, all from Opus with the app prompt;

    • a loop per LED: 11 hits in 10;

    • noise octaves per LED: 7 hits in 7.

  • Fixed point helps less than people think under the VM. The median fixed-using program costs 13.2 k cycles per LED and the median float-only one 17.3 k. The 54-cycle dispatch per opcode stays.

  • 9 of 90 programs crash the device. clamp(color, 0.0, 1.0) is idiomatic GLSL. The compiler emits CLAMP 3 over 3 + 1 + 1 stack slots, the VM indexes below its stack (an index-out-of-bounds panic), and the player’s panic handler is loop {}, so the render task hangs. 8 of the 9 are skill/opus programs. The compiler fix (broadcast scalar bounds, or reject them) is follow-up PR #338. The VM should also never panic on a malformed stack (recommendation R3).

  • The two prompts contradict the compiler in places. The in-app prompt says sqrt log tan pow atan2 hsv2rgb are float-only, but the compiler has fixed paths for all of them (SqrtFix, Atan2Fix, Hsv2RgbFix, …). It also promises the JIT gives “a further ~2×”. The VM has SmoothstepFix, DotFix, LengthFix and HashFix, but the compiler never emits them: smoothstep(fixed…) and hash(…) always go through float.

The tested prompt change. tools/fx_agent_eval/perf_addendum.txt is 24 lines appended to the in-app prompt. It has the measured per-op cost table, six rules (hoist; cache LED-invariant geometry in a buffer; fixed-point phases in turns; no pow/exp per LED; fixed() around literals; no per-LED loops) and one compiled fast example. On the same 15 prompts with Opus it cut the median measured cost by 30% (paired geometric mean 0.74×). The number that fit 33 ms went from 4 to 7 (10 of 15 with the JIT), and float transcendentals per LED fell from 0.93 to 0. Feeding the linter’s warnings back for one revision round (app_lint) helped less: 0.89× median.

Your library: 45 agent-written effects (splanc_library.json)¶

Every effect was measured on the synthetic sign at 500 LEDs and the synthetic cylinder at 250 LEDs. The raw library isn’t committed; only these derived numbers and four rewrites are. The VM numbers below exclude the ~0.6 k-cycle firmware framing, so on the device each ms column is about 1.9 ms (sign) or 0.9 ms (cylinder) higher.

Why the 500-LED sign versions run “a fair bit” worse than the 250-LED lamp ones. It isn’t the geometry. Each effect costs the same per LED on both fixtures, within 1–4%. The sign-targeted effects are, if anything, cheaper per LED: a median of 15.5 k cycles against 19.5 k for the lamp ones. Three things combine:

  1. Twice the LEDs gives exactly twice the frame time.

  2. The frame budget is a cliff. The lamp effects run a median of 31 ms on 250 LEDs, under the 33 ms frame. Their sign siblings run 49 ms on 500 LEDs. 24 of the 35 sign deployments exceed 33 ms against 5 of 19 lamp ones, so on the sign the autoscaler drops to its 25 fps rung or the effect lags below it, and the motion looks visibly worse. A 2× cost reads as much more than 2×.

  3. The firmware’s ~600 cycles per LED of framing and the longer transmit both double as well.

The per-effect cost drivers are listed in full in the appendix; the worst ones are:

  • Rainbow overload #2 (103 ms on the sign): five hsv2rgb per LED (~1.4 k cycles each), 3 hashes, 2 float sines, a per-LED sqrt of an LED-invariant radius, and 42 float ops.

  • Metaballs, 3 variants (86–87 ms): 5 float divides and 2 hsv2rgb per LED, plus a per-LED exp and smoothstep.

  • Rainforest (80 ms): five float sines, each with its time phase rebuilt per LED (24% of its cycles are hoistable), plus a per-LED exp whose value depends only on time.

  • The ten fire-* effects (28–68 ms): the helix family runs an atan2 of led.pos per LED per frame (17–34% of cycles are LED-invariant) plus a float fract/floor value-noise hash. The flow family uses 2–3 float sines.

  • Handwriting and Pass the ball: a 28-way if ladder in a rank() helper, run per LED. #328’s memo pass doesn’t reach into user functions.

  • Call and response: 4 exp per LED.

Only 5 of the 45 use any fixed type (Rainy afternoon, your hand-optimised rain, plus Windy meadow, Snowfall, Fog bank and Aurora). All 5 are among the 7 cheapest (15–23 ms on the sign).

Against the old estimates. The stored estimates are 1.1–7.2 ms. Paired with the measurement on the same fixture, they are 6–36× low (median 14×). The fire-helix effects were all estimated at 1.1 ms on the lamp and measure 31–35 ms there (29–32×).

The techniques were: fixed-point LUT sines in turns; time phases folded into state once per frame; hue and colour-ramp tables baked once instead of hsv2rgb per LED; LED-invariant atan2 and sqrt cached in a buffer; a random table instead of a float hash. Prompt and tooling recommendations (Q2)¶

  • Fix the false or contradictory statements in both prompts. In the skill: “sin/cos cheap LUTs”, “hash cheap”, all-float examples. In the in-app prompt: the “float-only” list and “JIT ~2×”. Add the measured cost table from perf_addendum.txt to both. That addendum measured −30% on its own.

  • Give the skill path fixed-point examples. At least the addendum’s rotating rainbow, and a fixed-point noise and plasma.

  • Close the estimate loop with the corrected cost model (#328). Today estimate_effect says a 60 ms effect costs 0.4 ms, so the “iterate with the estimate” workflow can’t work. Return the top 3 lint findings from analyze.py’s rules with the estimate (cheap, and it tells the model what to change).

  • Make the cheap form the easy form in the compiler. Warn on a float literal inside fixed maths (29 hits). Wire SmoothstepFix, HashFix, DotFix and LengthFix. Treat fixed x * 0.6 as fixed by default behind a lint (#328 lists this follow-up).

Why main is 20–1,000× low.

  • The default table (costTableStore.ts DEFAULT_COSTS) prices PushConst/LoadLocal at 2–3 cycles, Add at 12 and UnMath:sin at 65. It has no dispatch term and shade_fixed = 60. The device costs 54–88 per op, ~110, ~580, and ~1,800 per LED (the invocation).

  • The walker (costModel.ts walkEntry) knows opcodes only up to ExpFix (69). On the first byte above that (AbsI, ClampI, LoadCtxFix, TeeLocal, IncLocalI, BrCmpI, …) it stops the walk. The optimizer emits those superinstructions in almost every program, and every fixed-point program uses the fixed ops. That is why every fire-helix effect estimates 0.003× (it reads LoadCtxFix almost immediately) and why the agent programs estimate a median of 0.006×.

#328’s remaining errors, and why.

  • Under-estimates (worst 0.29×):

    • Call and response 0.29×, and Handwriting and Pass the ball 0.42–0.46×. These are if ladders and user-function rank() chains. The walker prices a forward branch at the midpoint of its two arms and walks a structured if once, but a 28-way ladder executes ~14 compares per LED on average.

    • texture 0.41×. SampleTex is priced like a cheap op; the bilinear float sample measured ~3,800 cycles.

  • Over-estimates:

    • rain 1.67×. Its cloud branch runs for 1/16 of the LEDs per frame (mod(i, 16) == slot), but the walker prices it as half of all LEDs.

    • lavalamp 4.6× against its golden. The estimator is right here: the golden was measured with all uniforms at 0 (see Method).

  • Fixes: a per-op cost for SampleTex/PaintTex/LoadBuf (~1–4 k); a probability hint (or dynamic profiling in the preview, which the estimator already accepts as dynamicShadeHist) for data-dependent branches; and walk user functions at each call site.

Reading the fx_bench numbers (“negM ≈ 1.57 M cycles”)¶

How pi/hitl/harness/fx_bench.py produces each value:

  • compile_fx compiles each calibration program with --no-opt.

  • _linear_map submits an N-LED linear map, where N comes from the program’s “Intended LED count” header: 128 for the *M programs, 16 and 256 for sweep16/sweep256, 200 for lavalamp.

  • set_perf FULL is poll-only. The harness waits and reads a PerfReport.

  • stable_cycles() takes frameCyclesMin, the minimum over the window of perf_frame_c in main.cpp: c_frame_end − c_frame_start around lm_fx_update + the whole shade loop (lm_map_led + lm_fx_shade + cc_apply per LED), excluding the LED transmit.

  • show is the mean of show_c, the cycles inside the transmit call.

  • The CI log prints "<label>: frame=X show=Y @ N LEDs". ingest_fx_bench.py parses that line into the Grafana dataset as cycles with metric ∈ {frame, show} and leds = N.

So negM = 1.57 M is the CPU cycles to render one frame of 128 LEDs (9.8 ms at 160 MHz). It is not a per-op cost, not a loop of M iterations, and not one LED. negM is “Neg ×32”: each LED runs a = (-(a)) 32 times, which is LoadLocal, Neg, StoreLocal = 96 opcodes, about 117 with the prologue. That gives:

  • 1.57 M / 128 = 12.3 k cycles per LED, of which ≈2.3–2.45 k is the empty floor (VM invocation + firmware framing);

  • ≈10 k for the 32 statements, i.e. ≈310 cycles per a = -a, or ≈100 cycles per opcode.

x2M programs repeat their operation 64 times; (frame(x2M) − frame(xM)) / (128 × 32) is the cost of one extra statement with the floor cancelled.

Dashboard changes in this PR (pi/hitl/observability/fxbench/gen_dashboards.py, regenerated dashboards/fxbench-{drift,distribution,overview}.json):

  • every panel carries this definition as its description;

  • a “What these numbers are” text panel sits at the top of each dashboard;

  • titles now say “CPU cycles per frame (all LEDs)”;

  • the time-series y-axis is labelled “CPU cycles / frame (update + shade × LEDs)”;

  • the sample tables gain a derived cycles/LED column (Grafana calculateField, cycles ÷ leds).

The data pipeline is unchanged.

What the prototypes are:

  • P2, VM invocation overhead. fx_vm gets an out-of-line LoadCtx, so its soft-float can’t be hoisted into run()’s prologue, and an integer RGB epilogue unit_u8(). The epilogue is bit-exact with (clamp01(x) * 255.0) as u8: it is tested on every f32 bit pattern in [0, 1.5] plus specials, and the fx_vm, compiler and optimizer differential suites pass. An empty invocation drops from 1,790 to 1,031 cycles. P2 is draft PR #339; the fx_bench goldens move (empty −54%, sweep256 −45%), so it needs a golden regeneration on a rig.

  • P3, the interpreter in IRAM (#[link_section = ".iram1"] on the 37 KB run()) gains <1%. The flash I-cache is not the bottleneck.

  • P1, P2 plus a bit-exact fast fadd/fmul in the Add/Sub/Mul/Scale/Mix arms is worse than P2 alone: the C6’s ROM soft-float already takes ~55 cycles. Don’t pursue this.

  • PR #328 (memoization and hoisting) is orthogonal to P2. Its rows include 2–5% from #328 being based on an older main (its addM is −5% with no memo change).

Opportunities¶

The gains below are measured, or estimated from Q1’s terms where marked “est.”:

  1. VM invocation overhead (P2): −760 cycles per LED on every effect. For a cheap effect that is −40%; for a typical one −5 to −16%. About 60 lines, bit-exact. Do first.

  2. Cache the Q16 context mirrors and uv at map load instead of per LED per frame: −1.1 to −1.5 k cycles per LED for every fixed-context program (rain −20%) and −330 for every uv reader (est.). It is firmware-only, and the struct comment already describes this design.

  3. Register-style superinstructions. Use opcodes that read operands from locals and constants and write a local directly (AddLL, MulFixLC, SinFixL, LoadCtxFixL, …), driven by real opcode traces. That removes most of the 60–65% plumbing ops. Est. 1.5–2× on interpretation, which is −30 to −45% on heavy effects. The cost is moderate: the compiler’s opt.rs already has the fusion framework and the ~7 coupled edit sites per opcode are documented.

  4. Leaner dispatch. Hoist the budget, counter and stack high-water work out of the per-op path: count per basic block, and only on the counted path. Drop the Op::from_u8 range check behind a verified-at-load bytecode; elide bounds checks on verified stack depth. Est. 54 → ~25 cycles per op (−15 to −25%). A threaded match doesn’t exist in Rust, but a table-driven fn pointer per op plus tail calls is not reliably faster on RV32; measure before committing to it.

  5. Faster maths helpers. Do the float paths at bit level: floorf/fractf without f32↔int round trips (217/269 → ~30), a sinf phase built from the mantissa and exponent (482 → ~120), a table hsv2rgb (1,371 → ~200). Fix logf. Wire the existing *Fix opcodes into the compiler. Est. −10 to −25% on float effects.

  6. JIT expansion. Lift the 64-constant and 1 KB-code limits (rain is excluded today). Add the fixed ISA (SinFix, ClampI, MinI/MaxI, AbsI, LoadCtxFix, LoadState, BrCmpI branches inside a block), then calls to the soft-float helpers. Est. up to 2× on fixed-point effects (the rainforest rewrite got 2.2× with today’s JIT once its code was fixed-point). Float effects gain little until the JIT emits soft-float calls with a real register allocator.

  7. AOT native effects (see the next section). 3–20× on top of an optimised VM (Q1: VM ÷ q16 is 7–70× today). This is the only route to 2048 LEDs at 30 fps for non-trivial effects.

A native AOT path: feasibility¶

  • What runs. The compiler emits position-independent RV32IMC (C6, C3) or Thumb-2 (ARMv8-M) for shade() and update(). It can do this for a whole effect, or for hot functions with VM fallback. Calls go only to a fixed, versioned helper table passed in a register (soft-float, LUTs, hash, buffer/texture accessors).

  • Size. The native study effects compile to roughly 0.5–4 KB each. The JIT region today is 2 KB. A 16–32 KB region fits easily in the bench image, but on the netstack firmware it competes with TLS heap (FUG-71/133). Use a fixed IRAM/PMP region sized from board_caps, or execute from a flash partition through the cache. The P3 measurement shows execution from flash is not the bottleneck.

  • Trust. This is executable code arriving over the network, so the loader must verify an Ed25519 signature over the code segment before mapping it, with the same key chain as OTA images. The device never runs unsigned native code; unsigned effects keep the VM.

  • Sandboxing, even for signed code:

    • (a) memory: all loads and stores go through a base register into the effect’s arena, masked or bounds-checked by the code generator (and with a power-of-two mask is 1 cycle), with a PMP region limiting write access to the arena and stack;

    • (b) control flow: no indirect jumps except returns and the helper table, checked by a simple loader-side verifier of the instruction stream, much as the JIT emits only known encodings;

    • (c) time: a per-frame cycle budget checked at loop back-edges (the VM’s deadline flag) plus the existing wall-time watchdog.

  • Static installations (no network trust problem) can build effects into the firmware image and skip all of the above.

  • Incremental plan.

    1. Lower the VM’s verified bytecode to RV32 on the host, in a fx_compiler --aot, as straight-line per-block code that reuses fx_jit’s encoder and emulator-based differential tests. Run it unsigned on the bench image only.

    2. Extend the helper table and buffers, and run the differential test against the VM.

    3. Sign it, and add a loader on the device, gated by board capability.

    4. Add a Thumb backend for future boards.

    Expected speedups come from Q1: native-f32 is 2–5× faster than today’s VM, and native-q16 7–70×.

What is not verified¶

  • Agent effects were measured on the bench, not through fx_bench_netstack. The bench reproduces fx_bench within ±2% plus a constant ~600 cycles/LED of firmware framing (validated on 8 programs). The framing term is an average from empty/sweep256. It differs by effect (colour correction is per LED; lm_map_led reads the stored 9-byte position).

  • Both fixtures are synthesized. The sign is a crude stroke font with our own segment numbering, so the library’s hard-coded led.seg tables (e.g. Handwriting’s rank()) take different branches than on FugWidget. Per-LED cost is geometry-independent within 1–4% here, but a real map with unmapped LEDs (seg < 0 returns early) would be cheaper.

  • rain_naive is a reconstruction, not the real first draft.

  • Two natives are not exact twins. sparkle’s q16 twin computes pow exactly and differs from the VM’s inaccurate powf. The fire-helix rewrite uses a different random field.

  • The #328 numbers are confounded. They come from #328’s branch as it is (based on an older main), so about 2–5% of its delta is base drift.

  • P2, P3 and P1 are prototypes, measured on the bench and not on the netstack firmware. P2’s golden shifts are predicted from the bench, not from an fx_bench run.

  • The “est.” gains in Q4 (superinstructions, dispatch, maths helpers, JIT, AOT) are derived from the measured cost terms, not from implementations.

  • No OpenAI model was evaluated (no key).
