Mesh M6: effect sharing, scenes and catch-up¶

M6 of mesh.md §15: the blob kind on the page protocol (§5.5), effect distribution, scenes and catch-up for late joiners (§5.6), and the mesh_effect HITL. Stacked on M7 (#594). Before M6 only a message that fits one mesh packet (~160 B) was relayed, so an effect with any uniform (its manifest alone is ~250 B) reached only the connected fixture. Now an effect of any size up to the player’s effect limit (4 KiB of .fxb on a C6) reaches every member, and they all switch to it on the same frame. Maps stay per fixture.

Measured with //pi/hitl/harness:mesh_effect on hitl-rig-1: three C6 players (c6-123170 = a, the gateway; c6-711130 = b; c6-711f48 = c) on the rig AP, :esp32c6_netstack_meshdev, membership and grants as in M7 (mesh_admin_tool stands in for the app). The client holds one wss connection to a only. Logs and JSON: /workspace/.claude/mesh/m6/runs/.

Results¶

Runs r4, r5 and r6 (2026-11-22), all passing every M6 gate; r6 is the final code (scene dispatches moved onto the relayed-control path for the loopTask stack bound): 8/8 rounds, spread 0.23–1.33 ms, submit to all 0.59–1.31 s, late joiner 5.7 s, reboot 1.5 s with no reload, member heap min 42 896 B. r5 and r6 first reported late frames the gate could not attribute: on r5 a’s membership write at its first join (logged after its [late] line), on r6 the gateway’s sharded-upload windows to /lfs/upload.tmp (only the first window is logged). Both are flash writes; the gate now attributes them, and r4–r6 re-judged from their logs have no late frame outside effect loads and flash writes. Numbers below from r4. Effects: meshStorm (1 981 B fxb, 7 uniforms, a 1 998 B submit_effect frame) and meshEmber (2 907 B fxb, 11 uniforms, 2 924 B frame), alternating; odd rounds go as 1 KB upload_chunk windows (the BLE path’s sharding).

How the app uses it¶

Nothing changes for the app’s code paths: with whole-mesh control on (client.meshAll), client.submitEffect(id, fxb, activate, deck) shares the effect with the whole mesh instead of loading it on the connected fixture alone. The explicit call is client.meshShareEffect(effectId, fxb, {deck, activate, nodes, group, leadMs, wantReply, timeoutMs, skipGateway}) (web/src/net/meshEffect.ts):

  • the direct reply is the connected fixture’s result_ready{effectId} (or the error a load would give: bad_fxb, effect_too_large), as before;

  • with wantReply (the default) the per-member outcome is pushed as mesh.result (op id) to client.onMeshResult subscribers;

  • meshLargeShare(client, msg) is the hook for the app-UI’s setMeshLargeSender (#613): it shares an effect the app already loaded on the connected fixture with the other members (skipGateway; they switch together, the connected one switched on arrival).

On the wire it is the M5 arm: mesh_send{inner: submit_effect}, now of any size. Bigger than one transport window (BLE, or over 4 KiB on wss) the whole mesh_send goes as upload_chunk windows of kind EFFECT, which the firmware reassembles and hands to the mesh gateway (after the kiosk gate) instead of the player core.

Design¶

Blobs (firmware/mesh/src/blob.rs)¶

A blob is an encoded ClientMessage (a submit_effect frame, or a state blob of small frames), named by its SHA-256 (8-byte id on the wire). It travels on BULK (non-admin, lowest relay priority, inside the mesh envelope), one hop at a time, pulled like the swarm’s page protocol with one page per blob:

  • a node that wants blob H sends REQ{id, size, 64-bit missing bitmap} to a holder (one that announced it with HAVE, or the scene’s origin) when nothing came for 120 ms;

  • a holder answers with the missing 160-byte chunks, one DATA per paced bulk slot (send_bulk’s 20 ms gap and credit gate, §10.6); to a neighbour that is a one-hop broadcast, so every node fetching the same blob takes it too;

  • complete, the receiver checks the SHA-256 against the scene’s hash and only then keeps it, announces HAVE and serves it in turn. A mismatch is dropped and counted (bad_hash); a fetch without progress for 15 s is given up (stalled).

Largest blob: 64 chunks × 160 B = 10 KiB.

Scenes (SCENE port, admin-class)¶

SceneMsg{op, flags, apply_at, targets, ≤ 2 × (sha256, size)} goes through the admin session (send_admin: per-receiver MACs, M7), so members take blob names only from the session, never from gossip, and a blob is authorised by the admin’s unlocked connection and content-addressed. Flags: APPLY (at apply_at on the show clock), NOW (as soon as held), WANT_REPLY, CATCH_UP (an effect equal to what the member shows is not reloaded).

Sharing an effect (MeshStack::distribute)¶

  1. The gateway checks the frame as a load would (lm_fx_check_effect: parses, fits, not a driver, not a store write), answers the app, keeps a copy (heap, only while in use) and sends Scene{hold, want_reply}. No show clock yet: the session starts one (desk #79).

  2. Members fetch it and answer “have”; the gateway collects the answers (Fanout, 12 s).

  3. Then (Event::Fetched) the gateway picks apply_at = now + lead_ms (default 250 ms) on the show clock, sends Scene{apply, apply_at} and stages its own copy for that time.

  4. Each member hands the frame to the render task (lm_fx_stage), which loads it at the first grid frame at/after apply_at (inside lm_sched_apply, like a scheduled set_effect): everyone switches on the same frame. A member that got the blob after the time loads it at once (late) and says so. The loaded deck-A effect is persisted on loopTask like a deck-A upload (it resumes at boot).

Catch-up (members that join or reboot later)¶

The gateway counts scene changes made through its session (shares, relayed control). A member that syncs with the session after one (its Ack: it joined, or rebooted) gets a catch-up scene: the gateway’s persisted effect frame (/lfs/fx.pb) and a state blob of its current look (deck A’s uniform values, brightness, crossfade: lm_fx_scene_frames), applied at once. A member whose persisted effect is that blob (the rebooted one) finds it there by hash: no fetch and no reload; the state frames still apply. A member that answered a share’s second phase with anything but OK gets a catch-up too.

Firmware (firmware/player_app/mesh_blob.rs)¶

  • Store: three slots (receiving, complete, staged), heap buffers only while a blob is in use, freed after 20 s idle; /lfs/fx.pb is found by hash. Zeroed before free.

  • Where the work runs: fetching, serving and hashing in the mesh tick (below the render task, PR-40); applying scenes (staging, dispatching state frames, the catch-up’s reads) in ns_mesh_scenes on loopTask’s stack after the tick; the effect load itself on the render task at its frame (the only way to switch on one frame).

Cost¶

  • Flash (default image, both built here from the same base): M7 tip (fc7177a01) 2 275 104 B → M6 2 283 056 B (+7 952 B); //firmware/loader:layout_test passes with 10 704 B of its headroom left. M6’s own code is ~+19 KB (the crate’s blob/scene/share 7 KB, the firmware store and scene glue 7 KB, the player’s staged load, check and scene frames 2.5 KB, the gateway arm 1.5 KB); the swarm’s firmware image updater and settings bundles were made host-only here (−12.5 KB), then kept on the C6 by desk D-463 (OTA over the mesh comes back on them): +12.5 KB again.

  • RAM, default image: .dram0.data identical, .dram0.bss +56 B (the staged load’s slot in the player, the tick-stack trampoline). Mesh off: nothing else.

  • Heap while on: the mesh block grows by the blob slots’ bookkeeping (~200 B); blob bytes are heap only while a blob is in use (≤ 3 slots, freed 20 s after last use).

  • Stack: fetching, serving and hashing on the tick stack (painted high water as M7); the staged load on the tick stack from the render task (render keeps its 5 KB); a scene’s dispatches go through the transport like relayed control (ns_mesh_take_dispatch), so //firmware/player_app:stack_bound_test holds: loopTask bound 9 280 B (11 920 B with margin of 12 288 B).

Open¶

  • Catch-up needs the session. As for the show epoch (M7), a member that rejoins while no app holds a grant renders what it persisted until the next unlock (desk item).

  • Deck B is part of a catch-up (desk D-465): every fixture keeps its loaded deck-B frame in RAM (never persisted; up to the effect cap of heap while one is loaded), and the catch-up scene carries it as a third blob, with deck B’s uniforms in the state blob.

  • Multi-hop: every holder serves its neighbours (Deluge), but a member keeps a blob only 20 s after it applied it; a far member that starts fetching later than that asks the gateway (unicast over the mesh).

  • The effect load costs a frame on every fixture, as any direct upload does (24 KB arena reset, JIT build); [mesh] fx applied load_us= reports it.

Members show 20–24 late frames (16–25 ms intervals) whenever the harness opens a wss connection to them to check what they run: the TLS server handshake’s ECDHE shares the CPU with an effect running near its frame budget.
