Mesh HITL rig: conducted RF, real display flip timing¶

Status: proposed, software built (desk D-92). The hardware is still arriving. Everything below that names a part number is a recommendation or a question for the owner (§11).

Revision 2 (2026-11-20): one attenuator per DUT. The owner overrode desk D-241: no PE4302 step attenuators. The owner’s five Jeanoko 0–6 GHz digital attenuators (0–31.75 dB in 0.25 dB steps, UART text commands, OLED and buttons) go one per DUT, each calibrated per unit on the owner’s VNA. The pair chains keep only the HMC849 cut switch and a fixed pad; the HMC7992 delay bank is unchanged. This revision reworks the path-loss and isolation budget (§2.1), the controller (§3.3, §5), the sequencing rules (§5.4), the calibration (§5.8) and what the scenarios can still do (§6.1). The board-level delta for the rfctl-host PCB is in .claude/rigorder/rfctl-host-followup.md (outside the repo).

This rig extends the conducted RF bench (/workspace/.claude/rfbench/RF-BENCH.md, the “starter N = 4” purchase) into a reservable HITL unit that measures what the mesh is for: every fixture showing the same frame at the same instant, while links come and go and change length. The M4 gate (mesh_apply_at_skew) measured apply skew against AP beacons, because rig-1 has no analyser on its strips. This rig measures the light itself, with an overhead camera, and the data wires, with a 16-channel analyser.

1. Summary¶

  • Topology. A host SBC runs hitl-reserved with one pin-only composite unit, mesh-bench-1. The unit holds the RP2350 controller, 4 TinyC6 u.FL DUTs on the conducted matrix, 2 P4+C6 boards, the camera and the analyser. Each DUT strip has 3 LEDs. The camera looks down on all strips and on a sync LED that the controller drives. The analyser taps 11 strip data lines and the controller’s SYNC line.

  • Links come and go through the matrix’s cut switches (one per pair) and one attenuator per DUT, so a pair’s loss is the sum of its two DUTs’ settings plus a fixed part (§2.1). Links change length through the delay bank on pair 0–1: 16 lengths from 0 to 375 cm of cable. A scenario is a timeline played by the controller on its own clock. Every switch step is one latch edge, accurate to about 1 µs. The attenuators are UART units: their commands start on the latch edge and land milliseconds later, so the compiler sequences them around the switches (§5.4).

  • Display flip timing. Each strip shows a three-scale clock of its own show time. A camera frame that integrates a flip gives the flip’s instant to a fraction of the exposure. Fits over 1 s windows give each fixture’s display offset. The spread across fixtures is the measurement.

  • Accuracy targets.

2.1 Path loss and isolation with one attenuator per DUT¶

Each DUT’s Jeanoko sits between its u.FL pigtail cable and its node splitter’s common port. The pair chains keep the cut switch and a fixed pad. For pair i–j, with the cut switch through:

L_ij = F_ij + A_i + A_j

  • F_ij is the fixed part: both node sides (pigtail, 1 m RG400, the splitter’s through loss) plus the pair chain (2 × 50 cm RG400, the HMC849, the pad).

  • A_i is DUT i’s unit: its insertion loss plus its setting (0–31.75 dB), from its VNA table (§5.8).

The new limitation: a detour skips the relay’s attenuator. A cut pair i–k with a live common neighbour j still leaks: i’s splitter → chain i–j → port to port across j’s splitter → chain j–k → k’s splitter. That path never passes j’s common port, so it never passes A_j. Relative to the pair’s own through loss, the detour costs one extra pair chain plus the splitter isolation:

detour_ik|j = (L_ik if it were through) + c + I, with c = the pair-chain loss, I ≈ 20 dB.

In the old design every detour crossed two pair chains’ DSAs, so a cut was about L_ij + L_jk deep. Now a cut is only c + I deeper than the same pair through. The fixed pair pad is the only lever on that margin, so this revision raises it from 10 dB (the cart as first written) to 30 dB (the bought 10 dB pad stacked on a 20 dB pad). TX power compensates for the higher fixed loss: link edges move with TX (RF-BENCH §1).

5. RF controller (rfctl)¶

5.1 Why an RP2350 and this design¶

The controller plays the scenario on its own clock, so USB and host jitter never reach the RF timing. An RP2350 with no radio cannot leak into the bench. The 595 chain makes every switch line one latch edge: all switches of a step change in the same instant. SYNC and the sync LED mark that instant for the analyser and the camera. The attenuators can’t join that instant: each Jeanoko has its own MCU that takes a text command. The controller starts every attenuator command of a step on that step’s latch edge, in parallel on PIO UARTs, and the compiler moves the step earlier or later so the RF change lands where the scenario needs it (§5.4).

5.4 Timing¶

Switches (cuts, delay bank, sync LED): unchanged. The main loop stages a step 400 µs early: it shifts the chain in while outputs are unchanged. A hardware alarm fires 20 µs before the step. The handler spins to the exact µs with interrupts off, then raises RCLK, sets the direct GPIOs and toggles SYNC in one SIO write.

  • Expected lateness: ≤ 1 µs. It is measured (log) and faulted above latelimit (50 µs).

  • Minimum step spacing: 50 µs. 48 bits at 10 MHz SPI take 5 µs.

  • The SP4T switches settle in about 0.1–1 µs. During a delay-bank change the path is open for under 1 µs: a link changes length without being cut for longer.

  • A UART-only step that lands within 50 µs of another step joins that step.

  • Compiled.uart lists (t_issue, t_land, device, value) for every attenuator change. The results can then put error bars on attenuator-only events.

  • Never rely on two units moving together. Each pair loss depends on two units, so during the ~ms between two landings a link passes through an intermediate value. Anything that has to be simultaneous (a link appearing or vanishing) is done with the cut switches.

  • A changed unit also changes every live link of its DUT, not just the pair named in the step (§6.1).

5.5 Safe default¶

  • Power-up. /OE is high, so the 595 outputs are hi-Z and the pull resistors hold the lines safe: every cut open and the delay bank all-off. The firmware shifts in and latches the safe vector: every cut open, the delay bank at arm 0 (pair 0–1 is cut anyway), the sync LED off. Only then does it drive /OE low. It also sends 31.75 dB to every attenuator.

  • The attenuators power up at 0 dB, and may boot after the controller (OLED init). So the cut switches carry the safe state on their own: a cut is in series with every pair. The firmware re-sends every unit’s value every 0.5 s for 5 s after power-up and after every safe or lease expiry. attcheck confirms each unit.

  • A hardware watchdog (500 ms) resets a hung loop back into that state.

  • The lease covers a dead host or agent. The rig agent always takes one.

  • safe and stop work while playing. safe opens every cut on one latch edge (µs), then the attenuators follow to 31.75 dB (ms).

5.7 Fallback: the units on USB¶

If a unit’s header turns out not to be a usable UART, its micro-USB port is (both reviews drove it that way). Then:

  • the units hang off a powered USB hub on the bench host (one of the two Atolla hubs owned);

  • jeanoko.py drives each unit directly (Jeanoko(port).set(dB), which waits for ok);

  • the harness, not the controller, times the attenuator changes. Host and USB jitter add about 1–10 ms on top of t_proc. The rules in §5.4 still apply, with a larger guard;

  • the lease no longer covers the attenuators. The cuts still carry the safe state;

  • every unit is another USB cable into the RF enclosure (a leak path: clip a ferrite on each) and another /dev/serial/by-id entry. Identical units may share a USB serial number, so the catalog must name them by hub port path.

The rfctl-host design keeps both paths open: UART headers for the controller, and power-only USB-A ports that also fit the units’ own cables.

5.8 Attenuator calibration (per unit, on the VNA)¶

Each unit gets its own table: S21 at all 128 settings at the mesh channels. The table follows the unit (label each one, J1–J7) and is copied into the bench file for its slot. Tooling: atten_cal.py (//pi/hitl/meshbench:atten_cal).

  1. VNA set-up. The owner’s 28 GHz VNA. Use 2.40–2.50 GHz, 201 points (plus 2412/2437/2462 MHz, Wi-Fi channels 1, 6 and 11), IF bandwidth 1 kHz, port power −10 dBm (the units take < 1 W). Do a full 2-port SOLT calibration at the ends of the two test cables (GSS405 50 cm). Check it with a thru: |S21| < 0.05 dB.

  2. Drive the unit from the controller on its ATT header (--rfctl … --dev att0), or over its own USB (--jeanoko /dev/ttyUSB0). Let it boot for 5 s. Confirm attcheck → ok.

  3. Sweep: atten_cal.py sweep --unit J1 … --scpi <vna>:5025 --out J1.csv. It steps 0 → 31.75 dB, waits 50 ms per step, reads one sweep, and repeats 0 dB at the end. That is about a minute per unit. The default SCPI commands (INIT:IMM;*OPC?, CALC:DATA:FDAT?, SENS:FREQ:DATA?) suit most SCPI VNAs. Check them against the owner’s VNA manual. With no SCPI, export one Touchstone file per setting and write the CSV (setting_db,freq_mhz,s21_db) by hand.

  4. Repeat and reverse once per unit (31.75 → 0). The two runs agree to ≤ 0.1 dB rms, or the unit is suspect. Record the 0 dB drift between the start and the end.

  5. Return loss: S11 and S22 ≤ −14 dB (VSWR 1.5) at 0, 15 and 31.75 dB. The listing claims ≤ 1.4.

  6. Latency (t_proc, §5.4): use the VNA in CW zero-span mode at 2437 MHz, if it has one, or the HackRF on a 20 dB pad. Toggle 0 ↔ 20 dB with set 20 times, and time the RF step from the controller’s SYNC edge (analyser D12, or the VNA’s trigger input). Store the median plus 2 × the spread as latency_us. attcheck’s latency_us (command end → ok) is a lower bound to compare it with.

  7. Unit file: atten_cal.py unit --unit J1 --csv J1.csv --latency-us 2600 --out J1.json, then atten_cal.py check J1.json: insertion loss, range, worst step error, monotonicity.

  8. Bench file, after assembly (§12 step 15): measure each pair end to end at both units’ 0 dB (cut through, delay bank at 0 cm), then atten_cal.py bench --channel 2462 --map att0=J1.json,… --pairs 01=…,02=… --out bench_cal.json. Put it on the rig host and point HITL_MESHBENCH_CAL_JSON at it.

6. Scenarios: links that come and go and change length¶

scenarios.py builds on the board’s device names. It picks the node model when the board has att<i> units (the default) and the pair model for the legacy three-DSA table. compile():

  • merges steps;

  • drops values that didn’t change;

  • prepends the sync-LED start code (toggles at 0, 80, 160, 400, 480 and 800 ms, an unambiguous pattern for the camera);

  • toggles the LED at every labelled event;

  • sequences the UART attenuators around each step’s latch edge (§5.4) and spaces each unit’s commands ≥ 1 ms apart;

  • checks spacing, capacity (8192 entries) and leak paths.

A cut pair whose effective loss is under 100 dB is flagged, because there the “cut” is not real. In the node model the effective loss is the power sum of the direct path through the open switch and every one-hop detour (§2.1). The pair model keeps the revision 1 check (L_ij + L_jk).

7. Camera flip timing¶

7.1 Camera¶

Recommended (Q5): a global-shutter UVC camera with manual exposure up to the frame period, at 120 fps: an OV9281 (mono) or OV9782 (colour) USB board, about $60–90. Mono is enough, because the pattern is brightness only.

Global shutter removes the row offset. A rolling shutter works too (Pi Camera Module 3 at 120 fps), but the line time must then be known to 0.5 % (§7.5). Its readout must also fit the frame (about 8 µs per row at 120 fps).

Optics:

  • Mount the camera about 1 m above a 60 × 40 cm strip area. That gives about 1.8 px/mm, and WS2812 LEDs 16 mm apart land about 30 px apart.

  • Use a black, non-reflective background and a 1 cm black foam baffle between strips, so one LED’s light doesn’t spill into another’s region.

  • Fit an ND filter or stop down until a full-on LED sits at about 80 % of full scale with gain at minimum.

  • Turn off auto-exposure, auto white balance, power-line compensation and dynamic frame rate (the rig agent does this).

7.3 Estimator (flip_cv.py)¶

  1. Regions. Each LED’s region comes from the assembly layout (boxes for each strip and for the sync LED). Or detect them: the temporal-range image goes through a threshold and connected components, the idea of web/src/cv/diffuseDetect.ts applied to blinking.

  2. Series. Take the region’s mean per frame, linearised by the camera response LUT (§7.4). Normalise to x ∈ [0, 1] with plateau levels: the medians of the bottom and top 8 % bands, or levels measured in calibration. Note that 2-means fails at 60 fps, where every third frame is a partial.

  3. Flips. Between two plateau frames of opposite state:

    • One partial frame k gives the flip instant directly: rising t = s_k + E(1 − x_k), falling t = s_k + E·x_k. Here s_k is the exposure start of that region’s row, which includes the rolling-shutter offset row·line_time.

    • With no partial frame, the flip was at an exposure edge, and a plateau frame still holds a share above 5σ: use that share.

    • Otherwise the flip was in the gap. It is timestamped at the gap middle and excluded from fits.

  4. Offsets. Each flip gives (t − pattern time) mod period (rising = 0, falling = on-time). A Huber line fit over the window gives the offset at the window centre, the drift (DUT clock against camera clock), the residual RMS (render jitter + noise) and the standard error.

    • A window is untrusted if fewer than 25 % of its flips were timed inside an exposure. That is the commensurate-gap trap; untrusted windows are excluded and counted, never silently used.

  5. Skews. Per-LED differences are unwrapped coarse to fine. Per-flip skews pair each flip with the other fixture’s nearest flip of the same polarity, so the gate gets a distribution, not a mean.

  6. Camera time → controller time. Fit the sync-LED flips to the controller’s toggle instants (bench_core.fit_sync: tries ±4 index shifts with polarity matching, so a late camera start is fine). V4L2 timestamps are used only to number frames, which catches drops. Frame starts are a line fit of timestamp against frame number, so host timestamp jitter does not enter.

Camera clock errors common to all regions cancel in skews: start jitter, exposure-length error, clock offset and rate (test test_common_camera_jitter_cancels). Errors that don’t cancel are the row offset, response non-linearity, and per-LED latency.

7.4 Calibration (once per assembly, and on any optics change)¶

  1. Layout. Light each DUT’s strips in turn. detect_rois proposes boxes and the operator names them. The result is stored as HITL_MESHBENCH_LAYOUT_JSON in the catalog.

  2. Response. Exposure sweep (10 steps, 5–100 % of the frame) with every LED held on → LUT (Response.from_sweep). Without it, gamma 2.2 costs about 100 µs at a 4 ms skew (the error is about (g′ − 1) × skew). With it, the error is 15 µs.

  3. Levels. Hold all LEDs on, then off, for 1 s each. This is required below 100 fps.

  4. Rolling shutter only. Line time from one strip laid across 400+ rows. Its 3 LEDs latch at the same reset, so their apparent offsets fall on a line whose slope is the line time.

  5. LED stage. Camera offset minus analyser offset on the tapped strips. That is the data time plus the latch time plus the PWM restart, constant per LED part. With mixed parts or reels it becomes a per-LED correction.

  6. Zero check. Two strips of the same C6 (one RMT/PARLIO frame) must read 0 ± 50 µs apart.

8. Analyser phase (la_phase.py)¶

  • Signals. The WS2812 data lines (§3.2) plus SYNC. The analyser measures the wire: when each fixture’s frame data starts. The camera measures the light. Their difference is the LED stage (§7.4 step 5).

  • Sample rate. 10 MS/s: WS2812 bits are 1.25 µs; T0H 0.4 µs and T1H 0.8 µs are 4 and 8 samples, so decoding is clean. Timestamps have 0.1 µs resolution. 4 MS/s still gives frame starts, but decoding is marginal.

  • Decode. Edges → frames (split at idle-low > 50 µs) → the first 3 LEDs’ GRB → the pattern state of each frame → flips at the frame start. This feeds the same fit_offset as the camera, so camera and analyser skews are the same quantity.

  • Correlation with controller and mesh time. SYNC toggles once per commit. The analyser starts before play, with no commit in between, so its edges pair in order with the steps. A line fit maps analyser time onto controller µs (rms < 1 µs in tests).

  • Show time. Each fixture’s pattern encodes its show time, so its offset is display time minus shared show time. Relative to one fixture this is the skew. Against the controller it is absolute, once the harness logs the epoch the app set in controller time (via the clock map).

  • Also reported: frame periods (median, max) per channel, which catch render stalls; PARLIO lane skew (D10 and D11 against D8).

  • Frame-start inputs (FSTI). 12 RP2350 inputs record the first rising edge per frame, with a 2 ms holdoff, at µs resolution on the controller’s own clock. That gives always-on frame-start timing with no analyser. It has no data decode, so it can’t tell which frame is which by itself.

9.2 Harness and rig agent¶

hitl_mesh_bench.py runs these steps in order:

  1. Reserve mesh-bench (atomic, pin-only).

  2. Probe the instruments: SKIP if they are missing.

  3. Read the device table from the controller and compile the scenario.

  4. Optional --gate-cmd: start the DUT workload and wait for MESHBENCH READY.

  5. meshbench_rig.py run in the container:

    • safe state, a lease, and a selftest;

    • load the timeline and fit the clock map;

    • start ffmpeg (MJPEG copy, no re-encode) and sigrok-cli;

    • play +500 ms, renewing the lease while it plays;

    • drain the FSTI, read the log, return to safe.

  6. Fetch the video, frame timestamps, VCD and controller JSON.

  7. Analyse, then write results.json and the metrics.

9.3 Simulation (--simulate, benchsim.py)¶

The same pipeline runs with no hardware:

  • the real controller core (rfctl_sim --virtual) plays the scenario;

  • a toy mesh-time model reacts to link states:

    • cut-off nodes drift at their ppm;

    • they slew back at 0.5 ms/s on reconnect;

  • camsim renders the camera series (render jitter, a camera clock at +35 ppm, the sync LED);

  • synthetic WS2812 wires feed the analyser path;

  • the real analysis and verdict run on the result.

test_end_to_end_simulation runs link_flap and partition_heal this way. In the latter, nodes 2 and 3 drift more than 3 ms apart while cut off and reconverge 2–15 s after the heal.   • load meshFlipClock.fx;

  • print MESHBENCH READY after sync.

mesh_apply_at_skew should then also flip ev with apply_at_ms, so its apply skew is measured on the light (camera) and on the wire (analyser) next to its AP-beacon reference. Until then, the DUTs are meshed by an operator, or by any command passed as --gate-cmd.

10. HITL registration¶

pi/hitl/reserve/catalog-meshbench.json defines one unit:

  • Name and type. mesh-bench-1, type mesh-bench, pin_only (the c6-sdr pattern): caps-only netstack jobs can never land on conducted DUTs.

  • Components. c6-0…3 (esp32c6, env HITL_TTY[_N] / HITL_ADAPTER_SERIAL[_N]), p4-0/1 (_4, _5), rfctl-0 (HITL_RFCTL_TTY, HITL_MESHBENCH_FIXED_DB), cam-0 (HITL_CAMERA_DEV, HITL_CAMERA_LINE_TIME, HITL_MESHBENCH_LAYOUT_JSON), and la-0 (HITL_LA_DRIVER, HITL_LA_MAP).

  • Placeholders. Board serials are 00:00:00:00:C6:0n and 00:00:00:00:F4:0n, and the instruments are SERIAL_*. Assembly step 21 replaces them.

  • Checks. The catalog joins the PR-24 isolation and PR-30 stable-identity tests as its own host (mesh-rig).

No hitl-reserve engine feature is needed for the unit itself: composites, per-component env and pin_only already exist. Host-side follow-ups, through pi/hitl/nix (the allowed route), are all genuinely missing HITL features:

  1. a board target for the host (NanoPi needs a new sbc-deploy board; a Pi 5 needs none, Q1);

  2. container toolbox additions:

    • ffmpeg, v4l2-ctl and sigrok-cli with the DSLogic firmware;

    • picotool, so bootsel + load can reflash rfctl from a reservation;

  3. hitl-devices lists only DUTs 0–2: extend it to N;

  4. the privileged single-unit environment that c6-sdr uses, for the raw-USB analyser.

Owner rules.

  • Tests change only DUTs and the bench’s own instruments, never the host.

  • The pattern is an ordinary effect that never parks.

  • The bench sends no frames itself. The matrix monitor tap (RF-BENCH §2) lets a sniffer check the mesh frames for an Espressif OUI.

  • Untrusted windows are counted and reported, never retried away.

12. Assembly checklist (owner)¶

  1. Unpack and photograph the switches, the Jeanoko units (and the header by the OLED) and the delay lines. Answer Q2. Label the units J1–J5. Don’t power any RF part yet.

  2. VNA every delay cable (one-way delay) and label it. Calibrate every Jeanoko (§5.8 steps 1–7) → J1.json … J5.json.

  3. Fit the controller: the rfctl-host PCB (bare RP2350A, 6 × 74HC595, pulls; see the follow-up), on a 5 V supply.

  4. Flash rfctl.uf2 (§5.6). Check hello, selftest → chain=pass, and state (all safe). Probe a switch EN line: high. Plug one unit into ATT U0 and its USB-A power port: its OLED shows 31.75 within 5 s, and attcheck att0 → ok.

  5. Wire the switches to the chain per board_default.c and the units to ATT U0–U3 (U6 = the spare). Toggle each device with set <dev> <v> and check its lines with a meter or the analyser (or the unit’s OLED). Fix any wrong code in the table.

  6. Assemble the matrix in the aluminium enclosure (RF-BENCH §2): pair chains (cut switch + 30 dB pad), one unit between each DUT cable and its node splitter, splitters, the delay bank on pair 0–1, 50 Ω on every unused port, SMA torqued.

  7. Fit TinyC6 u.FL pigtails (mate once). Connect nodes 0–3. Terminate the node AUX and monitor ports.

  8. Wire the strips: 3 LEDs, 330 Ω, a 74AHCT125 buffer, green-only test with any effect. Use GPIO20 and GPIO14 on each TinyC6 and the 16 lanes on each P4 (§3.1). Strip 5 V comes from the separate supply on the P4s.

  9. Tap the analyser (D0–D11 on the 3.3 V side through 1 kΩ, D12 = GP7 SYNC, common GND) and the FSTI inputs (GP10–GP21, same taps).

  10. Mount the sync LED (GP8) in a corner of the camera’s view.

  11. Mount the camera about 1 m over the strips. Add the black background, baffles and ND filter. Close the enclosure.

  12. Host: Pi 5 or NanoPi (Q1). Put the DUT hub, camera and analyser on separate USB controllers (§4). Use powered hubs.

  13. Power-check: journalctl -k | grep -c over-current stays flat while all DUTs transmit.

  14. Isolation (RF-BENCH §5): per pair with its cables unplugged, a CW tone at max power from one DUT, raw RSSI on the other. Record L_leak. Set the bench TX power rule (TX ≤ L_leak − 99 − 10 dBm).

  15. Path-loss calibration: every pair end to end with both its units at 0 dB → atten_cal.py bench → bench_cal.json on the rig host, HITL_MESHBENCH_CAL_JSON (§5.8 step 8). Spot-check 3 random unit states per pair against Bench.path_loss: ≤ 0.5 dB.

  16. Camera calibration (§7.4): layout boxes → HITL_MESHBENCH_LAYOUT_JSON; exposure-sweep response LUT; on/off levels.

  17. Zero check: two strips of one C6 read 0 ± 50 µs on camera and analyser.

  18. Camera against analyser on the tapped strips (baseline, 30 s): agreement within 0.3 ms. Commit that recording as testdata/recorded/first/.

  19. Dry run: mesh_bench --scenario baseline with DUTs meshed by an operator (or --gate-cmd).

  20. Run every scenario once and look at results.json and the plots.

  21. Fill the real USB serials into catalog-meshbench.json (replacing the placeholders) and deploy the host (follow-ups 1–4 in §10).

  22. Add mesh_bench to the HITL lane (manual first, then periodic).

13. Risks and open items¶

  • Isolation without shield boxes may cap “cut” at 100–110 dB (RF-BENCH §5). Run at low TX power and measure first.

  • Detours bypass the relay’s unit (§2.1). A cut beside a live common neighbour is only c + I (52.5 dB with 30 dB pads) below the same pair through. The pad sets it; measure the Wilkinson port-to-port isolation, which is half of it.

  • The Jeanoko interface is from reviews of the same module family, not from this listing. The header pinout and level, wv0XXYY, ok and t_proc are unconfirmed until the units arrive (Q2). USB (§5.7) is the fallback.

  • Jeanoko insertion loss at 2.44 GHz is 1.5 dB on the listing and ~6 dB in the review. That moves the min loss by 9 dB per pair. The VNA decides.

  • The switch truth table decides the safe state (Q2).

  • The time precision in the VM. If effect time is float32 seconds, its step at 600 s of show time is 61 µs. The workload should re-base the time base at each run start; check it on the first device run.

  • The host’s USB topology (Q1) is the most likely source of flakes, as on rig-3.

  • The P4 16-lane encoder isn’t merged. Until it is, lanes 2–15 stay dark (their strips are wired anyway).

  • The camera estimator is validated only on synthetic and MJPEG-round-trip video until step 18.
