Mesh M4: mesh time and apply_at across the mesh¶

M4 of mesh.md §15: mesh time from time beacons (§6.2), the show epoch mapped onto each member’s existing time base (§6.3), so a change scheduled with apply_at_ms lands on every member at the same instant. Stacked on M3 (#565). Gate: skew < 10 ms.

Measured with //pi/hitl/harness:mesh_apply_at_skew on hitl-rig-1 (desk #69): three C6 players (c6-123170, c6-711130, c6-711f48) on the rig AP, each playing plasmaHueFixed (60 LEDs, 80 fps), built as :esp32c6_netstack_meshdev (the default image plus the dev-only MESH KEY). Logs and JSON: /workspace/.claude/mesh/m4/runs/.

Results¶

Run r3 (2026-11-19, PASS; r1 and r2 also PASS, below). “Skew” is the spread across the three DUTs of when a scheduled set_brightness{apply_at_ms} applied, against the AP reference.

Budget left. Of the 10 ms gate, mesh time uses ~0.05 ms. The rest of the measured skew (0.3-2 ms) is each member’s frame start: render sleeps in 1 ms FreeRTOS ticks, so its frames land within a tick of the grid point, and under BLE coex or a busy loop a frame can start a tick or two late. A sub-tick wake (an esp_timer one-shot) would take it to ~0.1 ms; not needed for the gate.

How the skew is measured¶

The rig has no logic analyser on the C6 strips (its analyser’s one channel is on pi-ledmapper-1), so the reference is the rig AP’s beacons. Every member hears each beacon at the same instant (± propagation, nanoseconds). Once per AP-TSF second each member logs the AP’s TSF, the beacon’s hardware RX time on its own clock (the RX vector’s stamp, mapped onto esp_timer the way mesh time maps every frame), and its mesh time at that instant:

  • Agreement is the difference of the members’ mesh values for one beacon: their disagreement at that instant, with nothing in between.

  • Apply skew: each member logs esp_timer at the start of the frame that applied the change; that instant maps onto the AP’s TSF through the member’s own (rx, tsf) pairs (linear between the two bracketing beacons, so its crystal’s drift against the AP is taken out). The spread across members, per round, is the skew. Mesh time is not used.

  • Error bars of the reference: the scatter of each member’s (rx, tsf) pairs about a line (oracle_rms_us), which contains the RX-stamp jitter, the RX-stamp-to-esp_timer mapping’s wobble and the AP’s own beacon timing. Agreement and skew are good to about that much (tens of µs).

  • Cross-check without the radio: the serial bridge stamps every console line with the rig’s CLOCK_MONOTONIC (all three reservation containers share it). The apply lines’ stamps give the skew through USB serial (latency jitter ~1-4 ms): an upper bound that agrees with the AP reference to within that jitter.

  • What “applied” means: the start of the render frame that applied the change. The LEDs show that frame after its compute and its transmit (60 LEDs), the same work on every member, so the visible skew is the frame-start skew plus the members’ compute-time jitter (sub-ms on this effect; not separately measured: the rig has no analyser on the C6 strips).

Design¶

TX stamp: two-step (M3 handoff)¶

The C6 has no hardware TX timestamp and its MAC can hold an armed frame ~10 ms while BLE has the radio, so a stamp taken at seal time is wrong by the queue and the hold. Of M3’s three options:

  • Two-step (chosen). The backhaul brackets each time beacon’s completion: it reads esp_timer before every MAC poll, so the completion lies between the last poll that saw the frame in flight and the poll that saw it done; the midpoint is the stamp, half the bracket its error. The next beacon carries it. Receivers drop stamps with a bracket over 150 µs (the poll was preempted, or the MAC reported late). Broadcast, never retried.

  • Two-way unicast exchanges (NTP-style) still need TX times on both ends and are O(n²) unicasts; a retried unicast carries the first attempt’s stamp.

  • The probable ACK-TX timestamp (RX-vector bytes 76-79) times the ACK we send for a frame we received; it says nothing about when our own beacon left. Not used.

Two-way over broadcasts. Each beacon also carries the sender’s current fit of each neighbour. The two directions sum to -2L (L: the constant delay from “done” to the receiver’s RX stamp: airtime, the RX vector’s reference point, the poll), so L drops out of the offset without knowing what the RX vector stamps.

The backhaul exposes every frame’s (armed_us, done_us) (WifiP2p::last_tx, TxStamp) and its worst hold (hold_max_us).

RX clock: the MAC’s µs counter¶

The RX vector’s 1 MHz stamp is not the TSF counter at 0x600AD020: on our netstack that register reads frozen (≈1.4 s, the MAC’s start) raw and after the vendor latch sequence (r1, tsf probe). A scan of the MAC blocks (MESH REG, dev image, r2) found the clock at 0x600AD000: it advances 1 µs per µs and runs a constant 0.688 s behind esp_timer, exactly the RX stamps’ offset. The transport reads it next to esp_timer every loop pass (RxClock::anchor, reads with a bracket over 6 µs skipped), and every RX stamp maps exactly: in r3 all frames mapped through it, the smallest reap lag was +142 µs (so the stamps are this clock), the counter’s drift against esp_timer −14 ppb (the same crystal) and no jumps. M3’s reap-lag floor stays as the per-frame fallback for an implausible mapping. Agreement went from max 169-221 µs (floor, r1/r2) to 47 µs (r3).

Mesh time (firmware/mesh/src/meshtime.rs)¶

  • Beacons: link FRAME_TYPE_TIME (swarm_wire.proto, new), one hop, sealed in the mesh envelope (members only; replays fail its counter window), every 500 ms (jittered).

  • Per-neighbour fit: a least-squares line (offset and drift) over the last 12 samples, outliers (> 5 MADs) dropped, reset after 3 outliers in a row (the neighbour rebooted).

  • States: unsynced joins the synced neighbours’ aggregate; if none is synced after 1.5 s it founds mesh time at the latest clock it hears (nobody’s time steps back); forming (10 s): founders converge on the latest, a synced neighbourhood wins; synced: timesync’s Marzullo over itself and its neighbours, then the inverse-variance mean of the agreeing sources; steps need a quorum, slews ≤ 500 ppm.

  • No root, no leader: every member is one source among its neighbours (§6.2).

The show epoch: a time field outside the admin port¶

TIME_BASE is an admin-class port, which needs M5’s unlocked gateway session. M4 carries the epoch in the time beacon instead: (generation, origin, epoch in mesh µs | cleared), gossiped by every member, newest generation wins. On each member, mesh_ffi.rs maps it onto the player’s time base (player().set_time_base_ms, main.cpp lm_mesh_tb, a try-lock so the mesh never waits on a frame) and re-maps it as mesh time slews, moving the queued apply_at changes by the same amount so they keep their frames. A set_time_base the app sends to this device originates the next generation (MeshStack::originate_epoch): that is the seam M5 restricts to the gateway’s admin session.

Frames on the grid¶

Without more, a member applies a change at its first frame start after the due time, and frame phases are independent: at 80 fps two members would differ by up to 12.5 ms. While the mesh’s time base is set:

  • render paces its frames onto epoch + k·period (at most a quarter period of correction per frame, so the cadence never jumps);

  • a scheduled change applies on grid frame ceil(apply_at / period): the due time is compared with the frame’s grid instant, not with millis(), so a frame start that jitters by a tick still applies on the same frame on every member.

So every member changes on the same frame index (24/24 rounds in r1), and what remains is mesh-time disagreement plus each member’s 1 ms tick phase.

Cost¶

  • RAM, default image (:esp32c6_netstack vs M3): .dram0.data and .dram0.bss byte-identical. The one new static (g_tb_align, 1 B) sits in existing padding.

  • Heap while on: 40 460 B (93 112 → 52 652; the block is 40 336 B: M3’s state + 14 KB tick stack + mesh time 2.6 KB + a 1 KB log), against M3’s 36 956 B; back to 93 112 after MESH OFF. The create task still dips the heap minimum to 25.6 KB once at ON.

  • Flash: +28.4 KB text, +2.4 KB rodata over M3 (+31.1 KB; with #569’s size diet the default app is 2 189 088 B, 104 672 B under layout_test’s 64 KiB margin; desk D-86). About a third is the console reports and logs; the clock fit, aggregation and beacon codec are the rest (i64 only: no 128-bit division linked).

  • Stack (call graph over the ELF, auipc+jalr long calls resolved, M3’s method): ns_mesh_create 22.6 KB (unchanged, 26 KB task), ns_mesh_tick 8.4 KB (M3: 8.6 KB with the same walk; its own 14 KB stack), lm_mesh::service frame 496 B (+32 B).

Open¶

  • A ~32 µs constant offset of DUT a against b and c (b and c agree within ~10 µs) in every r3 phase. The two-way estimate cancels a symmetric delay; this is an asymmetric one on a’s links (most likely its done-poll latency: a’s pump loop differs, e.g. it held the app’s session). Harmless at this gate; the next step if anyone needs < 10 µs.

  • Coarse stamps: 14-17 % of completion brackets exceed 150 µs (BLE holds up to 18 ms, loop preemption) and are not used; one beacon in six then carries no sample.

  • Who may originate a show epoch: any member today (closed mode: every member holds the site secret). M5 gates originate_epoch on the gateway’s admin session; locked/open modes need a signed epoch (the admin TIME_BASE port).

  • Two synced meshes merging with clocks seconds apart and equal sizes have no quorum to step, so they converge only at the 500 ppm slew. Not reachable AP-anchored on one site; revisit with partitions (M10).

  • stack::mesh_time_s (grant expiry) still runs on the local clock.

  • //tools/stack_bound is not fixed here (M3’s finding); the numbers above are the call-graph walk.

What M5 (gateway fan-out from the phone) needs from M4¶

  • Time base: nothing new. The phone’s set_time_base on the gateway already originates the mesh epoch; members apply it. M5 decides who may originate (gate MeshStack::originate_epoch behind the gateway’s session) and may move the epoch onto the admin TIME_BASE port for locked/open modes (signed epochs).

  • Relayed control with apply_at_ms: dispatch Event::Control’s inner ClientMessage through lm_player_handle as if it came from the app; the member’s time base is already the mesh’s, so apply_at_ms means the same instant everywhere. Lead: ≥ 150 ms one hop.

  • Mesh time API: MeshStack::time.mesh_us(local) / local_us(mesh) / synced(), for send_live keyframes (interp) and MeshStatus (state, offset, error, peers).

  • Grants: stack::mesh_time_s still runs on the local clock (TODO(M4) kept): switch it to mesh time when M5 validates grant expiry across members.
