Smart-home integrations: feasibility study¶

Status: study only. Nothing here is built. Written 2026-11-20 for the website team, who need a realistic status for the store page. All web sources were read on 2026-11-20. Figures marked (est.) are our estimates, not published numbers.

TL;DR¶

  • Matter on the lamp’s ESP32-C6 is not feasible on today’s image. Matter needs IPv6, UDP, lwIP-style sockets, its own BLE commissioning and about 1.5 MB of flash. Our heapless netstack is IPv4 TCP only, the vendor lwIP is gone on purpose, and we have about 266 KB of flash headroom. Thread does not change this.

  • The cheap, broad route is a Matter bridge in the Splanc Agent (the Pi bridge appliance, or any always-on computer running splanc-agent). It exposes every lamp as a bridged Matter light. It costs nothing on the lamp and covers Apple Home, Home Assistant and probably Alexa and SmartThings while uncertified. Google Home will not accept an uncertified Matter device for normal users.

  • Home Assistant first. A HACS custom integration that talks our protocol straight to the lamps reaches the most technical early buyers for the least money. HA’s own HomeKit Bridge and Google/Alexa connectors then cover the other ecosystems for HA users at no extra cost to us.

  • Prerequisite for everything: the planned lamp on/off state, the on/off transition effects and a pushed state report (so a touch on the lid shows up in the smart home). Without these, every integration is just “brightness 0”.

  • Never ship HomeKit (HAP) directly on the lamp or in our bridge commercially without MFi. Never claim “Works with Google Home / Apple Home / Alexa” on the store page until the relevant programme says so.

3. Route 1: Matter over Wi-Fi on the lamp’s C6¶

3.1 What Matter needs¶

A Matter Wi-Fi device (connectedhomeip, packaged for Espressif as esp-matter) needs:

  • IPv6 (link-local plus SLAAC/ULA) with ICMPv6 neighbour discovery and MLD. Google states plainly: “IPv6 must be enabled on your home wireless network” [G-hub].

  • UDP transport with Matter’s own reliability layer (MRP), optionally TCP.

  • DNS-SD over mDNS for _matter._tcp (operational) and _matterc._udp (commissionable) discovery, over IPv6.

  • Commissioning: normally BLE (BTP over a GATT service) to hand over Wi-Fi credentials, then PASE (SPAKE2+) for the setup code and CASE (sigma, P-256 ECDH/ECDSA) for each fabric. Every controller (“multi-admin”) adds a fabric.

  • Attestation: a per-device DAC, signed by a PAI, signed by a CSA-approved PAA, plus a Certification Declaration from the CSA.

  • Persistent storage for fabrics, ACLs, session resumption and counters.

On ESP32 the CHIP platform layer is written against lwIP (via esp_netif) and against NimBLE or Bluedroid.

3.4 Matter over Thread on the C6¶

The C6 has an 802.15.4 radio, but:

  • It has one RF path shared by Wi-Fi, BLE and 802.15.4, and “802.15.4 receive operations have the lowest priority” [IDF-coex]. Our product needs Wi-Fi for effects upload, the app and the agent, so Thread would run in leftover airtime.

  • esp-matter builds a C6 device as Wi-Fi or Thread, not both.

  • Thread adds OpenThread (more RAM and flash) and needs a border router in the home (HomePod mini, Apple TV 4K, Nest Hub 2nd gen, Nest Wifi Pro).

  • It removes none of the Matter costs above, and adds Thread Group certification.

Verdict: does not help. A Thread-only lamp would also lose the Splanc app protocol. Never pursue it for the current product.

On top of that:

  • Authorised Test Lab (ATL) testing. Prices are quoted per project and not published. A community estimate puts fees plus ATL at about $19.5 k for one product [ND-cost]. We estimate $8-20 k per product (est.), more if re-testing is needed. Pre-testing with the Matter Test Harness reduces the risk of re-tests.

  • Vendor ID: issued with membership; Google verifies ownership of a production VID before you can use it in its console [G-test].

  • DAC/PAI attestation. Each device needs a unique DAC chained to a PAI and a CSA-approved PAA. Providers (SSL.com, DigiCert, Kudelski, and Espressif’s pre-provisioning service) quote by volume and publish no prices [SSL-dac]. Expect ~$0.10-$1.00 per device plus a PAI set-up fee in the low thousands (est.), and a factory step that injects the DAC and key into each lamp (flash or the C6’s eFuse/HMAC-protected storage).

  • Wi-Fi Alliance / Bluetooth SIG. A pre-certified module (ESP32-C6 modules carry Wi-Fi and BLE listings) avoids most separate radio fees. Matter certification itself does not require Wi-Fi certification of the end product, but Google and Apple ecosystem programmes may ask for it (est., verify per programme).

  • The Certification Transfer Program (rebranding an already certified product) only applies to an unchanged white-label product. It does not cover our own firmware.

  • There is no fee-free certified path. The “certification-free” path is simply shipping uncertified with a test VID, see 3.6.

Total first year for one certified product (est.): Adopter $7.5 k + certification $3 k + ATL $8-20 k + PAI/DAC set-up $1-5 k, so about $20-35 k and 3-6 months. After that, $7.5 k a year in dues plus fees for each recertification and each new product.

4. Route 2: a Matter bridge in the Splanc Agent¶

4.1 Shape¶

Apple Home / HA / Alexa / (Google)  ──Matter over IPv6 on the LAN──▶  Splanc Agent bridge
                                                                       (Pi appliance or any always-on host)
                                                                         matter.js Aggregator endpoint
                                                                           ├─ bridged Extended Color Light: "Coil (bedroom)"

                                                                           └─ ...

                                                                                    │ existing app protocol
                                                                                    ▼
                                                                                 lamps (no change)

  • Implementation: matter.js (@matter/node), the TypeScript Matter implementation that Matterbridge and Home Assistant Matter Hub build on [MJS], [MB], [HAMH]. It fits the agent’s Node/TS codebase and the existing hub.ts (discovery plus one session per player).

  • Mapping: each player becomes a bridged Extended Color Light (or a Dimmable Light if colour is not offered): OnOff → planned SetPower with its transition effect; LevelControl → SetBrightness; ColorControl → a colour uniform convention; Scenes Management → keypoints. Name and room from the player registry; reachability from hub liveness.

  • Commissioning: the bridge itself commissions (on-network, via QR or manual code shown on the Pi’s HDMI/OLED screen and in the app). No BLE is needed because the Pi is wired or already on Wi-Fi.

  • Lamp cost: zero RAM and zero flash, beyond the protocol prerequisites in section 1.

4.2 Effort and cost¶

  • Uncertified MVP: 2-4 weeks (est.) for on/off/level/colour/scenes, persistence of fabrics in $SPLANC_BRIDGE_STATE_DIR, a pairing screen on the bridge display, and tests against Apple Home, HA and Alexa.

  • Ongoing: matter.js and controller releases. Matter 1.x minor versions arrive about twice a year, so budget a few days per release.

  • Running cost: none. It is all local.

4.3 Certification of the bridge¶

A bridge is certified as one product (an Aggregator). Bridged devices are not certified individually. The cost is the same as section 3.5 (about $20-35 k in the first year, est.). Problems:

  • A software bridge running on user-supplied hardware (any Pi or any computer) is awkward to certify, because the certified product has to match what ships. A dedicated “Splanc Bridge” box sold by us is certifiable; a curl-installed agent is not.

  • Every bridge needs its own DAC. For a box we sell, that is a factory step; for a software install it would mean issuing DACs from a cloud service, which is unusual and needs PAA policy review.

  • Comparable open-source bridges (Matterbridge, HA Matter Hub) ship uncertified and rely on Apple/HA/Alexa tolerance.

4.4 UX¶

  • Good: one pairing step covers every lamp, current and future. New lamps appear automatically. It works with Apple Home today (with “Add Anyway”).

  • Bad: the user must own and run an always-on bridge. Google Home users are shut out until the bridge is certified. If the bridge is down, every lamp drops out of the smart home (the Splanc app still works).

  • The bridge already exists for cloud agents, so it now has two jobs. That strengthens the case for selling or recommending a bridge.

5. Route 3: Home Assistant¶

HA is where technical early buyers are. It also re-exports devices to Apple Home (HomeKit Bridge), Google and Alexa (Nabu Casa cloud or manual set-up), so one integration gives HA users every ecosystem.

5.1 Native integration (HACS, then core)¶

  • Shape: a Python custom component splanc. It discovers lamps by zeroconf (_splanc._tcp, already advertised), has a config flow with trust-on-first-use of the per-device certificate (as agent/src/trust.ts does), keeps one wss session per lamp, and creates a light entity (on/off, brightness, effect list = keypoints and library effects, RGB if the colour convention lands) plus diagnostic sensors.

  • Protocol: reuse shared/protocol/python (the generated protobuf package). Publish it to PyPI, because core requires the device library to live outside HA.

  • Session budget: the lamp caps concurrent TLS sessions at 2, at about 28 KB each (ram-budget.md), and the agent keeps “one socket per player” (cloud-relay.md). HA plus the agent uses both slots, so the phone app is locked out. The integration therefore needs a mode that talks to the bridge’s hub instead of the lamp when a bridge exists. This is the main design risk for direct-to-lamp. A third session slot is not affordable on the C6.

  • Effort (est.): HACS release 2-3 weeks. Core submission needs the Bronze tier of the quality scale (18 mandatory rules: UI config flow, tests, docs, a PyPI library) [HA-qs]. The core review queue typically takes weeks to months. HACS integrations are not graded or supported by the HA project.

  • Maintenance: low to medium. Pin to a protocol version, and run its CI against HA’s monthly releases.

5.2 MQTT discovery¶

  • On the lamp: an MQTT client means a new outbound TCP client in the netstack, a broker address setting, MQTT framing (~10-20 KB flash, a few KB RAM, est.), plus TLS if the broker needs it (another ~28 KB session: not affordable alongside wss). Users must run a broker. Not recommended.

  • On the bridge/agent: mqtt.js plus HA discovery payloads is about a week (est.). It is generic: Node-RED, openHAB and others also speak MQTT. It is a good cheap add-on once the bridge has a state model, but it is inferior to the native integration for HA itself.

5.3 ESPHome-style exposure¶

  • Flashing ESPHome replaces our firmware and loses every Splanc feature. Never.

  • Emulating ESPHome’s native API on the lamp (protobuf over TCP 6053 with a Noise PSK, advertised as _esphomelib._tcp) would let HA auto-discover the lamp with no custom component. RAM is small (a few KB, est.), but it is a private protocol that changes with ESPHome releases, needs a second TCP listener, and presents the lamp to HA as an ESPHome device. Defer: interesting, but a support trap.

7. Route 5: Google Home / Alexa cloud-to-cloud¶

  • How: each platform calls our cloud. Google sends SYNC, QUERY and EXECUTE intents and expects Report State; Alexa sends Smart Home directives. Our cloud forwards them through the relay to the user’s bridge, then to the lamps.

  • Needs:

    • an OAuth 2.0 authorisation-code server and user accounts. Google requires account linking for all users and support for several Google users per account [G-al]. We have no accounts today: the relay pairs bridges by token, with no account and no hosting bill.

    • a fulfilment endpoint. A Worker route is fine for Google. Alexa Smart Home skills are normally fulfilled by an AWS Lambda (free tier likely sufficient, est.).

    • always a bridge, because the cloud cannot reach the lamp without one.

  • Certification: Google: a Test Suite run, an integration test with a test account we supply, and a policy review. A rejected integration cannot be resubmitted as-is [G-cert]. No fee is published. Alexa: skill certification (~5 days); “Works with Alexa” badging adds lab testing negotiated with an authorised lab [WWA].

  • Effort (est.): OAuth/accounts 2-3 weeks (new security surface), Google fulfilment 2-3 weeks, Alexa 2-3 weeks, plus review cycles.

  • Ongoing cost: within Cloudflare free-tier limits at small scale (100 k Worker requests/day), but it adds a cloud dependency, accounts and personal data, and with them privacy-policy and account-deletion duties.

  • Why it matters: it is the only route into Google Home for normal users without paying the CSA, because Google’s C2C certification has no published fee while uncertified Matter is blocked.

  • Risks: cloud latency on a local device, security of a remote-control path into homes, and platform churn (Google is steering partners toward Matter).

8. Route 6: other options¶

  • SmartThings: supports Matter (the bridge route probably works; test it) and community Edge drivers (Lua, local LAN). A community driver is possible, but SmartThings is low priority.

  • IFTTT: publishing a service needs a paid IFTTT business plan, and it only reaches the cloud. Skip it.

  • Documented local API: the cheapest win for tinkerers. Document the app protocol (shared/protocol, the Python package) and the agent’s MCP tools, and optionally add a tiny local REST/JSON endpoint on the bridge (/lamps, /lamps/:id/power). Node-RED, openHAB, Hubitat and scripts can then integrate themselves. About 1 week (est.).

  • The AI assistant path already exists: the Splanc MCP connector lets Claude and other agents control lamps today. It is a real, shippable claim.

10. Recommended phased approach¶

Phase 0: prerequisites (in the planned lamp work). Lamp on/off state, the touch toggle, ON/OFF transition effects, a SetPower arm and an unsolicited PowerState report, and a colour convention (a well-known color uniform or a built-in solid-colour effect). Every route needs these. Owner decision D1.

Phase 1: most users, least cost (about 1-1.5 months total, est.).

  1. Home Assistant custom integration (HACS), talking to the lamp directly, with an option to go through the bridge hub when the agent holds the session. Document HA’s HomeKit Bridge and Google/Alexa exposure.

  2. Uncertified Matter bridge in the agent/Pi bridge (matter.js). It reaches Apple Home and HA natively, and Alexa/SmartThings after testing.

  3. Local API docs for the protocol and bridge (Node-RED etc.).

Phase 2: decide on Google, driven by demand. Choose between: (a) Google + Alexa cloud-to-cloud through the relay (no CSA fees, but accounts and a cloud path), or (b) CSA Adopter membership and a certified Splanc Bridge box (~$20-35 k, est.). Measure demand first: store-page interest and HA/Matter usage. Owner decisions D3/D4.

Phase 3 (deferred): HA core submission once the integration is stable and the protocol is versioned. Also deferred: MQTT on the bridge and ESPHome emulation.

Defer indefinitely: Matter on the C6 (revisit only for a new 8 MB / P4+C6 SKU with a Matter requirement); IFTTT.

Never: HAP/HomeKit directly on the lamp or in our bridge for sale without MFi; Matter over Thread on the current lamp; flashing ESPHome; an MQTT client with TLS on the C6; a second TLS session slot on the C6 for integrations.

11. What the store page can honestly say today¶

Today, no third-party smart-home platform is supported. Suggested copy:

Control: the free Splanc app (Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, on your phone or in the browser), and AI assistants such as Claude through the Splanc connector.

Smart-home platforms: Home Assistant and Matter (Apple Home and others through the Splanc Bridge) are on our roadmap. We’ll update this page when they ship. Splanc lamps are not Matter-certified and do not currently work with Google Home, Apple Home or Alexa.

Do not use “Works with …” badges or ecosystem logos. Those need each programme’s approval, and the CSA logo needs certification. Once Phase 1 ships, the page can say “Home Assistant integration (community install via HACS)” and “Apple Home via the Splanc Bridge (Matter, uncertified: iOS shows an ‘Uncertified Accessory’ prompt)”. “Google Home” can appear only after Phase 2.

12. Owner decisions¶

Each decision is separate. (rec.) marks the recommendation.

D1. Lamp power-state protocol (prerequisite).

  • (a) (rec.) Add SetPower + an unsolicited PowerState report as part of the planned touch/on-off work, designed with smart-home mapping in mind.

  • (b) Model “off” as brightness 0 and add the state later.

  • (c) Postpone all smart-home work until the touch feature ships.

D2. First integration to build.

  • (a) (rec.) HA custom integration (HACS) first, then the uncertified Matter bridge.

  • (b) Uncertified Matter bridge first (it covers Apple Home and HA in one go, but needs a bridge).

  • (c) Both in parallel.

  • (d) Neither for now.

D3. Google Home strategy.

  • (a) (rec.) Defer. Decide after Phase 1 shows demand.

  • (b) Cloud-to-cloud (Google + Alexa) through the relay, accepting user accounts and OAuth.

  • (c) CSA membership + a certified bridge (see D4).

  • (d) Never support Google directly; point users at HA’s Google exposure.

D4. CSA membership / Matter certification spend (~$20-35 k first year, est.).

  • (a) (rec.) Not now. Revisit when a hardware bridge SKU exists or Google demand is proven.

  • (b) Join as Adopter ($7,500/yr) now and certify a Splanc Bridge box.

  • (c) Join and certify a Matter-native lamp (needs a new SKU, see D5).

D5. Matter on the lamp itself.

  • (a) (rec.) No for the current C6/4 MB lamp. Record it as a requirement to evaluate for the P4+C6 / 8 MB hardware generation.

  • (b) Start a netstack IPv6/UDP + CHIP port spike now (3-6 engineer-months, est.).

  • (c) Build a separate Matter-only firmware image (esp-matter, without the Splanc protocol) as an option users can flash.

D6. Selling a Splanc Bridge box.

  • (a) (rec.) Keep the bridge as software on the user’s Pi for now; position it as “recommended for smart-home and AI features”.

  • (b) Sell a pre-imaged Pi bridge (prerequisite for a certified bridge).

  • (c) Fold bridge duties into a future P4-class lamp so it needs no separate box.

D7. Store-page wording.

  • (a) (rec.) Publish the section 11 copy now: no claims, roadmap mention only.

  • (b) Say nothing about smart homes until Phase 1 ships.

  • (c) Mention HA/Matter as “coming soon” with a target quarter.

D8. HomeKit via HAP.

  • (a) (rec.) Never ship HAP. Use Matter for Apple Home and document HA’s HomeKit Bridge and Homebridge for users who want it.

  • (b) Apply to MFi for a HAP product.
