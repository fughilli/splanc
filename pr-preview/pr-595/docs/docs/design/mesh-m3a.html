Mesh spike M3a: Splanc frames on our netstack (results)¶

The spike that gates M3 (docs/design/mesh.md §4.4, PR #516). Two ESP32-C6 DUTs on hitl-rig-1 run our netstack (//firmware/player_app:esp32c6_netstack_meshspike, no vendor Wi-Fi on the mesh path) and exchange Splanc Public Action frames: category 4, action 9, the Splanc CID 0A:5C:4D, kind 0xF3 (link probe, test-only), each carrying an M2 envelope (AES-128-CCM, long header, 64-bit counter, MIC 8, replay window) under a dev-only key. No Espressif OUI and no ESP-NOW framing anywhere.

Measured by //pi/hitl/harness:mesh_smoke (runs r4, r5 and r6 on the rebased image, all on 2026-11-19; full logs and JSON under /workspace/.claude/mesh/m3a/runs/). The boards sit centimetres apart (RSSI −8 to −22 dBm), so these numbers say what the radio path does, not what range it has (that is R0).

Byte 0 (signed dBm). Moves with TX power exactly as expected (−19 → −29 → −39 dBm for power bytes 0x14 → 0x0C → 0x04)

Found: bytes 12–15, a 1 MHz clock a constant ~0.70 s behind esp_timer (offset stable to ~2 ms over minutes)

TX timestamp

No hardware TX timestamp for our own frames; completion is polled (µs-resolution software stamp). The receiver’s vector does carry a probable ACK-TX timestamp (bytes 76–79, present only on unicast to us: RX ts + 2 130 µs for a 260-byte 1 Mb/s frame)

Loss with BLE active

Gateway holding a BLE central: broadcast 79–97 % either way across r4–r6 (depends on the connection’s timing); unicast both ways 100 %

mesh_smoke’s gate: ≥ 95 % both ways, idle and associated, for unicast and for broadcast with advertising off; timestamp validated; no DUT reset. r5 and r6: PASS.

What the spike found¶

Our direct TX path sent everything at 1 Mb/s with “no ACK expected”. PLCP1’s rate field (bits 12–16) was always 0, and PLCP0’s ack-type field (bits 24–26) 0. With ack-type 0 the MAC never waits for an ACK, so there was no completion status and no possibility of a retry. M3a adds TxRing::set_rate_ex / arm_ex (ack-type 1) and the completion poll (TXQ_STATE_B bit q, the PMD word’s status nibble: 0 OK, 5 ACK timeout).

Retries are software on the C6 (lmacProcessTxComplete in the blob): the MAC reports one attempt; the driver re-arms. The spike retries up to 4 attempts with the Retry bit, the same sequence number, and a growing backoff (2, 4, 6 ms + jitter). Without the backoff, all 4 attempts could land inside the same BLE event and the frame was lost (r3: 94–99.5 %); with it, 100 %. The receiver’s replay window drops the duplicates a lost ACK produces before any AES.

The rate encoding is the vendor’s wifi_phy_rate_t (read off silicon by //firmware/netstack:wifi_tx_rate_probe, the vendor stack as an oracle): PLCP1 = rate << 12 | (length + 4); RESP_DUR carries the response rate (6, 12 or 24 Mb/s for OFDM). 6, 12 and 24 Mb/s OFDM and 2/5.5/11 Mb/s CCK all go out on our path; the receiver’s RX vector reports the same code. Golden words are in p2p.rs tests.

BLE advertising costs broadcast ~4–8 %. The player advertises every 200 ms; each event blinds the receiver for one 1 Mb/s frame. Lost probes fall ~20 apart at 10 ms spacing, and with advertising stopped (MESH BLEADV 0) broadcast is 100 %. A BLE connection stops advertising, but the connection’s own windows take its place: with a central attached, broadcast ran 96–97 % in r4 and 79–82 % in r5/r6 (in r5, every frame the gateway armed inside its BLE window, every 50 ms, was lost; the negotiated interval is not logged yet). Unicast retries recover every case.

High rates saturate at centimetre range. 11 and 24 Mb/s broadcasts lost up to 24 % at power byte 0x14 and RSSI −16 dBm; at 0x0C/0x04 (−24 to −39 dBm) they delivered 97–99 %. The field kit’s 24 Mb/s numbers at short range carry the same effect.

Latency. Ping (unicast, ACK + retries) RTT p50 6 ms at 1 Mb/s, 2–3 ms at 11/6/24 Mb/s; p90 7–12 ms idle, 14 ms with BLE connected, 23 ms associated. The estimated one-way (RTT minus the peer’s turnaround, halved) is 0.5–3 ms. Both loop tasks are inside these numbers.

Crypto. Seal 0.23 ms, open 0.22 ms per 200-byte body (ccmp.rs, HW AES).

RAM and the enable switch (owner, desk #68)¶

The default player image has none of this code (no mesh_spike/ns_mesh_/ns_p2p_ symbols; .bss identical). In the spike image every buffer lives in one 5 720-byte heap block that MESH ON allocates and MESH OFF frees (free heap 87.7 KB on, 93.3 KB off); the transport only holds the pointer, and with it null both RX paths reap exactly as before. M3 moves that switch into mainline as is.

Follow-ups for M3¶

  • Unicast with ACK + retries + backoff for anything that must arrive (replies to the gateway, effect blobs). Done in the spike; move to the backhaul.

  • Broadcast vs BLE: pick one (owner): slower or paused advertising while meshing, mesh TX scheduled outside the gateway’s BLE windows (§4.3, M3b), or flood redundancy so one missed frame per 200 ms does not matter.

  • Power control for short-range installs at high rates (the 0x54ac power byte).

  • Pacing in tx_pace_after must use the real rate (the old 6 Mb/s assumption was wrong for every direct-path frame, which went at 1 Mb/s).

  • One boot in five runs printed the bootloader banner after a flash, then nothing, with its USB port gone until a reset; mesh_smoke records it (bootfail-*.log) and resets. Not reproduced since; root cause open.
