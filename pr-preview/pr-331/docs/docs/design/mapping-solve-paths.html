Mapping solve paths: why a good preview could end in a degenerate map¶

Status: implemented (branch agent/mapping-debug-bundles).

The report: “I had a few failed captures where the successful capture was clearly visible in the mid-capture solver preview, but went degenerate when doing the final solve.” No capture data from those runs survived, because the app didn’t log failed runs. This note compares the two solve paths, names the root cause found on synthetic captures, and describes the fix and the debug-mode capture bundles that make the next failure replayable.

The two paths¶

Both paths run the same wasm build of solver/ (reconstruct_vio in solver/src/pipeline.rs) on the phone. On a Pi host the live map comes from the host’s own solver instead.

Root cause: the cold start’s scale sign¶

solve_vio’s cold start (solver/src/vio.rs) builds the camera path and LED positions up to scale from the rays, then solves a linear least-squares system (inertial_alignment) for the metric scale s, gravity and velocities, and multiplies the visual init by s.

On a gentle capture (the phone barely moves) or a decode-swapped one (a few percent of detections carry the wrong LED id, which the linear ray init isn’t robust to), that scale estimate is noise-dominated. It can come out at or below zero. Applied as-is:

  1. A negative s mirrors the whole init through the gauge camera, so every LED lands behind every camera.

  2. The BA’s behind-camera residual is a constant 50 px with zero gradient, so the optimizer can’t climb out.

  3. reconstruct_vio keeps only LEDs with views in front of the camera, so the map comes back empty (all residuals 50 px). A tiny positive s (cameras a few cm from the fixture) does the same.

The sign is a property of the exact record set. Each interim solve runs on a different prefix and draws a different s. In a sweep of a gentle capture’s prefixes the cold-start scale went 0.31, −0.10, 0.56, −0.33, 0.56, −0.79, −1.06 and the final drew −0.88. The preview showed whichever solves came out positive (an empty interim just leaves the previous overlay up). The final solve re-initializes from scratch on all the data, so it threw away everything the preview had shown and drew its own sign. That explains “preview right, final degenerate”: the final isn’t worse than the interims in general, it just doesn’t share their luck.

Reproductions (synthetic captures from solver/src/scenario.rs; seeds found by sweeping):

  • A gentle orbit (0.5 rad over 40 s, 2 cm bob), seeds 1, 8 and 9: the final cold-start scale is −1.18, −0.29 and −0.03, and the final map is empty. For seeds 8 and 9 the interim solve on the first half of the capture rendered the fixture correctly.

  • A well-excited orbit with 3 % decode swaps, seed 3: scale −0.012, the final map is empty.

These are the regression tests in solver/src/regression_tests.rs.

Other differences examined¶

  • Re-anchor applied after every stage. It multiplies the geometry by s_post after the initial solve and again after each warm-started outlier round. On well-excited synthetic orbits those factors were consistently below 1 (0.93, then 0.86, then 0.96) while the BA pushed back between them, so the final size settles around 0.9 of true. An experiment re-anchoring only on the initial and last stage moved the median size from 0.90 to 0.93, which is within noise. That’s a modest size bias, not a degeneracy, so it’s left as is and noted here.

  • The outlier loop’s score (solved_led_count, then −rms) can’t see scale: reprojection is scale-invariant. It can’t catch a collapse, which is one reason the assessment below exists.

  • Keyframe decimation (120 vs 250), the optimizer budget and the strip prior changed sizes by a few percent in the sweeps, never the verdict.

  • Diffuse stride captures (only one stride class lit per phase, 1–2 s of decode dead time per switch) solve fine as long as the bridge phases co-light anchors (web/src/code/stride.ts). Without bridges the classes are only IMU-linked and shapes go wrong in both paths.

The fix¶

  1. Cold-start scale guard (vio::plausible_init_scale, ported to the Python reference pi/reconstruction/reconstruction/vio.py). If the alignment’s scale puts the cameras outside 5 cm to 30 m of the fixture (this includes s ≤ 0 and NaN), the solver seeds an arm’s-length prior instead (0.75 m), with finite-difference velocities. The BA and the inertial re-anchor then settle the metric scale, and the existing scale-uncertainty warning still fires when the IMU can’t. The stage trace records initScale and initScaleFallback. options.initScaleGuard: false turns the guard off, for replaying a run the old way.

  2. The final solve warm-starts from the best interim. The app keeps every interim solve’s state (diag.state, the un-leveled VIO state) and passes the best one as options.warmStart. reconstruct_vio then runs the initial solve both cold and warm and continues from the better one: not degenerate first, then more solved LEDs, then lower residual (diag::better). The trace records both and which one won (diag.start). LEDs the interim never saw are triangulated under the warm-seeded poses. Seeding them at the origin would leave them stuck behind the gauge camera.

  3. The final is validated against the preview, with a fallback (web/src/solver/degeneracy.ts decideFinal). If the final solve throws, comes out degenerate, keeps fewer than 60 % of the preview’s measured LEDs, or collapses to a line or plane the preview didn’t have, the run keeps the best preview map. The map then carries a warning that names the reasons, and the run outcome is fallback-degenerate or fallback-failed. A size or shape disagreement alone (more than 2× in spread, or an aligned shape error above 25 % of the spread) keeps the final but flags the run, because either side could be the wrong one.

Degeneracy, precisely¶

solver/src/diag.rs (Rust, the solver’s own verdict) and web/src/solver/degeneracy.ts (the same verdict from an OutputMap alone):

Reason

Condition

non-finite

A NaN or ∞ in any LED position, the residual or the trajectory

too-few-leds

Fewer than 4 measured LEDs (nViews > 0), or fewer than 25 % of the LEDs the capture saw in ≥ 2 distinct frames

huge-residual

Global reprojection rms > 20 px. The behind-camera penalty is 50 px, so this means most rays miss.

scale-collapse

Median camera-to-fixture distance < 0.15 m, or LED rms spread < 2 mm

scale-blowup

Median camera-to-fixture distance > 30 m

scale-uncertain (warning only)

Scale relative standard error > 0.5

Rank collapse is only judged relative to the preview: a straight strip is legitimately rank 1. collapsed-to-line means the preview’s 2nd/1st principal ratio was ≥ 0.05 and the final’s is < 0.005. collapsed-to-plane applies the same test to the 3rd principal axis.

Results on the synthetic sweep¶

Seven scenario families (well-excited orbit, gentle orbit, dwell-and-hop diffuse, stride 3 with bridges and dead time, 3 % decode swaps, a planar wall, a long gentle orbit), 4 seeds each. Each run did six interim solves on growing prefixes with the live options, then the final solve. “Good” means ≥ 80 % of the LEDs, size within 30 % of true and shape error under 10 mm, measured against ground truth.

The two runs that are still “bad” after the fix are dwell-and-hop seeds 1 and 2. They place all 60 LEDs with under 1.1 mm shape error, but at 0.66 of true size. That’s the known dwell-heavy scale regime (Bug B, #259), not a degeneracy, and it was the same before. The warm start won in 17 of the 28 runs. The interim column improved too, because the guard also protects the interim cold starts: before the fix, decode-swap captures never rendered in the preview at all.

Capture bundles (debug mode)¶

With Settings ▸ Behavior ▸ Developer ▸ Enable Debug Settings on, every run whose outcome isn’t ok writes a bundle to IndexedDB (ledmapper-capturebundles; the newest 20, up to 150 MB). “Keep successful runs too” logs ok runs as well. Settings ▸ Debugging ▸ Mapping capture bundles lists them with Export (one .splanc-capture.json) and Share (Web Share). The format is described in web/src/solver/captureBundle.ts:

  • The final solve’s exact input

  • The best interim plus the last three interims. Each one is a prefix (recordCount, imuCount) plus its options, so it replays exactly.

  • The raw final output with the solver’s stage trace and assessment

  • The decision and its reasons

  • Capture parameters, the fixture’s firmware version, the solver version and the SHA-256 of the exact wasm build, and app and device info

  • Downsampled camera frames, opt-in

Replay:

  • bazel run //solver:replay_bundle -- <file> runs the wasm build, so the replay is bit-for-bit. It flags a bundle recorded by a different build. --cold drops the warm start, --check exits non-zero unless every solve reproduced, and --record <problem.json> <out> turns a bare problem (an iOS session-*.json log, solver_cli --synth output) into a bundle the way the app would.

  • solver_cli --replay <file> runs natively and prints each stage’s trace (frames, observations, solved LEDs, rms, re-anchor factor, cold-start scale, fallback, degeneracy). Native libm can differ from wasm in the last bit, so an unconverged solve can drift by about 0.1 mm here.

  • solver_cli --synth '<spec>' prints a synthetic capture with its ground truth (solver/src/scenario.rs: fixture, motion, stride/dead time, decode swaps, noise).

Bit-for-bit means identical JSON in, identical doubles out. The solver has no randomness or threads. serde_json’s float parsing is deterministic but best-effort, so the native replay slices each input out of the bundle’s raw text rather than re-serializing parsed values. The wasm replay serializes with JSON.stringify exactly as the worker did.

Still unverified¶

  • The user’s captures. No bundles from the failed runs exist; the app kept nothing from a failed run, only iOS session-*.json logs of final problems, and none came with the report. The root cause above comes from synthetic captures. The next failure with debug mode on is the check.

  • Hardware. None of this has run on a phone yet: the wasm build, the worker’s wasm fingerprint (it needs crypto.subtle, so a secure context), IndexedDB sizes, and the Share flow on iOS.

  • The size bias. The re-anchor applied after every stage leaves sizes about 10 % small on synthetic orbits. That’s separate from the degeneracy, so it’s left for a follow-up.
