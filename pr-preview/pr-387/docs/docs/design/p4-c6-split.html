Splanc on ESP32-P4 + ESP32-C6 (split player)¶

Status: proposed. Nothing here is built yet. Phase A runs on the bare ESP32-P4 dev boards we already have. Phase B waits for the P4 boards with an on-board C6 on SDIO, which are on order.

Owner direction (2026-10-26): “Let’s integrate a c6 and p4 to have the expanded compute headroom while still having wifi and ble.” And: “We can do some initial porting and FX engine validation against the bare P4 boards and then do the wireless bringup when we have the ones with the attached C6.”

The design in one paragraph. The C6 stays what it is today on the network side: our netstack, TLS, wss, BLE provisioning (Improv/GATT), mDNS, the Wi-Fi rejoin policies and the OTA loader, all of it already covered by HITL. It stops rendering. Every player message (effects, uniforms, maps, textures, video, keypoints) goes, still encoded as the client sent it, over a 4-bit SDIO link to the P4. The P4 runs the player core: fx_vm with a PIE SIMD tier, the decks and maps, and 16 WS2812 strips from one PARLIO 16-bit DMA stream. Replies and pushes come back the same way. The protobuf envelope (shared/protocol/proto/ledmapper.proto) is unchanged on the wire, so the app, the agent and the bridge see one player.

Related docs: the P4 accelerator survey is §9 of docs/design/fx-gpu-lowering.md (PR #368, branch agent/fx-gpu-lowering). This doc relies on it and does not repeat it. Also relevant: ota.md, live-control.md, wifi-rejoin-policy.md, ram-budget.md, memory-regions.md, fx-perf-study.md and sensor-core.md.

Fact tags. [V] means checked against a primary source; §8 lists the sources. [V-pin] means read from the ESP-IDF build we pin: the esp32-arduino-libs zip in patches/embedded-esp32-libs-c6-c3.patch, IDF release/v5.5, arduino-esp32 3.3.10. [U] means unverified, to be confirmed at bring-up. [E] means an estimate.

1. Why split¶

What the P4 adds. Two HP RISC-V cores (RV32IMAFC plus Espressif’s PIE SIMD extension). They run at 400 MHz on die revision v3.x and 360 MHz on v1.x [V-pin]. It has a single-precision FPU and 768 KB of on-chip L2MEM. The IDF build we pin carves 256 KB of that out as L2 cache [V-pin], so about 512 KB is left for static data and heap. It also has:

  • 16 or 32 MB of in-package PSRAM, at 200 MHz in our config [V, V-pin];

  • one PARLIO TX unit, up to 16 bits wide [V];

  • the BitScrambler, JPEG and PPA blocks;

  • an Ethernet MAC (RMII);

  • USB: 2.0 HS OTG, FS OTG and USB-Serial-JTAG [V].

It has no internal flash, and no radio [V]. §9 of fx-gpu-lowering.md surveys these blocks for FX use.

Why split, and not a P4 alone. The P4 needs a radio, and the C6 already has a radio stack that works and is tested. Splitting the work gives the render path its own core, isolated in hardware. The render-isolation machinery of #371, the loop priorities and the frame-wait sleeps all become unnecessary on the C6. The P4 keeps rendering through anything the network does, including rejoins, PBKDF2 and a C6 reboot. That makes the owner rule in bootloop.txt (“effects must keep running”) true by construction.

2.2 Where the split falls in the code¶

lm_player_handle (firmware/player_app/ffi.rs) already routes on the envelope arm before it decodes anything: envelope_arm(frame) reads the oneof field number and dispatches. The front-end build replaces the player branches of that match with “forward this frame on the link”. The arm table becomes:

Handled on the C6

Forwarded to the P4

get_tls_info 45; set_wifi_policy 90 / get_wifi_policy 91; ota_* 70–75 targeting the C6; set_device_name 27 (applied, then also forwarded)

everything else: mapping 3–20, effects 21–24 and 36, perf 25–26, textures 28, uploads 29, colour 30–31, JIT 32, hardware config 33–35, pose, time base, crossfade, keypoints 37–44, fps 46, sensors and drivers 48–56, ota_* targeting the P4

hello 1: forwarded; the C6 patches its own fields into the P4’s Welcome on the way back

time_sync_ping 2: forwarded

Rules:

  • No re-encode. The C6 forwards the WS (or GATT) payload byte for byte, and relays the P4’s ServerMessage bytes the same way. The C6 front end needs no player bindings beyond envelope_arm. Nothing is decoded twice, and nothing on the link has to track proto changes.

  • The P4 builds the Welcome. It knows the player state, features, fxb_format and board. The C6 overwrites only the fields it owns (mac, device_name, fw_* for the front end, the ota block) and adds a new coprocessor block: link state, link protocol version, the P4’s firmware version, and the reason when the P4 is down. A splice like Player::encode_reply does this without a full decode. While the P4 is down, the C6 sends a minimal Welcome itself, with coprocessor.state = DOWN, and answers forwarded arms with error{code: player_unavailable}.

  • Pushes. perf_report, fps_state, playback_state and the like are unsolicited ServerMessages from the P4. The C6 sends them to the session that owns the matching request, or to the current session. The best-effort drop rule from #353 still holds: a live reply is dropped when the TCP send window can’t take it, and RX is never blocked.

2.3 State and persistence¶

  • On the P4 (its own SPI flash and littlefs): maps, topology, effects and the effect library, keypoints, the colour LUT, the LED and hardware config, the sensor calibration. The P4 boots into its stored effect before the link is up.

  • On the C6 (NVS, as today): Wi-Fi credentials and policy, the TLS identity, the device name (it is authoritative because BLE and mDNS advertise it; the C6 pushes changes to the P4), and the OTA state.

  • Migration: none. P4 boards are new devices. They are provisioned like a C6, over BLE Improv to the C6.

2.4 Rejected alternative: ESP-Hosted / esp_wifi_remote¶

Espressif’s stock answer is ESP-Hosted-MCU. The C6 runs Espressif’s coprocessor firmware as a Wi-Fi/BT NIC, and the P4 runs esp_wifi_remote, lwIP, mbedTLS and the BT host. The IDF build we pin ships it ready-made: the P4 libs link esp_hosted and esp_wifi_remote with CONFIG_ESP_HOSTED_ENABLED, and the zip carries hosted/esp32c6-v2.12.8.bin [V-pin]. Arduino’s WiFi on a P4 already works this way.

We don’t build on it:

  • It discards the netstack work and its HITL coverage. That work is the heapless netstack, the GTK/RSN fixes for commercial APs, the stale-IP listener fix, the airtime TX pacing, the rejoin policies, the TLS region, the per-device cert, the BLE/wss coexistence fixes and the single-task wss reliability work. Every one was a field bug that HITL now gates. On ESP-Hosted, Wi-Fi, TLS, wss, BLE Improv and the GATT transport would be new code paths on the P4, with lwIP and Espressif’s stack, and each needs new HITL coverage before it is as good as what we have.

  • It moves the network back onto the render chip. lwIP, TLS and the BT host would share the P4’s cores with the renderer. That brings back the contention that #371 and #366 spent weeks fencing off. Dual cores make it easier, but it is the same class of problem.

  • The C6 would be a black box on the HITL rig: a prebuilt image we can’t instrument with the netstack’s status lines or JTAG heap watchers.

Where it still helps:

  • Bring-up aid. When the C6 boards arrive, run the stock hosted firmware first. It proves the board’s SDIO wiring, pull-ups, the reset line and the bus clock with a known-good stack, before any of our link code is suspect. The throughput numbers in §2.5 come from exactly this setup.

  • Fallback. If the SDIO slave cannot coexist with the netstack’s DMA and RAM budget on the C6 (risk R3), ESP-Hosted is the plan B for the radio side. The player split on the P4 would not change.

3. Phase A: bare P4 boards (no radio)¶

Goal: prove the toolchain, the player core, the FX engine and LED output on the P4, driven by the existing protocol and tests, before any wireless work.

Note that the v3 set starts at v3.1, not v3.0. Espressif’s documentation agrees:

  • No shared image. “v1.x and v3.x chips cannot share the same firmware image” [V].

  • IDF support. v3.x needs ESP-IDF v5.5.3+ or v6.0+ [V]. Our pinned SDK is release/v5.5, and the from-source pieces are v5.5.4.

  • Kconfig. CONFIG_ESP32P4_SELECTS_REV_LESS_V3 defaults to n. CONFIG_ESP32P4_REV_MIN defaults to ESP32P4_REV_MIN_301, and 360 MHz is only selectable with LESS_V3 [V].

  • Mismatch. A wrong-revision image is refused by the bootloader (“Image requires chip rev >= v3.0, but chip is v1.0”) [V].

The die revision also decides what the BitScrambler can do (§3.5). Check every board before anything else, and record the result in the HITL catalog notes. Use whichever of these is easiest:

  • esptool. Espressif’s docs say to use esptool chip-id (esptool v5 spelling; v4 is esptool.py --chip esp32p4 chip_id) [V]. The exact output line differs between esptool versions. Look for the revision vX.Y [U: the exact P4 wording].

  • The second-stage bootloader log. Any IDF app prints boot: chip revision: v1.3 on the console at reset [V].

  • The chip marking. The manufacturing-code letter on the package gives the revision: A = v0.0, C = v1.0, E = v1.3, F = v3.0, G = v3.1 [V]. The “P4X” in a board name (for example ESP32-P4X-Function-EV-Board) means a v3.1+ chip [V].

  • At runtime. efuse_hal_chip_revision() returns major × 100 + minor. esp32p4_hello prints it (§3.1).

Expect a mix of revisions:

  • The Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6 uses an ESP32-P4NRW32, a non-X, v1.x-era part [V, vendor wiki].

  • The ESP32-P4-Function-EV-Board v1.5.2 is v1.x. The P4X version is v3.1+ [V].

On a v1.x board, Phase A builds against esp32p4_es. That still covers the whole player, but not the BitScrambler expansion path (§3.5).

3.1 Toolchain and target bring-up (Bazel / Nix)¶

The C3 port (#212, #216) is the template. It needed four things, and the P4 needs the same four:

  1. SDK libs. patches/embedded-esp32-libs-c6-c3.patch unzips only esp32c6/* and esp32c3/* from the libs zip. That restriction exists because the full zip unpacks to about 10 GB. Add esp32p4/* (or esp32p4_es/*, chosen per §3.0) and stage it at sdk/esp32p4. Measure the extra disk use first: each variant has about 4,200 entries, a size similar to the C6’s.

  2. Platform and C toolchain. In the style of patches/embedded-esp32c3-build-target.patch (949 lines): an @embedded//platforms:is_esp32p4 constraint, a cc_toolchain with IDF v5.5.3’s -march=rv32imafc_zicsr_zifencei_xesploop_xespv -mabi=ilp32f [V], a libs/board:esp32p4 and bootloader, and the esp32p4 section of the generated arduino_esp32 scaffold. The ABI changes: ilp32f passes floats in FPU registers, so every object in the image must be built ilp32f: our C/C++, Rust, mbedTLS and the prebuilt libs. The prebuilt P4 libs already are. libesp_driver_bitscrambler.a from the pinned zip reads single-float ABI, with Tag_RISCV_arch rv32i…_f2p2_c2p0…_xesploop1p0_xespv2p2 [V-pin, readelf].

  3. Rust triple. Add riscv32imafc-unknown-none-elf to rust.toolchain(extra_target_triples) in MODULE.bazel. It is a tier-2 target with llvm-abiname = ilp32f, and rust-std for our pinned 1.85.0 is published [V]. We use the bare target with IDF’s C drivers, not esp-hal. That matters: esp-hal supports only P4 v3.0+, and marks PARLIO and the BitScrambler as unsupported on the P4 [V]. The netstack is not linked on the P4, so its C6 register map (lmac.rs, regs.rs, ccmp.rs) doesn’t matter here.

  4. Per-chip constants. led_caps.bzl gets a P4 branch (§3.5: 16 channels × 1024), and the P4 build gets a board_caps textproto of its own.

Memory map differences that bite (all [V-pin] or from the TRM [U]):

  • The C6 linker work doesn’t carry over. It is C6-specific: the sections.ld override, IRAM pinning of the shade entry and the PMP TOR exec window for the JIT (#381). Start the P4 on the stock linker script.

  • L2MEM vs PSRAM placement is the main tuning knob. Hot per-LED caches, the VM stack and the PARLIO frame go in L2MEM. Maps, textures, the effect library and video frames go in PSRAM. Allocate with MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL / MALLOC_CAP_SPIRAM explicitly, and never through a default malloc that might land a DMA buffer in PSRAM without the alignment rules.

  • Caches and DMA. The P4 has L1 and L2 caches in front of PSRAM and flash. A DMA buffer the CPU wrote has to be written back (esp_cache_msync) before PARLIO or SDIO reads it, and it must be cache-line aligned. The pinned config uses 128 B L2 lines [V-pin]. This is new: on the C6, DMA from internal SRAM needed no cache maintenance.

  • The JIT. fx_jit emits RV32IM, which the P4 executes. The exec window, the PMP and the I-cache sync after writing code all differ from the C6. Phase A runs with set_jit off. Port the JIT only if the scalar VM is still the bottleneck after the PIE tier.

  • The console. The pinned P4 config uses UART0 as the primary console and USB-Serial-JTAG as the secondary [V-pin]. The C3 lesson was to fix the console before anything else: an app that takes over USB-Serial-JTAG drops the host CDC.

The first image is //firmware/p4:esp32p4_hello, not the player. It prints the chip revision, the CPU frequency, the L2MEM and PSRAM heap sizes and the flash size. It then runs an fx_vm known-answer digest (§3.4) and blinks two PARLIO lanes. It validates the toolchain end to end before the player’s dependency graph is involved.

3.2 Player core on the P4¶

The player is player_app_netstack with its transports swapped. The new //firmware/player_app:esp32p4_player target links:

  • Kept: the Rust session core (ledmapper_player, fx_vm, arena, keypoint, osc, store, fps), ffi.rs, the render task and main.cpp’s render loop, under LM_CHIP_ESP32P4.

  • Dropped: the netstack, BLE, Improv, mbedTLS, wifi_ffi, tls_region, the OTA loader hooks and ws2812_rmt.cpp.

  • New: the link endpoint (the Phase A transport of §3.3, the SDIO host in Phase B) and ws2812_parlio16.cpp (§3.5).

Use both cores. update() runs once per frame. The per-LED shade() loop splits into two LED ranges, one per core, with a barrier before the encode. Shading is meant to be per-LED independent: the per-LED caches from #350 are indexed by LED. Any VM state that shade() writes (buffers, memo slots, counters) must be per LED or per core. Audit this before enabling the split. Gate: the 2-core frame digest equals the 1-core digest for every corpus effect (§3.4).

Sizing. 16 × 1024 = 16,384 LEDs. At about 31 B per LED (ram-budget.md), the per-LED buffers take about 500 KB. That is too much for L2MEM next to the PARLIO frame, so:

  • FX_LED_TOPO and FX_LED_POS go in PSRAM, read sequentially, which suits the cache.

  • leds and the encode buffers stay in L2MEM.

Measure before tuning this. board.max_leds on the P4 comes from the same headroom-at-boot rule as on the C6.

3.3 Temporary control path (no C6)¶

Phase A needs the existing protocol and the existing HITL harnesses (fx_bench, led_timing, led_capture, player_probe) to drive a radio-less P4. The proposal is to run the Phase B link protocol (§4.1) over USB from day one:

  • On the P4. A link endpoint over USB-Serial-JTAG CDC, or over UART0 if the console fight from the C3 recurs. It carries the §4.1 framing (COBS plus CRC on a byte stream).

  • On the host. tools/p4_link_bridge.py serves ws://127.0.0.1:<port>/ws and, optionally, wss:// with a throwaway cert. For each WS message it emits one link PLAYER frame, and it relays the replies and pushes. In other words, it plays the C6’s role.

  • What this tests. The harnesses already accept a URL: player_probe takes ws:// or wss:// with --insecure. They connect to the bridge and can’t tell the difference.

  • Why this path. In Phase A it exercises the P4 half of the link (framing, routing, the Welcome splice, pushes, flow control) with the existing tests. Phase B then only swaps the byte transport.

Ethernet alternative. It applies to boards with an RMII PHY: both Function EV Boards have one (IP101GR on the P4X), and so does the Waveshare P4-ETH [V]. It needs lwIP and a WS server on the P4. That is a second network stack we don’t otherwise want, so treat it as optional. It earns its place later as a wired Art-Net/sACN input on installations, not as the bring-up path.

Throughput check. USB-Serial-JTAG is a full-speed (12 Mbit/s) endpoint. Expect well under 1 MB/s [U]. That is plenty for maps, effects and control. If Phase A needs video-sized transfers, move the link to a TinyUSB CDC on the HS OTG port (480 Mbit/s), which all the boards above expose [V]. The framing doesn’t change.

3.4 FX engine validation¶

The C6 goldens in web/tests/testdata/device-bench-esp32c6{,-jit}.json are cycle goldens: a device-measurement bundle plus margins. They are not pixel goldens. Correctness therefore has to be anchored on the host, where fx_vm already runs in rust_test and where fx_perf_bench’s host_test cross-checks native against the VM per LED. The plan:

  1. A frame digest (new, host and device). For each corpus program — the fx_perf_bench study, agent and valid groups, plus the fx_bench calibration set — run N frames at a fixed time step, map and uniforms. Hash every LED’s Rgb8 (FNV-1a or CRC32) per frame. The host rust_test commits the digests as a golden (testdata/fx_digest.json). Each device image recomputes them and logs fxdg <name> <frame> <hex> lines.

  2. Scalar first, bit-exact. The P4 build of fx_perf_bench (an esp32p4_bench image; the bench is already radio-less) must match the host digests exactly. The same digest generator then runs on the C6 bench image too, which closes the loop: host = C6 = P4. Why bit-exactness is expected even though the P4 has an FPU and the C6 uses soft-float:

    • IEEE add, sub, mul, div and sqrt are correctly rounded both ways.

    • Rust never contracts a*b+c into an FMA unless asked.

    • as conversions saturate by definition.

    • The VM’s transcendentals are our own LUTs and polynomials, not libm.

    Where it can still differ:

    • NaN payloads. RISC-V F returns the canonical NaN; compiler-rt soft-float can propagate a payload. This is visible only if a program reinterprets float bits (hash of a float slot, F2I of NaN is defined, bit-casts).

    • Subnormal handling in the P4 FPU [U: the spec requires IEEE subnormals; confirm on silicon].

    Any mismatch is a bug report, not a tolerance: the digest names the program and frame. Run one more check with -C target-feature=-f (soft-float on the P4). It splits “FPU numerics” from “port bug”.

  3. Performance goldens. Run fx_bench against the P4 over the Phase A bridge. Commit device-bench-esp32p4.json as the P4’s own golden: same programs, LED counts and margins policy. Report the ratio against the C6 golden per program, with 1 core and 2 cores.

    • Expected [E]: 2.5× from the clock alone. Add about 2× from the second core on the shade loop. Float-heavy effects gain much more from the FPU: soft-float ops cost 50–250 cycles on the C6.

    • The budget to beat: 2 × 400 MHz is 800 Mcycles/s. At 16,384 LEDs × 30 fps that is about 1,600 cycles per LED-eval. The C6’s fixed per-LED VM overhead alone is 1,790 cycles. The scalar VM will not reach 16 × 1024 at 30 fps on typical effects. That is what the next step is for.

  4. PIE tier for the fixed-point kernels. This is the survey’s item 1 and is not repeated here. What validation needs:

    • Work order.

      1. The fixed output pipeline first: colour correction, brightness, the crossfade blend, RetRgb8 quantisation, and the bit-plane transpose for PARLIO (§3.5). These kernels are small and fixed, are integer only, and the LA can check them end to end.

      2. Then a batched VM path: SoA over 8 × i16 or 4 × i32 LEDs, for the fixed-point and hash opcodes (MulFix, the fixed8 / fixed16 ops, Hash1 / Hash3).

    • Exactness. These are class 1 (“exact”) in the fx-gpu-lowering.md §3 numerics policy, so the PIE path must reproduce the scalar digests bit for bit. No tolerance class applies on the P4. Effects that use an op the PIE tier doesn’t cover stay scalar per program, never per LED.

    • How to write PIE. LLVM, and so Rust, doesn’t know the PIE instructions. The kernels are C with inline asm, or .S files, built with the IDF GCC, or .insn encodings. They link over FFI like the other C pieces.

      • The -march spelling. IDF v5.3 spelled the extension xesppie; v5.5.3 splits it into xesploop_xespv [V].

      • No intrinsics. No C intrinsics are documented [V: Espressif’s PIE introduction uses asm]. esp-dsp is in the pinned libs [V-pin] and is the reference for idioms.

      • The toolchain is ready. The nix toolchain we already use for the C6 assembles PIE (R1).

    • Gates. Digest equality against scalar, and an fx_bench speedup per program with no regressions over scalar.

3.5 WS2812 output: PARLIO 16-bit + BitScrambler¶

The wire format is the C6’s (#261), widened to 16 lanes. The P4 has one PARLIO TX unit, up to 16 bits wide [V], so all 16 strips share one DMA stream and one clock. Expect to write the driver ourselves, as on the C6:

  • Espressif’s led_strip component has RMT and SPI backends, not PARLIO [V].

  • The third-party ultraled component drives WS2812 over PARLIO, but needs P4 rev ≥ 3.0 and IDF 6.0+ [V]. It is reference code, not a dependency.

  • IDF’s BitScrambler examples are a loopback demo and a HUB75 panel, not WS2812 [V].

The format:

  • Samples. 3 samples per WS2812 bit at 2.5 MHz (400 ns per sample: 1 = 110, 0 = 100), with whole-frame GDMA.

  • Frame size. One 16-bit PARLIO word carries one sample for all 16 strips: 24 bits × 3 samples × 2 B = 144 B per LED index, so 1024 LEDs per strip is 147,456 B per frame.

  • Wire time. It is per strip, not per total: 1024 LEDs × 28.8 µs = 29.5 ms, so about 33 fps at 1024 per strip whatever the strip count. 512 per strip allows about 66 fps, and 256 per strip about 130 fps.

  • Padding. Keep the C6 rule from #261: pad every transfer with low samples to 4 B plus one word, or the tail of unaligned frames corrupts.

Two encoders, built in this order:

  1. The CPU encoder (works on every die revision). The CPU (PIE in §3.4) transposes 16 strips’ bytes into 16-bit bit-planes and writes the 3× expanded buffer directly. That is 147 KB in L2MEM, double-buffered if the encode overlaps the wire, or single-buffered with the encode in the latch gap. This is the baseline and the fallback.

  2. The BitScrambler encoder (die v3.x). The CPU writes only the bit-planes: 48 B per LED index, 48 KB at 1024. The BitScrambler, bound to the PARLIO TX DMA, expands each 16-bit plane word into the three samples (0xFFFF, the word, 0x0000). That cuts the buffer to a third and takes the expansion off the CPU. PARLIO TX binds a program with parlio_tx_unit_decorate_bitscrambler() [V]. Two caveats:

    • Die revision. Before rev 3.0, a BitScrambler-decorated PARLIO stream “must have the same length as the original bitstream” [V]. So the 1-in, 3-out expansion needs v3.x.

    • Program size. Programs are limited to 8 instructions, plus a 2 KB LUT [V]. Gate this first. Write the .bsasm expander, check that it fits and assembles, and run it in the driver’s loopback mode (IDF’s BitScrambler loopback example). Compare memory-to-memory against the CPU encoder’s buffer before attaching PARLIO. If it doesn’t fit in 8, look at the LUT path, then try two passes. If neither works, the CPU encoder ships.

    • Clock. The PARLIO output clock limit isn’t stated in the docs; the source clock is 160 MHz [U]. Our 2.5 MHz sample clock is far below any plausible limit.

Validation on the logic analyzer.

  • Sample rate. The rig’s FX2 samples at up to 24 MHz. A 400 ns sample is 9.6 analyzer samples, which is enough for WS2812 (and not for SPI, which this doesn’t need).

  • Tools. Use the existing //pi/hitl/harness:led_timing (per-frame start, duration, LED count, gaps, runt and split flags) and led_capture / --pixels with ledTimingRamp.fxb.

  • Which lanes. The analyzer can’t watch 16 lanes at 24 MHz. It watches the lanes its channel map exposes (two today: --split). Rotate lanes across runs: 0 + 1, then 7 + 8, then 14 + 15. The lane-swizzle bugs live at the edges.

  • Gate. Zero bad frames over 30 s per mode, as #261 achieved, with the ramp decoding to the right pixel on each watched lane.

Pins. Any 16 free GPIOs through the GPIO matrix. Avoid strapping pins, USB, the SDIO-to-C6 group on Phase B boards (§4.4) and flash/PSRAM pins. Each board gets a board_caps textproto with its safe pins. Level shifting to 5 V (for example 2 × 74HCT245) is a board concern (§5.3). Dev boards drive 3.3 V, which most strips accept on short leads.

  • esp32p4_hello boots, reports the revision, CPU, L2MEM and PSRAM figures, and passes its digest.

  • esp32p4_player, driven through p4_link_bridge, passes player_probe. It accepts a map, an effect, uniforms and a texture, and it reconnects.

  • The scalar digests equal host = C6 = P4 for the whole corpus, with 1 and 2 cores.

  • device-bench-esp32p4.json is committed, with the P4/C6 ratio table.

4.1 The SDIO link protocol¶

Layers:

  1. Bus. On the C6, IDF sdio_slave in packet mode (libesp_driver_sdio.a, in the pinned C6 libs [V-pin]). On the P4, sdmmc host plus ESSL (libespressif__esp_serial_slave_link.a [V-pin]). ESSL gives us the slave’s receive-buffer count, packet reads and the slave-to-host interrupt on DAT1, and it speaks SPI-HD for the fallback.

  2. Link frames (ours). They are the same over USB in Phase A, SDIO in Phase B and SPI as the fallback. On byte streams (USB, UART) frames are COBS-delimited. On SDIO and SPI, one frame is one packet.

SDIO has bus CRCs, so the link CRC is there for the SPI and UART transports and for end-to-end buffer bugs. It costs about 1 µs per KB on either core [E].

Max frame and fragmentation.

  • Frame size. The link MTU is 4,096 B of payload, which matches the existing chunk sizes (ota_chunk ≤ 4096, the upload_chunk windows). The C6 SDIO receive buffers are sized to one frame.

  • Forwarding. A WS message larger than the MTU is forwarded as MORE-flagged fragments as it is decrypted. The C6 never reassembles a player message (cut-through). The P4 reassembles into PSRAM. This needs the netstack’s WS receive path to release a frame in pieces; today it buffers one whole frame. It is a Phase B follow-up, not a day-one requirement.

  • What this unlocks. Today the largest message a C6 can accept is set by its WS buffer (about 6 KB), which is why HITL_CHUNK_BYTES=1024 and the client-side chunking exist. With cut-through, the P4’s memory sets the ceiling instead. A 2048-LED map, a big .fxb or a 256 × 256 texture can be one message. Clients keep chunking until a feature flag (big_frames) says the device takes more.

Large transfers (maps, textures, video).

  • Maps, topology and effects keep the existing upload_chunk protocol, which is stop-and-wait per window with a chunk_ack. The acks now come from the P4. The bulk lane carries the windows, so they can’t delay the live lane.

  • Textures and video. set_texture stays fire-and-forget with texture_ack (latest-wins, bounded in flight). The C6 forwards each frame on the bulk lane. A single-fragment texture still queued on the C6 is replaced when a newer one for the same tex_index arrives, the same rule as the live mailbox, so a slow link drops frames rather than queuing them. Multi-fragment frames go through cut-through. The client’s texture_ack window bounds them.

  • Decoding on the P4. JPEG (hardware) and PPA resize are the survey’s path for bigger video sources. They need a new format value and don’t change the link.

Flow control and lanes.

  • C6 to P4. The slave’s receive buffers are the credits. ESSL reads the count before every write, so the P4 host never overruns the C6. The C6 keeps at least one buffer reserved for the live lane, so a texture burst can’t block a knob move.

  • P4 to C6. The C6 queues send buffers (send_queue_size) and raises the host interrupt. The P4 drains live before bulk. Pushes and replies to a session whose TCP window is full are dropped by the C6 under #353’s rule. They are never back-pressured onto the link.

  • Live lane order. Live-lane frames are always sent before queued bulk frames, in both directions. Within a lane, order is preserved, which seq checks.

  • Heartbeat. A LINK heartbeat every 100 ms in each direction. Three missed heartbeats mean the link is down (§4.5).

LINK control messages:

  • HELLO: both directions at link-up. It carries the protocol version (major and minor), the MTU, the buffer counts, the build ids, and the P4’s player descriptor (features, board, fxb_format). That way the C6 can answer hello from cache for a few hundred ms during a P4 restart.

  • HEARTBEAT.

  • RESET (resync).

  • NAME (device name push, C6 to P4).

  • STATS (counts and errors for the status line).

Versioning. The major version must match. The minor is negotiated down to the lower one. A release pairs both images, but either chip may run N while the other runs N−1 during an update (§5.1).

4.5 Boot sequencing and failure modes¶

Boot order:

  1. The P4 boots and starts rendering the stored effect from its own flash at once, with the link down.

  2. The P4 resets the C6. It asserts the C6 reset line (P4 GPIO54, active high in the pinned hosted config [V-pin]) for ≥ 10 ms, then releases it. The hosted config waits 1,500 ms after a reset (CONFIG_ESP_HOSTED_SDIO_RESET_DELAY_MS) [V-pin], so start there and trim it down.

  3. The C6 boots. Its app, or its loader in recovery, initialises the SDIO slave before Wi-Fi and BLE, so the link comes up in well under a second of C6 boot [E].

  4. The P4 enumerates the card (CMD5/CMD3/CMD7, bus width, clock) and exchanges LINK HELLO. The C6 caches the player descriptor.

  5. The C6 starts Wi-Fi and BLE under its rejoin policy, as today. Clients connect and see one player.

5. Phase C: OTA, HITL, product¶

5.1 OTA for both chips¶

Recommendation: each chip updates itself, and the C6 relays the P4’s image. The C6 never writes the P4’s flash, and the P4 never writes the C6’s application flash.

  • C6: unchanged. The split loader and app, the manifest, the keyring (ids 0 release, 1 backup, 2 next), key epochs and the security floor all work as in ota.md. A smaller app (§4.3) only adds headroom.

  • P4: plain A/B. The P4 boards carry far more flash than 4 MB: 16 MB on the Function EV Board, 32 MB on the Waveshare P4-WIFI6 [V]. Two app slots, otadata, littlefs and NVS fit with room to spare. The P4 needs no split loader, because the C6 already plays the role the loader plays on a C6: it stays reachable even when the P4 image is bad. Rollback is the IDF bootloader’s: CONFIG_BOOTLOADER_APP_ROLLBACK_ENABLE with esp_ota_mark_app_valid_cancel_rollback() called after the health check. The health check: the link is up and HELLO completes, frames render, and the C6 has relayed one client hello (the C6 app’s “lease” rule adapted).

  • Same manifest, same keyring. The P4 app verifies the 108-byte manifest (ECDSA P-256 over bytes 0..44) against a compiled-in copy of the same ring, with the same kRing generator. It does this before it writes the slot, and it reads the slot back through SHA-256 before it switches otadata. It also checks the image header’s chip id: ESP_CHIP_ID_ESP32P4 = 0x0012, against the C6’s 0x000D [V-pin, esp_app_format.h]. The target is bound inside the signed bytes: use the manifest’s spare flags bits 24–27 as target (0 = C6 app, 1 = P4 app). A C6 image can then never be installed on a P4, or the reverse, even when both are signed with the same key. The C6 loader learns to refuse target ≠ 0 (a one-line check). Until it does, its existing chip-id check (0x000D) already refuses a P4 image. Each chip keeps its own sfloor (security floor) and its own key epoch.

  • Wire. OtaBegin gains target (new field). With target = P4, the C6 app does not reboot into its loader. It forwards ota_* 70–75 on the bulk lane, and the P4 answers ota_status as the loader does today. The flow is otherwise the same: RECEIVING + offset, 4 KB stop-and-wait chunks, resume at a persisted offset, INSTALLED. Throughput is the C6’s wss rate.

  • Release bundle. p4c6-<ver>.tar carries esp32c6-frontend-<ver>.ota, esp32p4-player-<ver>.ota, key-epoch.bin, and a manifest naming the link protocol version each image speaks.

  • Update order. Update the P4 first, then the C6. The C6 stays up to relay the P4’s image, and link-version N/N−1 compatibility (§4.1) covers the gap. The agent’s update_firmware and the web Flash screen sequence the two and show them as one update with two steps.

  • Recovery, by severity:

    1. A bad P4 image rolls back on its own.

    2. A bad C6 app falls back to the C6 loader. The P4 keeps rendering.

    3. A broken C6 loader, or a C6 that can’t boot at all, can be reflashed by the P4 through the C6 ROM download mode: the P4 drives EN, holds GPIO9 low and talks to UART0 with esp-serial-flasher. This needs GPIO9 and the C6’s UART0 wired to the P4 (§4.4). If a board doesn’t wire them, that board’s C6 loader is USB/header-only, exactly as on a C6 today. This path is also how the factory programs the C6 on our own board (§5.3).

  • Why not “the C6 flashes the P4”? The P4 is the SDIO host, and its boot straps and UART are not wired to the C6 on these boards [U]. More to the point, the P4 doesn’t need it: A/B plus rollback is safer than any external flasher, and the image still travels through the C6.

7. First steps with the bare P4 boards¶

The agent work for steps 3–6 can start before the boards are on the desk. Each step’s success criterion is what to look for.

  1. Identify each board. Plug it into USB and run esptool chip-id (esptool v5), or esptool.py --chip esp32p4 chip_id (v4). Also read the letter in the chip marking (§3.0). Write down the board model, the revision line (§3.0), the flash size, and whether it has PSRAM and an Ethernet jack. Success: every board is labelled with v1.x or v3.x. That decides esp32p4_es vs esp32p4 libs, and whether the BitScrambler gate can run on it.

  2. Prove the board with stock IDF, before our build is involved. With ESP-IDF v5.5:

    • idf.py set-target esp32p4;

    • on a v1.x board, idf.py menuconfig → Chip revision → select “support < v3.0”;

    • build, flash and monitor get-started/hello_world, then one parlio TX example and the bitscrambler loopback example [U: the example names in v5.5].

    Success: the boot log shows the revision and the CPU frequency, and the PARLIO example shows a waveform on a scope or the LA.

  3. //firmware/p4:esp32p4_hello (our Bazel build, §3.1). Flash it with the flash bundle the same way as a C6 (bazel run …:esp32p4_hello_flashbundle or the flash server). Success: it prints the revision, the CPU, the L2MEM and PSRAM heap totals and fxdg kat ok, and two PARLIO lanes toggle.

  4. The fx_perf_bench P4 image (esp32p4_bench). Success: every fxdg line matches the host golden. Then the cycle table against the C6, with 1 and 2 cores.

  5. The PARLIO CPU encoder on two strips plus the LA (led_timing --split). Success: zero bad frames over 30 s and a correct ramp on both lanes. Then move the strips to other lanes.

  6. esp32p4_player + tools/p4_link_bridge.py. Run player_probe ws://127.0.0.1:<port>/ws. Success: map, effect, uniforms and texture all work, and fx_bench runs end to end through the bridge.

Everything after that (the BitScrambler gate, the PIE kernels, the 16-lane LA rotation) follows §3.4 and §3.5. Phase B starts when the C6 boards arrive, with step 2’s equivalent: stock ESP-Hosted iperf on each board.
