Splanc AI credits: pay for Claude in the app¶

Status: design + sandbox-only scaffold (this PR). Nothing is deployed, no store product exists, no merchant account is linked, and every route answers 503 until the owner turns it on.

Today the in-app AI (the effect editor’s chat, Acid Mode) needs the user’s own Anthropic API key. Most people don’t have one. With Splanc AI credits a user buys a credit pack, and the app uses Claude through our server. We pay Anthropic and charge the user Anthropic’s price plus a small markup. Bringing your own key keeps working exactly as before.

Decisions¶

Owner decisions (2026-11-18)¶

  1. Channels. iOS uses Apple In-App Purchase (consumable packs). Android and the web use a web checkout (Stripe, with Apple Pay / Google Pay buttons). No Google Play Billing and no Play-distributed Android app for now. The iOS app never shows or links to the web purchase.

  2. Markup per channel, in the server-side config: higher on In-App Purchase (to cover Apple’s commission), lower on the web.

  3. Architecture: a server-side AI proxy on the relay, a reserve-then-settle ledger, purchase validation, accounts. Our Anthropic key (and any managed credential) never reaches a client. The app holds only a session token for its credit account.

  4. BYO key is unchanged. “Cloud ▸ Anthropic” with your own key still calls Anthropic straight from the browser, with the same request as before (a test pins the bytes).

Store rules (checked 2026-11-18)¶

  • Apple, App Store Review Guidelines (updated 2026-10-16) (guidelines).

    • 3.1.1: digital features and “in-game currencies” sold in the app must use In-App Purchase. “Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire.”

    • In the US storefront, apps may also show buttons and links to an external purchase (3.1.1(a), 3.1.3, after Epic v. Apple). Apple currently charges no commission on those. The Supreme Court is hearing Apple’s appeal (No. 25-1311), so a fee could come back.

    • We don’t link out (owner decision 1). That is the rule everywhere outside the US anyway, so it is the simplest compliant design.

    • 5.1.1(v): an app that creates accounts must offer account deletion in the app.

    • 4.8: offering Google or other social login needs an equivalent private login option (Sign in with Apple qualifies).

    • 5.1.2(i): disclose, and get explicit permission, when personal data is sent to a third-party AI.

    • 1.2: apps that create AI content need a content filter, a way to report content, and published contact details. The existing “Report” button on every AI reply (agent-feedback.md) covers reporting.

  • Apple fees. Small Business Program: 15 % on proceeds up to US$1 M a year (program), 30 % otherwise. Apple sells “as agent” and handles sales tax / VAT in most regions (Schedule 2), so we don’t file tax for In-App Purchase sales. Consumables don’t restore through StoreKit, so the balance must live on our server, tied to an account.

  • Google Play (for the record: not used now). Apps on Play must use Play Billing for digital goods (payments policy). A TWA reaches it through the Digital Goods API. In the US after Epic v. Google, alternative billing and links are allowed, with fees of about 10–20 % (answer 15582165). Fees were restructured on 2026-06-30 (a service fee plus a 5 % billing fee). Because Android users run the PWA in Chrome, not a Play app, no store rule applies to them. “Google Pay” on the web is just a wallet in Stripe Checkout, not Play Billing.

  • Web. No store rules. Stripe: 2.9 % + 30 ¢ per US card or wallet payment (pricing). Stripe is not the merchant of record, so sales tax / VAT is ours. Stripe Tax computes it for 0.5 %. Alternatives that are the merchant of record: Stripe Managed Payments (+3.5 %) or Paddle (5 % + 50 ¢). Lemon Squeezy is closing (no new sign-ups), so don’t use it.

Anthropic terms (checked 2026-11-18)¶

  • Allowed. The Commercial Terms let a customer use Claude “to power products and services Customer makes available to its own customers and end users” (A.1). Nothing regulates what we charge, so a markup is fine (commercial terms).

  • Not allowed. Reselling the Services as such (“resell the Services except as expressly approved”, D.4), building a competing product, and “Resell, proxy, or otherwise provide access to Claude through unauthorized means” (Usage Policy).

    • So the proxy is not a general Claude endpoint. It accepts only the editor’s request shape: custom tools, an allowlisted set of fields and models, a capped max_tokens. It is for Splanc’s own features.

    • Keep the feature about lighting.

  • Our obligations.

    • Our users must follow the Usage Policy, and we are “responsible for all activity under [our] account” (D.2, D.5). Anthropic may suspend us for a user’s misuse (I.3).

    • So we need per-account limits and caps, a way to suspend accounts, and an opaque per-user id in metadata.user_id for Anthropic’s abuse tooling.

    • Tell users that AI output may be inaccurate (D.3).

    • Disclose that users are talking to an AI (Usage Policy, consumer chatbots). The editor already says so.

    • Minors: products serving under-18s have extra requirements (support article). Decided: buying credits is 18+. The app asks once and the account records it (POST /billing/age); Buy shows only for a confirmed adult, and the web checkout will refuse without it.

  • Data. Anthropic doesn’t train on API content (B). It deletes inputs and outputs within 30 days, or keeps them up to 2 years if flagged for a policy violation (retention). Our privacy notice names Anthropic as a sub-processor.

  • Branding. Don’t imply Anthropic endorses us (trademark guidelines). Name models exactly as sent (“Claude Sonnet 5.5”), say “Powered by Claude” at most, and keep our own brand.

  • Limits. Rate limits and monthly spend limits are organization-wide (rate limits): all users share one pool. At the spend limit the API returns 429 enforced_spend_limit_reached. The proxy maps that to “busy” and releases the reservation. Before launch we need a usage tier with headroom: Start is capped at US$500 a month, Build at US$1,000.

Billing model¶

Prepaid credits, metered per request, priced from the API’s own usage report.

  • Pack price → credits (the markup): credits = floor(price_usd / (1 + channel.markup) × credits_per_usd), or a pack’s explicit credits.

  • Request → charge (no markup by default): charge = max(min_request_credits, list_usd × (1 + usage_markup) × credits_per_usd).

  • list_usd is a sum over the request’s usage fields. Each is multiplied by that model’s list rate for that token class (pricing):

    • input_tokens;

    • output_tokens (thinking tokens bill as output);

    • cache_creation_input_tokens, split into 5-minute and 1-hour writes by usage.cache_creation;

    • cache_read_input_tokens.

  • Claude Haiku 5.5 prices a request whose prompt (input + cache reads + cache writes) is over 100,000 tokens at higher rates, for the whole request. The config has a long_context block for that.

  • Every rate is per model in the config, not a multiplier. Cache reads are 0.05× on Opus 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5 but 0.1× on Haiku 5.5, so a single multiplier would be wrong.

Why the markup sits on the pack, not on usage: a credit is then one unit everywhere, so credits bought on iOS and on the web mix in one balance with no per-lot bookkeeping. The extra the store takes is paid when the pack is bought, on that channel. usage_markup stays available (default 0) if usage itself should carry a margin (e.g. to cover infrastructure).

Rough size of a request (an estimate, not measured): one editor ask on Sonnet 5.5 runs about 2–4 tool rounds. Each round reads a cached system prompt of about 6 K tokens and writes about 1–2 K output tokens (thinking included); the first round also writes the cache. That comes to roughly 3–12 credits per ask, about twice that on Opus. So a US$9.99 iOS pack (713 credits) is about 60–240 asks. Measure this in the sandbox before setting pack sizes.

Low balance, hard stop, refunds.

  • The app warns below low_balance_credits.

  • If the balance doesn’t cover a request’s worst case, the proxy first lowers max_tokens to what the balance does cover. If that would leave fewer than 1,024 tokens, it refuses with 402 and nothing is sent to Anthropic.

  • A request can still cost more than its estimate. The settle then debits up to max_overdraft_credits below zero and records the rest as written_off (our loss, which reconciliation shows). The next request is refused until the user tops up.

  • Store refunds and chargebacks claw back the pack’s credits (Apple REFUND / REVOKE notifications; Stripe charge.refunded / charge.dispute.created). The balance can go negative and stays blocked until it is topped up.

  • We don’t refund usage. Support can make manual adjustments (adjust entries).

Caps and abuse (config, per account):

  • a ceiling per request;

  • daily and monthly spend caps;

  • concurrent streams;

  • requests per minute;

  • new accounts per IP (Directory rate limit);

  • suspension (status = suspended) for misuse under the Usage Policy.

Region and currency. On iOS, each pack is one App Store product at a price tier. Apple converts it to local currency and handles tax, and the credits are the same everywhere. On the web, Stripe prices in USD to start (local-currency prices later). The config’s price_usd is the US reference price, used to check margins.

The AI proxy (agent/relay/src/billing/proxy.ts)¶

POST /ai/v1/messages. It takes a session token and an Anthropic Messages body (the same body the BYO-key provider sends), and handles a request in these steps:

  1. Authenticate. Check the session token (sst1.<account>.<exp>.<hmac>).

  2. Allowlist the request. Accepted fields: model (an enabled model in the config), max_tokens (capped by max_output_tokens), system, messages, tools (custom tools only), tool_choice, thinking (adaptive only), output_config (effort only), cache_control, stream. Everything else is a 400. That keeps out what usage doesn’t meter, or that we don’t want to pay for:

    • server tools such as web search (US$10 per 1,000 searches) and code execution;

    • MCP servers;

    • fast mode;

    • inference_geo: "us" (1.1×);

    • fallbacks, betas, batches, containers.

    The client’s metadata is dropped. We set metadata.user_id to the opaque account id.

  3. Reserve the worst case. Every prompt token is priced at the dearest input rate (the 1-hour cache write), and every allowed output token at the output rate. Prompt tokens are estimated as text bytes / 3, plus 5,000 per image. If the balance is short, the proxy lowers max_tokens to fit; below 1,024 it answers 402. The request id doubles as the reservation id: a client retry with the same Idempotency-Key finds its hold instead of reserving twice.

  4. Call Anthropic with our key, over a header set built from scratch. stream is forced on.

  5. Stream through. Each SSE frame is re-emitted as it arrives, after redact(). The usage meter reads message_start and the cumulative message_delta usage.

  6. Settle at the actual charge and append one event to the stream: event: splanc_billing with {charged_credits, balance_credits}. The app’s existing stream parser ignores unknown events, so nothing else changes.

    • The app hangs up: the proxy cancels the upstream (so we stop paying) and bills message_start’s input plus an output estimate (streamed bytes / 3, capped at max_tokens). The entry is flagged estimated: true.

    • Anthropic rejects the request (4xx/5xx before the stream): the hold is released uncharged, because Anthropic doesn’t bill a rejected request.

    • The Worker dies mid-stream: the hold expires after 20 minutes and is released. The expired ids are logged for reconciliation.

Reconciliation.

  • Daily: compare the sum of settled usage entries with Anthropic’s Usage & Cost Admin API for the key (raw HTTP usage_report/messages). That catches estimated entries and expired holds.

  • Monthly: compare credits sold minus credits spent with store payouts.

Ledger (ledger.ts, account.ts)¶

One BillingAccount Durable Object per account holds that account’s ledger, so all balance changes for one account are serialised in one place, and different accounts never contend. The Ledger class also serialises its own operations, so it is race-free on any storage. The tests run 50 concurrent reserves on a storage that yields on every call. With the lock removed, those tests fail.

Accounts and identity¶

  • Anonymous device accounts (scaffolded). POST /billing/account mints acct_… and a 180-day session token, rate-limited per IP. The app stores the token next to its other settings. It starts with no credit (no trial).

  • Linking (decided: Apple + Google, no e-mail). An anonymous account links to Sign in with Apple or Sign in with Google and keeps its balance, so credits follow the person across devices and survive reinstalls. Both are offered on every platform; Sign in with Apple satisfies 4.8 on iOS. The server verifies the provider’s ID token (Apple’s / Google’s JWKS, our client ids as audience) and stores only the provider’s opaque subject id (link:<provider>:<sub> → account). If that identity already has an account, the app offers to switch to it; balances are never merged automatically.

  • Buying is 18+ (decided). POST /billing/age {confirm: true} records the confirmation on the account (adult_confirmed_at); GET /billing/balance returns adult; the app shows Buy only for a confirmed adult (mayBuyCredits). An Apple purchase is still credited if it somehow arrives without it (the user has paid); the web checkout endpoint refuses to start one.

  • Restore. Consumables don’t restore through StoreKit, so “restore” means signing in on the new device. The balance is on the server.

  • Account deletion (Apple 5.1.1(v)): DELETE /billing/account, in the app. Decided: any credit left is forfeited, after a clear warning (DELETE_ACCOUNT_WARNING: “Any credits left on it are lost and are not refunded.”). It closes the account, drops open holds and unlinks Apple / Google identities. Ledger entries hold no personal data and are kept as long as tax law requires. Apple’s own refund flow stays available anyway.

  • iOS account binding. At purchase the app passes appAccountToken = accountRefFor(account_id) (a UUID derived from the account id). The server checks that the signed transaction carries the same token, so a receipt can’t be credited to another account. The code has this check in applyPurchase (wrong_account).

Account model.

  • With a session and an identity nobody has: link (the balance stays).

  • Without a session: an identity already linked signs in to its account (recovery on a new device); an unknown one opens a new linked account (or answers no_account for restore).

  • With a session and an identity linked to ANOTHER account: conflict. The answer shows both balances and linked providers and carries a 10-minute switch ticket. The app asks: “Switch to that account” or “Keep this device’s account”. Balances are never merged. When this device’s account is empty and has no sign-in or email, the app switches without asking (nothing can be lost); when it has credits and nothing leads back to it, switching needs an explicit “I understand … stay behind” tick.

  • One identity per provider per account (provider_linked), one account per identity (link:<provider>:<sub>, first claim wins).

  • Sign out is local (the device forgets its session); offered only when a sign-in or email leads back to the account.

  • Deletion revokes the Apple token first (Apple’s rule; one retry; if Apple can’t be reached the deletion answers 503 and nothing changed), then unlinks everything and closes the account (credit forfeited, as decided).

  • Stored: sub, aud, linked_at and (Apple) the encrypted refresh token. No name, no email, no hashed email: Apple is asked for no scopes, Google for openid; the email claim in Google’s token is never read.

  • With #557 (email recovery): email is one more way back. Unlink and sign out count a verified email as a way back; the conflict auto-switch doesn’t fire when this device’s account has one. When #557’s session generations land, SigninHost.session in endpoint.ts is the one place to add the generation check.

Apple In-App Purchase¶

  • App (StoreKit 2). The first-party Capacitor plugin @splanc/storekit-bridge (JS StoreKitBridge, Swift package SplancStorekitBridge, iOS 15+) wraps Product.products, Product.purchase(options: [.appAccountToken(…)]), Transaction.finish, Transaction.unfinished, Transaction.updates (listened to from launch, events retained until the web side subscribes), AppStore.sync and AppStore.canMakePayments. It passes on only transactions StoreKit itself verified, as {transactionId, originalTransactionId, productId, jws}. web/src/net/storeKit.ts binds it; web/src/effects/ai/iap.ts is the logic:

    • Packs are the relay’s apple packs (credits) that the App Store also returns (Apple’s localized displayPrice), in the relay’s order. A product not yet approved in App Store Connect is simply not shown.

    • Purchase. appAccountToken = the account’s account_ref (from GET /billing/balance). The signed transaction goes to POST /billing/purchase/apple {jws}; the transaction is finished only once the relay credited it.

    • Finishing rules. Finish on 201 (credited), 200 (replay), 409 already_claimed and 400 revoked (nothing is owed on this device). Keep it unfinished on 401 (signed out), 403 wrong_account (bought for another account, e.g. before a restore), a network error or a relay failure: StoreKit hands it back at every launch, and “Check for App Store purchases” re-sends it on demand. The relay credits once whatever is re-sent.

    • Background delivery (startIapDelivery, at app start on iOS with the flag on): unfinished transactions are re-sent, and Transaction.updates (Ask to Buy approved, a pending payment cleared) is credited and announced with a toast. Deliveries are de-duplicated per transaction.

    • Outcomes in the flow: purchased → success with the new balance; pending (Ask to Buy, or a payment that needs another step) → “Waiting for approval”, credited automatically later; cancelled → back to the packs (“Nothing was charged”); a StoreKit error → the packs with a plain message; paid but not credited yet → “Payment received, adding credits” with Check again.

    • Restore. Consumables don’t restore. The balance lives on the account (restore it with the recovery email); “Check for App Store purchases” (AppStore.sync + re-send) covers a purchase whose credit never landed.

    • No web checkout inside the iOS app: startWebCheckout refuses, and no Stripe copy or link is shown. An older iOS build without the plugin keeps credits hidden (“Update Splanc to buy credits”).

  • Relay (apple.ts).

    • JWS verification, local. The x5c chain must be exactly leaf → intermediate → root, the root byte-equal to the pinned Apple Root CA G3 (SHA-256 63:34:3A:…:91:79), the intermediate a CA with Apple’s WWDR marker (1.2.840.113635.100.6.2.1), the leaf with the App Store signing marker (1.2.840.113635.100.6.11.1), every signature valid (ECDSA P-384 / P-256, a small DER reader since Workers have no X.509 parser), every certificate valid at the payload’s signedDate, nothing signed in the future, and the JWS itself ES256 by the leaf. That is what Apple’s app-store-server-library SignedDataVerifier does with online (OCSP) checks off. No network call and no secret are needed to verify.

    • A purchase must be for dev.splanc.app, Sandbox or Production (Xcode / LocalTesting transactions are signed locally and refused), type Consumable, not revoked, quantity 1, an enabled apple pack, and carry the account’s appAccountToken (compared case-insensitively). The credit key is apple:<originalTransactionId> (= transactionId for a consumable), plus the Directory’s first-claim pur:apple:<id>.

    • App Store Server Notifications v2 (POST /billing/notify/apple, {signedPayload}): verified the same way, bundle id checked, the signed transaction inside verified too. REFUND and REVOKE claw back once (a retried notification is a replay: nothing more is clawed back or counted). CONSUMPTION_REQUEST is answered with consent (below). TEST, ONE_TIME_CHARGE (the app credits from the transaction itself), REFUND_DECLINED and REFUND_REVERSED (rare; adjust by hand) are acknowledged and logged (ai_apple_notification). Answers: 400 for a bad signature or another app; 200 for another environment (a Production notification on the sandbox relay), so Apple stops retrying; 500 when processing failed, so Apple retries.

    • Environments. AI_BILLING=sandbox credits only Sandbox transactions (TestFlight and development builds); a Production one is wrong_environment. Production stays refused until the production switch (staged plan step 8).

Web checkout (Stripe)¶

This is the web and Android channel (stripe.ts, endpoint.ts). The iOS app never offers it: mayShowWebCheckout() and startWebCheckout refuse inside the iOS app.

  • Buy flow (app). A dedicated flow, one step per screen with one primary action each (web/src/ui/screens/creditsFlow.ts; the pure state machine is web/src/effects/ai/creditsFlow.ts). Entry points: Settings ▸ AI ▸ Splanc AI credits, the “Set up AI” sheet’s credits card, and the editor’s out-of-credits (402) / low-balance messages.

    1. Intro (#/credits): what credits are, 1 credit = US$0.01 of AI usage with our fee in the pack price, a typical prompt’s 3–12 credits, and that prompts aren’t stored.

    2. Account + 18+: an anonymous device account, created together with an explicit “I’m 18 or older” consent (POST /billing/account, then POST /billing/age).

    3. Recovery email (optional): “Add email (recommended)” sends a 6-digit code (POST /billing/account/email, then /verify); “Continue without email” first shows that credits can’t be recovered without one, then records POST /billing/account/anonymous.

    4. Pack cards from GET /billing/pricing?channel=web (price, credits, about how many prompts).

    5. Hand-off: explains the secure Stripe page, then POST /billing/checkout {sku, return_url} and the same tab goes to Stripe Checkout. Only a https://checkout.stripe.com/ URL is ever opened.

    6. Return (?checkout=success|cancel#/credits/return): the balance is polled until the webhook’s credit lands (success, with the balance counting in), or “still processing” after ~16 s; cancel says nothing was charged and offers the packs again.

    Settings ▸ AI shows the account: balance (low / empty), Buy more, the recovery email (masked; change / remove with a warning), recent activity (GET /billing/statement) and a danger zone. Delete account is its own confirmation step with the forfeit warning. A fresh device restores with Restore an existing account (/billing/recover/start, then /verify), which signs the account out elsewhere; any 401 offers “Restore account”.

  • POST /billing/checkout (session; account active; 18+ required, 403 age_required). The pack must be an enabled web pack with a stripe_price_id in the pricing config: price ids are config, never code, and test-mode and live ids differ. The relay creates a Checkout Session (POST /v1/checkout/sessions, Stripe-Version: 2026-06-24.dahlia) with:

    • mode=payment and one line item, the pack’s price;

    • client_reference_id = the account ref;

    • metadata on both the session and payment_intent_data: account_id, sku, channel (refunds and disputes name the PaymentIntent);

    • automatic_tax[enabled]=true (Stripe Tax: Checkout collects the address tax needs);

    • success / cancel URLs back into the app. Only splanc.io, splanc.pages.dev and localhost return URLs are honoured (safeReturnUrl); anything else falls back to BILLING_APP_URL, default https://splanc.io/app/#/settings/ai (the app sends any ?checkout= return to #/credits/return).

    Payment methods are Stripe’s dynamic ones: cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay as enabled in the Dashboard. Apple Pay is registered for splanc.io and splanc.pages.dev. Stripe’s error message is never passed through: the user gets a 503 “Checkout is unavailable right now”, and the log gets the error type and code (ai_checkout_failed, which alerts).

  • Webhook POST /billing/notify/web (the test-mode destination points at the relay’s workers.dev address; events checkout.session.completed, checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded, charge.refunded, charge.dispute.created).

    • Signature. Stripe-Signature is verified on the raw body: HMAC-SHA256 of <t>.<body> with STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET, a 5-minute tolerance, and any v1 matching (secret rolls). A bad signature is a 400 plus ai_webhook_rejected.

    • Idempotency. A processed event id is remembered for 30 days (Directory sevt:<id>), so a retried event is a 200 no-op. The credit itself is also idempotent on web:<session id>, so completed followed by async_payment_succeeded credits once.

    • Credit. Only when payment_status=paid. applyPurchase checks:

      • the environment: a live-mode event can’t credit a sandbox relay;

      • that client_reference_id equals the account ref of metadata.account_id;

      • that the pack exists;

      • the global first claim.

      It then records PaymentIntent → (account, web:<session>).

    • Claw back. charge.refunded claws back the cumulative refunded fraction (amount_refunded / amount; partial refunds are proportional, and a later full refund takes the rest). charge.dispute.created claws back everything and logs kind=chargeback (the dispute alert pages).

    • Errors. A transient failure is a 500, so Stripe retries. A permanent problem (wrong account, unknown pack, wrong environment) is a 200 plus ai_purchase_rejected (an alert), because a retry can’t fix it.

  • Modes. AI_BILLING=sandbox accepts only test keys (sk_test_ / rk_test_) and test-mode events; a live key turns the whole feature off. The fake web purchase route closes once Stripe is configured. AI_BILLING=production requires live keys (sk_live_ / rk_live_), the webhook secret and BILLING_ADMIN_TOKEN, counts only live-mode events, and never uses the fake store. Sessions, account tags and the liability counter are per mode. The switch is docs/ops/billing-go-live.md.

  • Tests (agent/tests/billing_stripe.test.ts) use a fake api.stripe.com and events signed like Stripe’s: form encoding, session parameters, signature checks, 18+, packs, credit-once, partial and full refunds, disputes, wrong account, live events, the live-key guard, and no Stripe secret in any response or log.

Apple refund requests (CONSUMPTION_REQUEST)¶

When a customer asks Apple for a refund, Apple sends CONSUMPTION_REQUEST and asks how much of the purchase was used. The developer answers with “Send Consumption Information” (PUT /inApps/v2/transactions/consumption/{transactionId}) within 12 hours, and only if the customer consented; without consent, it doesn’t respond at all. Apple says the consent must be freely given, specific, informed, opt-in and withdrawable, and must not be the App Tracking Transparency prompt (Send Consumption Information). The body’s fields are customerConsented, consumptionPercentage (in milliunits), deliveryStatus, refundPreference and sampleContentProvided (ConsumptionRequest, and the field set in Apple’s app-store-server-library 3.1.0).

  • Consent (owner decision: yes). On iOS the app shows APPLE_CONSUMPTION_CONSENT_TEXT with an explicit Yes / No, once in the purchase flow (before the first purchase) and as a switch in AI settings. The default is off.

    • POST /billing/consent {apple_consumption: true|false} records it on the account with its time.

    • Withdrawing the consent stops every later answer.

    • The App Store privacy label discloses it.

  • The answer. Credits are one pooled balance, so the relay attributes usage FIFO: the oldest credits are spent first, and a refund ends its own lot (lotConsumption in ledger.ts). From that it sends:

    • deliveryStatus: DELIVERED: the credits were on the account and usable;

    • consumptionPercentage: the share of that pack’s credits spent;

    • refundPreference: GRANT_FULL if nothing was used, GRANT_PRORATED if part was, DECLINE if all was (owner decision 16);

    • sampleContentProvided: true: the free tier counts as a sample (owner decision 17).

  • Failures. If the API call fails, the relay answers the notification with 500 so that Apple retries, and logs ai_consumption_failed (an alert). An unknown transaction, or one without consent, is logged ai_consumption_skipped and nothing is sent.

  • The sender (AppStoreConsumptionSender, apple.ts): PUT /inApps/v2/transactions/consumption/{transactionId} on api.storekit-sandbox.apple.com or api.storekit.apple.com (by the notification’s environment), 202 expected, authenticated with an ES256 JWT (kid = key id, iss = issuer id, aud: appstoreconnect-v1, bid: dev.splanc.app, 5 minutes) signed with the In-App Purchase key (Worker secrets APPLE_IAP_KEY, APPLE_IAP_KEY_ID, APPLE_IAP_ISSUER_ID). Errors carry only the status and Apple’s numeric errorCode. Without the key, a consented request is a 500 plus ai_consumption_failed {reason: not_configured} (an alert).

  • In the app. The iOS buy flow asks once, before the first purchase, on its own step: two equal options (“Yes, share my usage” / “No, don’t share”), nothing preselected, Continue disabled until one is chosen (apple_consumption_asked on GET /billing/balance records that it was answered). Settings ▸ AI ▸ Splanc AI credits ▸ Apple refund requests turns it on or off.

  • Tests. The fake store’s CONSUMPTION_REQUESTs (billing.test.ts) and real-format signed notifications (billing_apple.test.ts) check: nothing is sent without consent (never asked, or no), the answer with consent (the sandbox host, the JWT, the body), a withdrawn consent stops answers, a failure or a missing key returns 500, and FIFO attribution.

Email and recovery¶

The problem it solves: credits sit on a server account, but an anonymous account lives only in the app’s storage. Clearing that storage, or changing device, loses the session token and with it the balance. A verified email lets the user get back in. It is optional (decision 6a), and it uses one-time codes by email rather than passwords.

Rules.

  • Codes. 6 digits from a uniform random source, valid 10 minutes, single use. A new code replaces the old one.

  • Attempts. Each code allows 5 attempts; the 5th wrong one burns it.

  • Rate limits. Codes sent: 10 an hour per IP, 5 an hour per address (and per account), 2,000 a day overall. Code checks: 30 an hour per IP. That caps a guesser at about 25 tries an hour against a 1-in-a-million code.

  • One email per account, one account per email. Verifying a new address replaces the old one and frees it. The map is claimed atomically, so a race can’t link one email to two accounts.

  • Recovery signs other devices out. Tokens are sst2.<account>.<gen>.…; recovery bumps the account’s session generation, so tokens minted before it stop working (401). Deletion bumps it too. sst1 tokens from before this change count as generation 0. Like sst1, sst2 tokens are scoped to the billing mode (AI_BILLING): a sandbox token never opens a production relay.

  • Emails are per billing mode. The keyed hash includes the mode in production (sandbox keeps the plain input), so the email map, codes and per-address limits are separate per mode. An email verified on a sandbox (test-money) account can’t recover it once the relay runs in production; the user attaches it again to their production account. Recovery also refuses an account of the other environment.

  • No account enumeration.

    • recover/start answers identically for known and unknown emails, and sends in the background (waitUntil) so timing doesn’t tell either.

    • account/email answers identically for free and taken emails.

    • Every code failure (wrong, expired, burned, or no code because the email has no account) is the same 400 with the same text. Attempts left are not reported.

What is stored, and why.

  • The lookup. A keyed hash of the normalized address (HMAC-SHA256 with BILLING_EMAIL_HASH_KEY) maps the email to the account (em:<hash> in the Directory). Recovery needs exactly this lookup; with a key, a storage leak can’t be dictionary-attacked.

  • On the account: the same hash, a display hint (k•••@gmail.com) and when it was verified.

  • Not the address itself. Every flow that sends a code gets the address from the user in that same request, so the server never needs to keep it. If a later feature has to email users unprompted (receipts, policy notices), add an AES-GCM-encrypted copy under its own key then, and update the privacy notice.

  • Codes: stored only as an HMAC with an expiry and an attempt count (ec:<purpose>:<hash>). The alarm purges expired ones.

  • Logs: no address and no code (a test scans storage and logs for both).

  • Normalization: trim and lowercase only. No provider folding such as Gmail dots: the code goes to what the user typed.

  • BILLING_EMAIL_HASH_KEY is set once. Rotating it orphans every stored email.

Deletion. DELETE /billing/account erases the email: it is removed from the account, the email → account map entry and any pending codes are deleted, and every device is signed out. The address can then be used on a new account.

Purchase flow (UI, on agent/credits-flow-ui).

  • Before the first purchase, ask for an email (“so you can get your credits back on a new phone”), with an explicit “Continue without email” that shows the warning and records POST /billing/account/anonymous.

  • AI settings shows the email hint, or “No email: credits can’t be recovered if you lose this device’s data”, plus “Recover my account” on a fresh install.

Privacy notice impact.

  • We process an email address, only if the user adds one, to verify it and to recover the account.

  • We keep only a keyed hash and a masked hint, not the address.

  • The address is passed to our email provider (a sub-processor, see below) to deliver the code.

  • Deleting the account erases it.

  • App Store privacy label: “Email address: App functionality (account recovery), linked to the user, not used for tracking”.

  • Retention (decided): no prompt content; ledger and financial records as long as tax law requires; request logs 30 days.

  • Default: no prompt content is retained by Splanc.

    • The proxy streams bodies through and never stores them.

  • The account id sent to Anthropic as metadata.user_id is opaque: no e-mail, no device name.

  • Anthropic. It keeps API inputs and outputs for up to 30 days (2 years if flagged) and doesn’t train on them. The app asks for consent before the first credits request (Apple 5.1.2(i)) and the privacy notice says this.

Org spend limit¶

Anthropic’s rate and spend limits are per organization: every Splanc user shares them. The owner launches on the current usage tier and raises it when needed, so the relay watches the org’s spend and fails gracefully:

  • Counter. After each settle the proxy adds the request’s Anthropic list cost (µUSD) to a month-to-date counter in the Directory DO (org:spend:<YYYY-MM>). The config’s org.monthly_limit_usd is the Console’s monthly limit: US$1,000 (owner, 2026-11-18). Each ai_settled event carries org_month_to_date_usd.

  • Alert. Crossing org.alert_fraction (80 %: US$800) logs ai_org_spend_alert once, with alert: true, and Grafana’s “spend near limit” rule fires on org_month_to_date_usd > 800. That is the signal to raise the tier.

  • Graceful stop. From org.stop_fraction (98 %: US$980) on, the proxy answers 503 ai_credits_busy (“Splanc AI credits are busy right now. Try again later.”) before calling Anthropic, so no turn dies halfway and nothing is charged. It logs ai_org_spend_stop (alert: true).

  • The real limit. If Anthropic answers 429 enforced_spend_limit_reached anyway (the counter is list cost, and the Console’s limit may differ), the proxy releases the reservation uncharged, answers the same busy message, and logs ai_org_spend_limit_hit (alert: true). Other 429s (rate limits) and 529 “overloaded” errors also answer busy, and 429s log with alert: true.

  • Reconciliation. The daily job (staged plan, Ops) replaces the estimate with Anthropic’s own cost report and alerts on drift.

  • Alerting. Grafana Cloud alerting (Observability and alerts).

Observability and alerts¶

Owner decision: billing alerts go to Grafana Cloud alerting, the same splanc.grafana.net stack the HITL rigs and fx_bench dashboards use.

    • ai_refused, ai_released, ai_upstream_error, ai_upstream_unreachable, ai_stream_error, ai_holds_expired;

    • the org events ai_org_spend_alert, ai_org_spend_stop, ai_org_spend_limit_hit;

    • ai_purchase, and ai_refund with its kind (refund / revoke / chargeback);

Risks¶

  • Store review. Apple may question an AI-credits consumable. The mitigation is following 3.1.1 exactly: In-App Purchase, no expiry, no steering, plus account deletion, AI-data consent and content reporting.

  • Margin squeeze. Anthropic prices change (usually downward), and a fee tier can change. Prices are config, validated against min_margin, and the ledger records the version used.

  • Estimate gaps. Cut-off streams are billed from an estimate. Daily reconciliation and the estimated flag find drift.

  • Org-wide limits. One user’s burst can rate-limit everyone, and we launch on the current usage tier. Per-account caps, the org spend counter with its 80 % alert, and the graceful 98 % stop keep it visible and polite; raising the tier is the fix.

  • Policy exposure. A user’s misuse can get our Anthropic account suspended. That’s why we have caps, suspension, the allowlist, and lighting scope only.

  • Tax on the web. We collect and file it (Stripe Tax computes it): registration thresholds per US state / the EU need watching.

  • Free plan. Workers Free CPU and request limits may not cover streaming at volume.
