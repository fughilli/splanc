Mesh ranging spike: FTM-style RTT on our own netstack¶

A spike for mesh.md §13.2: does the ESP32-C6’s 802.11mc FTM timestamp hardware work under our netstack (which replaces the vendor Wi-Fi stack), and how well does it range between two lamps? The target is metre to sub-metre link ranges, fed to the mesh first as link ranges and later as range_m on MeshMember.

Verdict: conditional GO.

  • The hardware path works under our MAC, with no vendor Wi-Fi stack.

  • One exchange has a σ of 0.3–0.7 m. The mean of 16 exchanges has a σ of 0.07–0.2 m.

  • Rate, frame length, TX power, channel and direction move the bias by less than 0.2 m.

  • One problem blocks product use: a per-boot offset of up to 2.6 m one-way. It follows a per-device clock-phase state that the device can read from its own stamps. Root-causing it is the next spike (S2, below).

Everything was measured on hitl-rig-1 with c6-123170 → c6-711f48, both silicon v0.2. The boards sit on the bench at a fixed distance that nobody has measured. The images ran the mesh spike build (:esp32c6_netstack_meshspike) with MESH TX type=ftm. Raw logs and analyses are in /workspace/.claude/ftm/runs/r1..r6b.

1. The timestamp hardware¶

Found by disassembling the vendor blobs (arduino-esp32 3.3.10 = IDF 5.5): libpp hal_mac_ftm_get_t3, wDev_record_ftm_data_local, lmac_record_txtime, and libnet80211 ftm_record_t2t3_cb, ftm_record_t1t4_cb, ftm_get_phy_comp.

Every stamp combines three parts:

  • the MAC’s 1 MHz counter (0x600AD000, the clock M4 found);

  • a 7-bit sub-µs tick (0–79, 80 MHz);

  • on receive, an 11-bit fine field.

The vendor converts each stamp to raw units of 1/640 µs (1.5625 ns). libnet80211 multiplies raw units by 1562.5 to get ps. One raw unit of RTT is 0.234 m of one-way range.

fold(x) = x if x <= 1023 else 2048 - x is the blob’s own treatment. On silicon, the fine field sits around −176 (signed) and the RTT regresses on it with coefficient 1.0, so the fold is right.

The per-queue words are the end of the queue’s existing status block (TXQ_STATUS_BASE 0x600A54E0, PMD 0x54E8). The hardware latches them whenever a queued frame gets its ACK. No enable bit was needed. The receiver’s MAC writes t3 back into the RX vector of the frame it ACKed. The vendor waits 50 µs before reading it, and our reap is always later than that.

Other findings:

  • Effective resolution: RX ticks are always even and TX ticks always odd. Sampling runs at 40 MHz (25 ns = 16 raw), and the fine field interpolates within that.

  • Vendor calibration: ftm_get_phy_comp adds a constant to t3 (608–786 raw, set by bandwidth and channel group). phy_ftm_comp returns 151/199/253 by bandwidth. Both are static, so we calibrate our own constant instead.

  • Silicon revisions: WIFI-9686 bars rev ≤ v0.1 as vendor FTM initiators. Most bench boards are v0.2: 123170, 711130, 711f48, 003f08, fa0324, 71081c, 71226c. c6-2ce684 is v0.1. The erratum is about the vendor stack’s initiator. Under our own exchange both sides only latch stamps, so whether v0.1 behaves the same is untested.

2. The exchange¶

  1. A sends a unicast ACKed frame (a sealed link probe, 64-byte body).

  2. A reads t1 and t4 from its queue’s registers after the completion.

  3. B logs its RX vector, which holds t2 and t3.

  4. The host joins the two logs by sequence number.

The formula is the FTM one: RTT = (t4 − t1) − (t3 − t2).

Clock drift dominates if left uncorrected. The two crystals differ by 49 ppm. The turnaround (t3 − t2: frame airtime plus SIFS, 207 µs at 6 Mb/s for 64 bytes) is timed on B’s clock, so the raw RTT moves by 10 raw (2.4 m) per 1000-byte frame. The ratio comes from the same stamps: the slope of (t2 − t1) across the exchanges. That needs no extra traffic, and its σ is about 0.1 ppm over 3 s. Correcting the turnaround by it makes the RTT independent of length and rate (§3).

  • To reach 0.25 m, an estimate needs about 2–3 exchanges on a clean channel and about 8 on ch 6. To reach 0.1 m, it needs about 14 and about 50.

  • Averaging follows 1/√k out to 64 exchanges.

  • About 3 % of exchanges are one-sided ±2.6 µs outliers, which a 5-MAD cut removes.

  • About 3 % of first attempts get no ACK (st=5); those are simply not exchanges.

  • DSSS (1 and 11 Mb/s) is unusable, with σ around 90 m. Its stamps carry no fine timing. Ranging uses OFDM only, as FTM does.

Bias stability within one boot¶

Over these conditions, the drift-corrected RTT stays within ±0.15 m:

  • frame length 64, 400 and 1000 bytes;

  • rates 6, 12, 24, 36 and 54 Mb/s;

  • RSSI from −9 to −42 dBm;

  • A→B and B→A.

Channel 1 reads about 0.3–0.4 m longer than channels 6 and 11, a small channel term like the vendor’s.

The per-boot offset¶

Across reboots, the pair’s constant moved over 11 raw (2.6 m one-way). Within each boot, A→B and B→A agreed to 0.05 m.

The offset tracks a per-device state that the responder can read from its own stamps: (t3 tick − t2 tick) mod 80 is 39 or 41 for a whole boot.

  • With both devices in state 39, the constant was 1407.5 ± 0.15 in all 6 such boots (r6a and r6b).

  • A device in state 41 adds 7–11 raw.

  • In r5 (both rebooted together), the two devices always came up in opposite states.

The likely mechanism is a TX/RX clock-domain crossing whose divider phase is random at boot. Changing channel does not re-roll it.

4. Go / no-go¶

Go:

  • The hardware gives FTM-grade stamps under our MAC.

  • The exchange uses frames we already send (ACKed unicast).

  • Precision beats the brief’s target by several times.

  • Drift is handled from the same stamps.

Not yet product-accurate:

  • The per-boot offset makes absolute ranges wrong by up to 2.6 m until it is fixed.

  • Absolute calibration needs a known distance.

  • Indoor multipath bias (typically 1–2 m for FTM) is unmeasured, because the rig boards are close together with line of sight.

S2 (next spike)¶

  1. Find the register behind the 39/41 state and either force it at bring-up or re-roll it without a reboot. A reboot is not acceptable: effects must keep running. Mitigation if it can’t be fixed: each device reports its state, and either the mesh solves one offset per state per device, or it uses only state-39 pairs for absolute range.

  2. Measure ground truth:

    • known distances, 0.5–10 m, with the boards moved by a human or on the mesh rig (#578/#598);

    • coax with known lengths through attenuators, for the conducted case;

    • room multipath.

  3. Try HT20/HT40 frames. Our TX path sends legacy OFDM only. A 40 MHz bandwidth should halve σ, and the vendor’s phy_ftm_comp already has a per-bandwidth constant.

5. Product plan (after S2)¶

Where it lives.

  • The netstack’s P2P TX completion (WifiP2p::last_tx, TxStamp) gains t1 and t4, read from the queue’s three registers when an ACKed unicast completes. That is three MMIO reads in the path that already polls the PMD.

  • RxVector gains t2 and t3.

  • A ranging module in firmware/mesh (next to meshtime.rs) keeps a small table per neighbour:

    • the last K (seq, t2, t3) for frames we received from it;

    • the last K (seq, t1, t4) for frames we sent it.

  • Nothing is in C++. The mesh FFI is untouched apart from a getter.

Protocol.

  • Every ACKed unicast mesh frame is a free half-exchange.

  • The receiver ships its (seq, t2, t3) pairs back in a link TLV on its next frame to that neighbour, or in its time beacon:

    • each pair takes 1 byte of seq plus two 5-byte stamps;

    • 16 pairs take about 180 bytes.

  • When traffic is idle, a member that wants a range sends a burst of K small ACKed probes:

    • 64 bytes at 24 Mb/s, about 110 µs each including the ACK;

    • 16 probes plus one report take about 2 ms of airtime per link estimate.

  • For 6 fixtures (15 links) refreshed every 10 s, that is about 30 ms in 10 s, or 0.3 % of airtime.

Drift. The relative clock rate comes from the same stamps, or from M4’s per-neighbour mesh-time fit, which already estimates it.

Render isolation.

  • Ranging runs in the netstack loop, below render priority (#371). Its added cost is the register reads plus the TLV, a few µs per frame.

  • The spike’s busy-wait for TX completion is not the product path. Bursts go through the backhaul’s normal async TX queue and its airtime pacing, so a burst never holds the CPU.

  • A burst is at most ~2 ms of radio time, shorter than a BLE event, and is scheduled like a time beacon (jittered), never back to back with an effect upload.

API to the mesh.

  • Each neighbour entry gets a LinkRange { range_m, sigma_m, n, age_ms, state } (MESH NB).

  • Ranges are gossiped one hop in the time beacon. The gateway’s get_mesh exposes MeshMember.range_m per link.

  • The app’s room solver (web/src/geom/multiFixture.ts) uses them as constraints (§12).

  • state carries the 39/41 phase bit, so consumers can drop or offset-correct until S2 lands.

Costs.

  • RAM: 6 neighbours × 2 × 16 pairs × 11 B ≈ 2.1 KB.

  • Flash: a few KB.

  • No new radio modes and no channel changes.
