Channel sounding and ranging between two ESP32-C6s (research track)¶

Status: research results from the amd-rig bench, 2026-11-17. Bench firmware firmware/chsound, host pipeline tools/chsound. Raw captures and the experiment scripts stay outside the repo (agent host /workspace/.claude/chsound/). This answers the mesh design’s §13 spikes S1a–S2 (docs/design/mesh.md on agent/mesh-design) and goes further, because the owner asked for the best ranging the C6 radio can do, not for BLE Channel Sounding conformance.

1. Summary¶

  • Both raw I/Q directions work on the C6 from our own firmware. RX: the modem dump engine captures 10-bit I/Q at 80, 40, 26.7 or 16 MS/s, up to 16 380 samples (205 µs at 80 MS/s). TX: the modem’s DAC playback engine plays an arbitrary host-uploaded waveform at 80 MS/s, looped, from the same SRAM bank. Both were checked against a HackRF One and against the other C6.

  • We don’t need tone-by-tone hopping. In HT40 mode the TX and RX analog filters pass a flat ~40 MHz (−20…+18 MHz). One 100 µs capture of a 1000-tone multitone gives 1000 channel samples at 39 kHz spacing. That beats a BLE-CS-style single-tone PBR sweep on every axis here: no 72 retunes (each retune costs 0.24 ms plus a random LO phase), and the measurement airtime is 0.6 ms per two-way exchange.

  • Measured precision (conducted, one LO, one exchange): round-trip group delay 2τ std 0.76–0.83 ns, which is 11–12 cm one-way. Averaging floors at ~0.26 ns (4 cm, ~1 min). No drift over 15 min. Synthetic delays of 0.5–12.5 ns injected into the reply waveform are recovered with slope 0.987 and 0.12 ns rms on medians (§6.3).

  • LO phase survives the TX↔RX turnaround when the PLL is not retuned. The two-way carrier phase (LO phases cancel) repeats to 13–30° per exchange (33 ps of 2τ, ~5 mm one-way). That makes a mm-level relative displacement tracker. It drifts thermally (~0.4 ns of 2τ in 15 min), and its 0.41 ns integer ambiguity can’t be fixed from today’s 0.26 ns group-delay floor (§6.5, §8).

  • What limits it: the 80 MHz sample clock wanders against the crystal and the RF LO by about 1 ns over milliseconds (§7.1). Group delay sees this wander. The carrier phase does not. So: keep exchanges short, average, and use carrier phase once the group delay is good enough to fix its integer.

  • Round 2 (§12): tight 2.4 ms bursts hold the carrier phase to 2–15°. With the calibration taken inside the burst, the carrier integer is resolved from ~35–60 bursts: synthetic delays of 0, 0.37 and 2.90 ns come back within +9 ± 3 ps (≈1.3 mm one-way, with a calibratable +9 ps bias). Group delay alone averages to ±0.08 ns (1.2 cm). Two HT40 LOs stitch to an 80 MHz span that shows a 15 ns echo a single 40 MHz shot cannot.

  • Round 3 (§13): 0–12.5 ns injected delays (264 bursts) all resolve to the right carrier cycle within ±1.6 ps of 2τ (±0.25 mm one-way) with ~38 bursts per estimate. The slip rate falls to 3.9 % at 30 bursts. The group-delay “bias” was noise. On-device FFT processing gives ~4× the rate (3.6–3.7 exch/s wall). One-sided tones bring the stitched 80 MHz echo fit to 15.8 ns (truth 15).

  • Absolute path on the ~30 cm coax (§14): the round-trip group delay of 1918 ns and the RF phase delay of 18.5 ns are almost all device latency. The coax is ~2.7 ns round trip. One known path calibrates the total device constant (good to ~±5 cm with the cable’s uncertainty). It can’t split A from B or TX from RX. A zero-length barrel swap is the cheapest way to separate them. A role swap (done) exposed and removed a 2.85 ns processing asymmetry.

  • Deployment (§15): P4+C6 runs it as is (~100–200 exchanges/s with raw captures over SDIO). A C6 player needs a ranging mode: it borrows the netstack’s idle TLS/RX buffers placed at the start of a block (0 extra RAM), all DMA buffers go to other blocks, and bursts take ≤ 4 ms with interrupts off. Effects keep rendering, at the cost of 2–4× more bursts for the carrier fix.

4. Two-way model¶

Each board has one crystal; its LO, DAC, ADC and CPU clocks all share one fractional error ε. Let δ = ε_A − ε_B, which we fit per capture from the tone grid (it is also the CFO: CFO = δ·f_c; A−B ≈ +61.3 ppm on this pair, which is +150 kHz at 2.44 GHz).

  1. A starts its comb at epoch e_A (stamp of the DAC start, A’s clock). B captures it. Per-tone phasors are evaluated at the receiver-clock tone frequencies (f_c + kΔ)(1+δ) − f_c, divided by the known tone amplitudes S_k, giving H_B(k).

  2. The delay D_B (in B’s capture, B’s clock) is the IFFT peak over the uniform tone grid, refined by a weighted least-squares phase slope. The period branch is fixed by the observed onset: arrival_B = y_B + D_B + j·T_P nearest the onset.

  3. B replies the same way. A gets arrival_A.

  4. Tround = arrival_A − e_A (A’s clock) and Treply = e_B − arrival_B (B’s clock).

2τ = Tround − Treply − δ·Treply          (the δ term: 61 ppm × 0.6 ms ≈ 37 ns, so it matters)
φ  = arg H_B(f=0) + arg H_A(f=0) + 2π·δ̄·f_c·(D_B + I_A)

I_A is A’s interval from its epoch to its capture start. δ̄ is δ averaged from both captures. φ is the two-way carrier phase: the LO phases cancel, and what remains is −2π(f_A+f_B)τ plus fixed TX/RX path phases. Both results contain fixed hardware offsets (§6.4).

tools/chsound/twoway.py implements this. cspipe.py holds the DSP.

5. Firmware (firmware/chsound)¶

Arduino-ESP32 3.3.10 image with the vendor Wi-Fi brought up (NULL mode, promiscuous, for calibration) and SRAM blocks 1+2 reserved with SOC_RESERVE_MEMORY_REGION (the image ends at 0x4081f3e0). The host drives it over the USB console. 6. Results¶

6.1 Raw I/Q¶

  • RX (e02): a CW from A seen by B tuned 1 MHz above lands at −850 kHz, and tuned 1 MHz below at +1150 kHz. The sign is consistent, so the RF-positive orientation is as stated. The rates match the cycle stamps.

  • TX (e03, e07b): the HackRF sees a host-uploaded tone at bin 128/2048 at +5.039 MHz (+39 kHz is A’s CFO against the HackRF). LO leakage is −29 dBc. The same image plays through dac, seq D and uploaded masters.

  • Bandwidth (e08, e09): HT40 on both boards gives −22…+20 MHz at ±5 dB, about 30 dB over the floor per tone at gain index 20.

6.4 LO hopping¶

Eleven LOs from 2422 to 2462 MHz (the ±19.5 MHz comb stays inside 2400–2483.5), six exchanges each (e23). Group delay per LO: 1910.7–1912.4 ns for LOs up to 2450 (spread ±0.9 ns: filter group-delay ripple, to calibrate per LO). It then jumps: 1904 ns at 2454, then +120 ns at 2458/2462. HT40-above is not valid above ch 9, so the vendor falls back to another filter setting. Re-run with HT40-below for LO ≥ 2452 (now the default in twoway.py): every LO from 2442 to 2462 lands at 1911.1–1913.0 ns, with 0.14–0.37 ns within-LO scatter (4 back-to-back exchanges each). The carrier phase falls about 36° per 4 MHz across the LOs. That slope is the RF phase delay (~25 ns round trip, dominated by the boards’ RF chains and filters, not the cable). With calibration it gives a third, independent range observable.

Group delay averages roughly as 1/√N to ~0.26 ns, and then the sample-clock wander floors it. The carrier phase is very quiet exchange-to-exchange but drifts slowly (thermal: the RF phase delay of the two boards’ chains). So:

  • Absolute range comes from the group delay: 11 cm per exchange, ~4 cm averaged over a minute. It has no visible drift, so one calibration holds.

  • Relative motion comes from the carrier phase, unwrapped over time: ~5 mm noise per exchange, valid while the motion between exchanges stays below λ/4 (3 cm), with a slow drift to recalibrate against the group delay.

  • Fixing the carrier integer (absolute mm ranging) needs group delay at ~0.07 ns. That is below the 0.26 ns floor measured here. §8 says what would get there.

6.6 Channel impulse response¶

One conducted path. The −12 dB skirts come from the uncalibrated TX/RX filter response (magnitude ripple and the band edges). A per-LO calibration of H (divide by the zero-distance response) is the next step before using the CIR for multipath work.

7. Limits, with evidence¶

7.1 Sample-clock wander (the dominant group-delay error)¶

  • Two captures of A’s free-running comb, 0.4 ms apart on B: epoch residual std 0.28 ns (e14, unimodal).

  • A restarting its DAC 2.7 ms later: ±0.9 ns, bimodal, with the two states ~1.8 ns apart (e14).

  • DAC kept running and TX gated off and on for 5 ms: still ±1 ns (e17). Over the same gap the carrier phase moved only ±30°, which is 34 ps.

So the 80 MHz sample timing wanders against the crystal and the RF LO by about a nanosecond over milliseconds, in steps comparable to one 480 MHz BBPLL cycle (2.08 ns). The LO does not. Consequences: shorter exchanges help (1.5 ms → 0.62 ms took 1.03 → 0.8 ns). The carrier phase is the precision observable.

7.2 Integer ambiguity of the carrier phase¶

The carrier phase gives 2τ modulo 0.41 ns. Fixing the integer needs group delay to about 0.07 ns (6σ). Single-exchange 0.8 ns needs ~130 independent exchanges averaged, or back-to-back bursts (0.3 ns → ~20), or wide-lane combinations across LOs (two LOs 40 MHz apart give a 25 ns ambiguity with σ ≈ 1.3 ns, so they are too noisy alone). Within-burst precision is what makes this practical. See §8.

7.3 Things that did not work, or bit us¶

  • IQ estimator frozen while keyed. phy_tx_tone(1) stops the IQE until a vendor unkey + RX front end (V/U). A register diff of FE/AGC/LPCON between the two states (e21) did not find a register-level fix. So the RX side uses the vendor unkey (~140 µs).

  • Owner switching with a running DAC. The DAC reads whatever block the owner selects. If the capture block is selected, the DAC plays the capture back into its own receiver (feedback, rms 170 LSB). So the DAC is stopped while receiving and restarted per burst.

  • Ambient Wi-Fi fires power triggers. The fix is the narrowband CW-preamble detector with DC removal. Its baseline must be taken before the peer starts.

  • Interrupt watchdog (300 ms): any seq with interrupts off must stay well under that, or the board reboots and loses HT40, gain and the waveform.

  • USB-CDC: base64 dumps occasionally lose a character (the pipeline retries). Holding the port open across a chip reset re-enumerates the C6 as a new ttyACM (the daemon now looks ports up by USB serial and closes before resetting).

  • Preamble misses: ~10–20 % of exchanges time out on B’s detector (PA ramp at key-up, baseline timing). A failed exchange is detected and retried.

Measured ranging performance (conducted, after calibration):

  • Single exchange (group delay, 0.6 ms airtime): σ ≈ 11–12 cm.

  • Averaged: ~1/√N down to σ ≈ 4 cm (0.26 ns, ~8 exchanges here). Below that the sample-clock wander sets a floor.

  • Carrier-phase tracking: ~5 mm per exchange for relative motion, with thermal drift of ~0.025 ns/min of 2τ to recalibrate.

Further gains, roughly in order of payoff:

  1. On-device DSP and fast bursts. Exchanges a few ms apart scattered 0.14–0.3 ns (e23) instead of 0.76 ns 9 s apart. A burst of 16 exchanges in ~20 ms, averaged, should reach ~0.1 ns. That is close to fixing the carrier integer.

  2. Measure the sample-clock wander instead of suffering it. Each board could loop its own TX into its RX (a calibration capture) inside the exchange to reference DAC vs ADC timing. Or the BBPLL/APLL could be locked or characterised so the ~2 ns steps are predictable.

  3. Wider synthesized bandwidth. Stitch HT40 shots at 2422/2462 (80 MHz) for multipath resolution. For precision the group-delay span is already enough. Calibrate H per LO.

  4. More SNR. Not the limit in conducted tests (thermal CRLB ~0.03 ns). Over the air at range it will matter: longer captures (16 k samples), power index 0, coherent averaging across periods.

  5. External hardware, if the owner wants absolute mm: a shared reference clock between fixtures removes the wander. UWB (DW3000) gives ~2–5 cm per shot anyway.

Over the air, multipath will dominate. 40 MHz resolves paths ~7.5 m apart; stitching the LO hops (80 MHz) halves that. The CIR in §6.6 is the tool for that.

9. Rate, airtime and coexistence¶

  • Airtime per two-way exchange: ~0.62 ms (A: 120 µs CW + 150 µs comb; B: 130 µs CW + 150 µs comb; turnaround ~140 µs each). The radio is busy ~0.9 ms per exchange, including waiting for the peer.

  • Rate now is host-limited (~3 s per exchange: two 64 KB captures over USB-CDC, Python DSP). On-device processing (period-average 4×2048 → one 2048-point fixed-point FFT, the C6 has no FPU) is a few ms, so ~100 exchanges/s per pair is plausible. With 11 LOs × a burst, that is a full-band measurement in ~0.2 s.

  • Coexistence with the netstack:

    • The exchange takes the radio off the AP channel for ~1 ms (two retunes, and HT40 needs the vendor channel API). Our MAC must be paused and the Wi-Fi channel restored after. That is the same shape as the netstack’s background scan.

    • The engines own whole 128 KB SRAM blocks while they run (1 + 2 here). On a C6 player that means moving every DMA buffer (LED RMT/PARLIO, Wi-Fi) and anything touched from ISRs out of those blocks for the window. Today’s player heap does not allow that (24 KB floor at 2048 LEDs). A P4+C6 board (C6 as the radio) can run it as is.

    • Interrupts are off for ~1–2 ms per exchange. Effects keep rendering (LED DMA keeps going if its buffers are outside the engine blocks); a frame or two may be late. Nothing reboots.

12. Round 2: tight bursts, carrier-integer resolution, hop stitching¶

Firmware v14 adds: long seq programs (up to ~300 ops), the DAC waveform tiled into the upper 64 KB of block 2 (H, DAC owner 0x10400), a second waveform in block 1 (J, owner 0x200), and Q<slot> to copy each capture into a block-1 slot.

12.1 Tight bursts¶

A burst is one CW-preamble sync followed by 4–5 two-way exchanges, one every 600 µs. Captures stay on chip and are read out afterwards, so a whole burst takes ~2.4–3 ms on air. Results (experiments/e32_pairs.py, analysis e33):

  • Carrier phase within a burst: 2–15° circular std (it was 21° for exchanges 9 s apart). Between bursts a minute apart it wanders ±50°: the “thermal drift” of §6.5 is really a random walk on a ~minute scale.

  • Group delay per exchange inside a burst: still 0.3–0.6 ns. The noise is not slow wander. It is per-exchange engine-start jitter: the DAC restart is bimodal by ±0.9 ns (e14), and each burst exchange restarts the DAC. The carrier phase is immune to it, because a baseband shift doesn’t move the f = 0 intercept.

  • Preamble misses: 3/28, 4/74, 10/89 and 14/133 bursts (5–11 % per burst), retried automatically. Since one sync covers 4–5 exchanges, that is ~1–3 % per exchange, down from 8–20 %.

12.2 Absolute ranging with resolved carrier phase (synthetic delays)¶

The carrier phase pins 2τ to ~10 ps, modulo 1/f_c = 0.41 ns. Its slow wander means the calibration must be taken simultaneously with the measurement. So B alternates two waveforms inside every burst: the normal one (even exchanges, the reference) and one pre-delayed by d both at baseband and at the carrier (odd exchanges). That is exactly what a real extra path length d on the B→A leg would do. Per burst: Δgd = gd(odd) − gd(even), Δφ = φ(odd) − φ(even). The integer comes from the median Δgd (σ ≈ 0.08 ns over 35–60 bursts spread across 5 LOs, 2422–2462 MHz). Then d = (−Δφ/2π + k)/f.

12.3 Hop stitching (80 MHz) and multipath¶

Two HT40 LOs (2422 and 2462 MHz; HT40-below at ≥ 2452) tile 2402–2481.5 MHz. In paired bursts, B’s odd exchanges carry an injected two-path channel (direct + 0.6 × echo at 15 ns). The per-tone magnitude ratio |H(echo)/H(direct)| shows the expected 1/15 ns = 67 MHz ripple across the stitched band. That ripple is not resolvable in one 40 MHz shot: the single-LO fits give Δ = 21.2 ns or a degenerate a. A two-path fit over the 80 MHz stitched span gives a = 0.57, Δ = 17.5 ns (truth 0.6, 15 ns). The residual bias comes from per-side (±f baseband) distortion of the reply: the edges of each 40 MHz segment step. The image/IQ imbalance of the TX path is the likely cause, and it needs a per-LO two-sided calibration before the stitched CIR is metrology-grade.

12.4 What was not done, and why¶

  • On-device DSP (~100 exchanges/s): not implemented. The C6 has no FPU. The plan is to period-average 4×2048 → one 2048-point Q15 FFT → tone phasors → slope, a few ms per capture, plus a ~40 B result per exchange instead of 48 KB of captures. Airtime already allows ~1600 exchanges/s; today’s rate (~1 exchange/s) is the USB link and host Python.

  • Self-measured TX→RX turnaround: not implemented. The C6 cannot capture its own keyed TX (§7.3). The DAC→ADC leakage with the PA off is real: rms 170 LSB of feedback when the DAC plays the capture block. So a device can see its own DAC epoch in its own ADC samples. The missing piece is a memory layout in which the DAC keeps playing a reference waveform while the capture lands elsewhere: every owner setting that maps the capture away also remaps the DAC read pointer (§6, e15/e16). That is the experiment to run next, because it would remove the per-exchange DAC-restart jitter that now limits the group delay.

13.2 DAC restart jitter: can it be avoided?¶

Keep the DAC running. Started once per burst and never stopped, the DAC makes the captures useless: broadband, equal power on the A-bins, B-bins and off-grid. That holds whether the owner points the DAC at its own block or at the capture block. The DAC playback and the capture are one modem dump engine with one SRAM-owner register; they don’t run at the same time. So a self-reference leakage capture of the device’s own running DAC can’t be taken during reception either. The interleaved-tone-set experiment (A on even bins, B on odd) confirms it: no clean components.

Detect the state. The per-exchange group-delay scatter isn’t visibly bimodal at the 2τ level (two devices × two states smear it). There is no handle on the device to read the state.

What works. In-burst pairing cancels everything common to the burst. The remaining per-exchange restart noise (~0.5 ns) is averaged over bursts, and the carrier phase is immune to it. The real fix needs a second engine or a timing reference that survives the DAC restart, for example a looped TX→RX path on the board, or reading the modem’s DAC-clock phase if such a register exists. This is open.

13.3 On-device processing¶

Firmware v20 adds three commands:

  • tones: a 2048-point fixed-point FFT of one or two periods, CFO de-rotated, with quarter-wave twiddles. The output is 1000 tone values. Validated against the host DFT: D within 0.06–0.3 ns, phase within 1–2°.

  • proc: a reference divide, a multi-lag phase slope and a weighted-LS refinement on device. Each exchange returns D, carrier phase and |H|.

  • fftp: sets the number of periods averaged.

The FFT costs 2.3–3.3 M cycles (~15–20 ms at 160 MHz; soft-float-free but not optimised). The weighted LS with soft-float atan2f dominates proc (~60 M cycles for 4 captures).

13.4 Stitching with one-sided tones¶

TX/RX IQ images of a symmetric comb land on the comb itself, and they biased the 2-LO fit (17.5 ns). With one-sided tones (0.04–19.5 MHz above each LO) at 4 HT40 LOs (2402, 2422, 2442, 2462 MHz → 2402–2481.5 MHz), the images fall on empty bins. The stitched 2-path fit now gives Δ = 15.8 ns (truth 15.0). Single 20 MHz segments give 15.0 to 45 ns (degenerate), and two segments 59 ns. The echo amplitude comes out low: a = 0.17 against 0.6. The injected two-path waveform has a higher crest factor and is peak-normalised, so the TX chain compresses the modulation depth. Spikes near each LO (DC/LO leakage) also remain. The delay is the robust parameter. Amplitude needs TX linearity calibration, or a lower injection level.

13.5 Preamble misses¶

5–13 % per burst over 600 bursts (17/137, 18/137, 57/331), about 2–3 % per exchange, all retried. They cluster at LOs near the rig AP (2432–2442 MHz).

14.1 What one known path can and can’t separate¶

Every two-way observable is device-A sum + device-B sum + 2·path. Each sum is TX + RX latency. With a single known path:

  • Can: the total device constant K = 2τ_meas − 2L/(v_f c). With K, any later measurement gives an absolute range. This holds for the group delay (~cm), and for the carrier phase once the integer is fixed (mm). The reference’s own error is the cable’s uncertainty: ±5 cm of length and v_f 0.66–0.85 give ±0.17 ns one-way, about ±5 cm.

  • Can: separate digital from analog latency. The group delay includes the digital pipelines; the carrier-vs-LO slope doesn’t. That is where the 1918 vs 18.5 ns split comes from.

  • Can: separate role-dependent processing bias (§14.2).

  • Can’t: split A’s latency from B’s, or a device’s TX from its RX. It also can’t give the coax’s velocity factor, or move the device constant between pairs (each pair has its own sum). Every new pair needs its own reference, unless the per-device terms are known.

14.2 Done remotely: role swap¶

Swapping the initiator (A↔B, 24 exchanges each, interleaved twice) moves the group delay by 2.85 ns: 1919.62 vs 1916.77 ns, reproducible to ±0.1 ns. The carrier sum also moves (−78° vs +144°). A physical path is reciprocal, so this is a processing/role term: initiator and reflector engine latencies differ (the DAC start relative to the stamp, and the order of the capture and DAC ops), plus the δ·Treply sign. Averaging both roles removes it: 1918.2 ns. Note also that the absolute group-delay offset depends on the firmware build (1911–1913 ns on v11, 1916.8–1919.6 on v20). The stamp-to-engine latency moves with code layout, so calibrate per firmware build (or stamp in hardware; see §16).

15. Deployment: P4+C6 boards and standalone C6 players¶

15.1 P4+C6 (C6 as the radio front end)¶

The bench firmware runs as is: the C6 has blocks 1+2 to spare, the P4 renders. Raw captures go over SDIO (10+ MB/s, versus ~0.2 MB/s over USB here), so the P4 can run the host-quality DSP (FPU, Hann/onset-aligned windows, per-tone LS). That removes the on-device estimator penalty of §13.3. Rate is then bounded by airtime and the C6’s sequencing: 4 exchanges per 2.4 ms burst, plus ~1 ms of turnaround per burst, gives ~1000 exchanges/s on air. With per-capture SDIO transfer (48 KB per exchange at 10 MB/s ≈ 5 ms) it is ~100–200 exchanges/s: a slip-free absolute fix (~150 exchanges) in about 1 s.

15.2 Standalone C6 player: a ranging mode¶

The constraints from the measurements:

  1. While an engine runs it owns a whole 128 KB SRAM block. The CPU and all DMA must stay out of the entire block.

  2. The DAC waveform and each capture sit at the start of the owned block (or at +64 KB with bit 16).

  3. One engine at a time (DAC or capture).

  4. A burst runs with interrupts off for ~2.4 ms (4 exchanges) up to ~3.6 ms (with LO steps).

  5. The radio leaves normal Wi-Fi operation for the burst.

Design: borrow the netstack’s buffers during a ranging window.

  • Linker placement: put the netstack’s TLS record buffers and the MAC RX ring (≈ 16 + 16 KB, idle while the radio is busy with ranging) at the start of one block, say block 2. Everything else in block 2 is CPU-only data that no ISR or DMA touches (for example, effect bytecode or the map). Every DMA buffer (LED RMT/PARLIO, the Wi-Fi vendor DMA, USB) and every ISR stack/data goes in other blocks. RAM cost: 0 KB extra. The engine region reuses buffers that hold nothing during the window.

  • Ranging window, per burst:

    1. The netstack drains TLS and pauses MAC RX (as background scans do).

    2. Wait for the LED frame to be handed to DMA.

    3. Interrupts off. 4 exchanges with captures kept in place: capture into the borrowed region, then FFT it in place (16 KB work) before the next capture. Each capture is processed or reduced to ~16 bytes before the next one.

    4. Interrupts on, MAC resumed, channel restored.

    A burst costs ~3 ms of radio and CPU, plus the on-device processing (~15–20 ms FFT + estimator per capture today) in normal task context afterwards.

  • Rendering: LED DMA keeps streaming during the window, because its buffers are outside the block. Frame computation stalls ≤ 4 ms, so at 60 fps a burst right after a frame dispatch misses no frame. Nothing reboots. The IWDT limit of 300 ms is far away.

  • Reduced-capture variant (if the netstack buffers can’t be colocated):

    • Reserve only 24 KB permanently: a 2048-word waveform (8 KB) plus one 4096-word capture taken at 40 MS/s (the band is ±20 MHz, so 40 MS/s keeps it), which is 102 µs, 4 periods. Each capture is reduced on device before the next exchange.

    • The cost is 24 KB of RAM, which the 2048-LED configuration (24 KB floor) can’t spare. A ≤1024-LED player can.

    • The accuracy cost is a 2-period window instead of 3, which is ~1.8 dB less SNR. The estimator penalty is §13.3’s ~2× group-delay noise until the onset-aligned window is on device. So integer fixing needs ~2–4× more bursts (≈80–150), or the player settles for group-delay-only ranging at 1–2 cm after averaging.

  • Latency/airtime per absolute fix (C6 player):

    • ~40 bursts over 5 LOs: 40 × ~3 ms = 0.12 s of radio time, plus 5 channel switches (~1 ms each, vendor retune).

    • Spread over 1–2 s in normal operation, that is < 10 % radio duty.

    • Group-delay-only ranging (±5–12 cm) needs one burst: ~3 ms.
