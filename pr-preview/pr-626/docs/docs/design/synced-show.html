A synced show: ~10 fixtures in step for hours¶

Status: drift correction (bridge), shared-time dt (firmware) and scheduled changes (apply_at, C6/P4 firmware + bridge) implemented; the Pi/host player has the time base but doesn’t schedule changes yet; the HITL long-run measurement is planned (below).

Goal¶

A live poetry reading. A speech recogniser turns the reader’s voice into text; a fast LLM scores each phrase semantically (mood, intensity, imagery) and the agent bridge turns the scores into uniform changes (set_uniforms, ramp_uniforms, crossfade_to, set_brightness) on about ten fixtures (ESP32-C6 and P4 players) in one room. Every fixture runs the same effect or complementary ones, and the room must read as ONE light: a pulse that crosses the room, a hue that shifts on every fixture at once. That has to hold for the whole show — hours — with the bridge, the Wi-Fi and the players’ crystals all imperfect.

The render phase is the biggest term left: two players at 30 fps sample the same effect time up to 33 ms apart. A shared time makes each frame show the right picture for when it is drawn; it does not make the frames coincide.

Clock sync and drift correction¶

Once (existing, #3 of agent-multi-fixture.md). align_time (and every multi-player deploy / crossfade) estimates each player’s clock offset with TimeSyncPing/Pong (min-RTT of several rounds), picks ONE room epoch on the bridge’s monotonic clock and gives each player that epoch in its own clock (SetTimeBase). The firmware renders time = (now - epoch) / 1000.

Then nothing corrected drift. Device crystals differ by tens of ppm (±40 ppm is normal for the C6’s 40 MHz crystal): 0.1-0.3 s apart per hour.

The discipline (agent/src/timesync.ts, TimeDiscipline). Every player that took a time base is adopted by the hub’s discipline and, from then on:

  • Re-sampled every 30 s (4 ping rounds, min-RTT; intervalMs), every 5 s while a slew is still under way (slewIntervalMs). The pings are the session’s traffic: they count as signs of life for the heartbeat (liveness.ts), so a disciplined session doesn’t also need its idle heartbeat. They are quiet: serialized with tool calls but not counted as activity, so they never keep an idle session open against the phone app (#444).

  • Fitted (DriftEstimator): weighted least squares of offset over hub time on the last 10 minutes (≤ 24 samples), weights 1/rtt². A sample whose round trip is above 1.5 × the window’s best + 2 ms is kept but not fitted (a retransmit makes its offset wrong by up to rtt/2). The drift rate is only fitted once samples span ≥ 60 s, and clamped to ±500 ppm. A sample off the line by more than its round trip can explain (> 50 ms beyond rtt/2 + the fit’s uncertainty) is a clock discontinuity — a reboot, a reconnect to a re-flashed player, the firmware’s own millis() step guard (lm_clock_observe) — and restarts the model.

  • Corrected by slewing: the wanted epoch is room epoch + predicted offset; the error is applied - wanted. Below 1 ms (deadbandMs; epochs are integer ms): nothing. Up to 50 ms: move the epoch by at most 2 ms per correction (maxStepMs) with a plain SetTimeBase. Each such step is a 1-2 ms step of effect time — invisible — and at one step per 5 s the slew absorbs up to 400 ppm. Above 50 ms (realignMs) or after a discontinuity: re-align at once (one SetTimeBase to the wanted epoch; a reboot has lost the time base anyway).

The protocol has no “adjust the epoch smoothly” message, and it doesn’t need one: firmware shift_time_base(delta) is internal (only the clock-step guard calls it), and SetTimeBase with an epoch 1-2 ms off the last is exactly that shift. Each SetTimeBase costs one small message (and a welcome back); in lock that is one per player per 30 s at most.

What the bridge reports. align_time’s result carries drift and the new read-only time_sync_status tool (also offered to the stage agent) returns, per player: offset_ms, drift_ppm, error_ms (applied - wanted epoch now, with the model’s drift since the last correction), uncertainty_ms (best rtt/2 + fit residual), samples, last_sample_age_s, last_correction (slew / realign, step, error, age), counts of slews / re-aligns / failed samples; and skew_bound_ms, the worst pair: the sum of the two largest |error| + uncertainty. align_time also uses a fresh model’s offset instead of a new one-shot sync (clockFor), so re-aligns (crossfade_to onto the shared epoch) don’t reintroduce a single noisy sample’s error.

The simulation in agent/tests/timesync.test.ts runs ten players at -80..+75 ppm through exponential Wi-Fi jitter with 5 % retransmit spikes for three hours: worst error stays < 10 ms (the same show uncorrected is > 200 ms apart after one hour), every correction is ≤ 2 ms, the fitted rates are within 5 ppm, and a reboot re-aligns within one interval without moving anyone else.

Limits of the discipline.

  • A released session (idle > 45 s with no call, #444) isn’t sampled: the bridge never reopens a socket just to sync. During a show something is always sent (uniform changes, a live show lock, an attached app); after a long silence the first samples slew the accumulated error out (50 ppm × 5 min = 15 ms: ~40 s at 2 ms / 5 s), or re-align when it is beyond 50 ms.

  • Path asymmetry (an AP that buffers one direction) biases a player’s offset by up to rtt/2 and no ping can see it; the HITL measurement below is the check.

Stateful effects¶

time is shared, but an effect that keeps state in update() integrates dt:

state float ph;
void update() { ph = fract(ph + dt * speed); }   // the skill's recommended form

dt used to be the local frame period (millis() deltas, main.cpp). Two players on one epoch then integrate their own crystals (the same tens of ppm the epoch was meant to cancel), and the drift corrections move time but never ph: the phases walk apart exactly as unsynced time used to. And with fixed(dt) (the fast path on the C6) every frame’s dt is truncated to Q16.16, losing ~0.5 tick (8 µs) per frame — ~1 s/hour at 30 fps, a different amount at each player’s frame rate.

Fix (firmware, minimal): dt derives from the shared time base. When a time base is set, main.cpp now asks lm_fx_clock(now, wall_dt, &time, &dt) (firmware/player_app/ffi.rs, time_base_frame) instead of computing time alone:

  • time = (now - epoch) / 1000, held at 0 before the epoch — unchanged;

  • dt = (now - epoch) - (previous frame's now - epoch), i.e. the change of the shared time, so Σ dt over the show is the elapsed shared time on every player — including the bridge’s 1-2 ms slew steps, which move integrated phases exactly as they move time;

  • dt is a whole number of Q16.16 ticks (difference of the shared time’s tick counts), so dt and the VM’s dt_fix mirror are both exact and both telescope: fixed(dt) integrators no longer leak per frame;

  • held at 0 before the epoch (so an epoch set slightly in the future starts every player’s integration at the same instant); 0 on a step back (an epoch moved later by more than a frame: integrators hold instead of rewinding); the wall frame period on a jump forward beyond the frame + 250 ms (a re-align onto an older epoch must not fast-forward an exponential smoother by an hour).

What it changes: only frames rendered while a time base is set, and only dt (the frame-period statistics, the autoscaler, the debugger’s step keep the wall period). Single-fixture use (no time base) is untouched.

What it does not do alone. Integrated state matches only if every player starts integrating at the same shared instant and sees every uniform change (speed) at the same shared instant. The first holds for players that loaded the effect before one epoch; an effect deployed while the epoch is already running starts on each player when its upload lands (a constant offset of speed × arrival skew, no longer growing). Both need scheduled changes — the section below.

Scheduled changes (apply_at)¶

A control change sent to N players applies on arrival, so a room changes spread over each player’s Wi-Fi latency (tens of ms, more under load). With the shared time base (set_time_base, agent/src/hub.ts alignTime) every player already agrees on effect time; apply_at_ms uses the same clock for the change itself.

Protocol. SetUniforms, SetEffect, SetCrossfade and SetBrightness carry optional uint32 apply_at_ms: ms on the time base (effect time, like SetTexture.present_at_ms). Firmware advertises apply_at. Unset, no time base, already due, or more than 10 s ahead (a stale epoch) means apply on arrival, today’s behaviour. The reply goes out on arrival (it shows the state before the change); persistence flags are recorded on arrival too.

Firmware. lm_player_handle holds a scheduled frame, as raw bytes, in SCHED (firmware/player/src/apply_queue.rs: 8 frames sharing 512 B on the C6, 16 / 2 KB on the P4). The render task calls lm_sched_apply(millis()) at the top of every frame, under the player mutex, before anything reads the effect, the crossfade or the brightness. Every frame due by then is applied in due order (ties keep arrival order), through the same apply code as the immediate path. Nothing is dropped, unlike video frames: a late change applies on arrival. When the queue is full, its earliest frame is applied early to make room. A device-clock step moves the queue with the time base (lm_clock_observe). A scheduled live move (seq + commit:false) skips the unlocked live mailbox and is queued like the rest; it is still acked on arrival.

Bridge. set_uniforms, ramp_uniforms, set_effect, crossfade_to and set_brightness take lead_ms. By default the lead is 150 ms when several players are targeted and all of them have apply_at. Otherwise changes apply on arrival, and the result says which players caused that. An explicit lead schedules every player that can take it and names the others. 0 means on arrival. The hub picks one instant at on its own clock and gives each player apply_at = at - epoch, where epoch is the room epoch alignTime gave that player. The player’s clock offset is already folded into its epoch, so the instant is the same everywhere. If a capable target has no epoch yet, the hub aligns first, re-using the epoch the others share so running effects don’t jump. Ramps and crossfades run on one shared timeline: step i is scheduled for at + i·step, sent a lead ahead, and paced on the player’s ack. A player that falls behind skips steps instead of drifting late. The final committed set or landing position is scheduled too. Residual skew is the time-base skew (half the clock-sync round trips) plus at most one render frame.

Gaps not covered yet¶

  • Pi player_rs and the host player don’t schedule changes yet. They follow the shared time base (#498, with --output-latency-ms for DDP/WLED latency) but don’t advertise apply_at, so the bridge sends them changes on arrival.

  • Render frames are not phase-locked. Each player renders on its own frame clock, so two players sample the shared time up to one frame apart (33 ms at 30 fps). For strobes this is the dominant error. A fix would align frame starts to the shared time (render when (now - epoch) mod period ≈ 0, a PLL on the frame timer); not attempted here.

  • float32 effect time loses precision. time is f32 seconds: its resolution is 0.24 ms at 1 h, 1 ms from 2.3 h, 2 ms from 4.6 h, 8 ms at 1.5 days. Effects that use time directly (sin(time * 30)) get visibly coarser after a day of uptime on one epoch. Re-aligning to a new epoch between sets (crossfade with a fresh epoch) resets it; a real fix is a wrapped time or an f64/Q32.32 time mirror.

  • millis() is 32-bit: the device clock wraps after 49.7 days; the epoch math (int64 from a u32 now) breaks across the wrap.

  • Asymmetric Wi-Fi paths bias the offset estimate (see Limits above).

  • Released sessions aren’t disciplined (see Limits above).

Test plan¶

Unit (done).

  • agent/tests/timesync.test.ts: the estimator (rate fit through jitter, retransmit outliers, discontinuity reset, rate clamp) and the discipline on simulated clocks: ten players for three hours with ppm drift and heavy-tailed RTT (worst < 10 ms, every step ≤ 2 ms); the uncorrected control (> 200 ms); a mid-show reboot (re-aligned within one interval, others untouched); a 30 ms error slewed in ≤ 2 ms steps; a 400 ms error re-aligned in one; a silent player retried.

  • firmware/player_app/tests/shared_dt.rs: time_base_frame (held before the epoch, slew-step dts, re-align clamps, dt in whole Q16.16 ticks summing exactly to the elapsed shared time where truncation loses thousands of ticks), three simulated players with different crystals and frame cadences integrating phase += dt for an hour (in step to < 6 ms vs > 200 ms with local dt), and the C ABI path (SetTimeBase → lm_fx_clock → lm_fx_update) integrating phase equal to time through an epoch nudge.

HITL (planned). Extend pi/hitl/harness/hitl_time_base_skew.py (today: two analyzer-tapped DUTs, one capture, timeBaseClock.fx binary clock) into a long run:

  1. Hold every analyzer-tapped DUT the rigs allow (two per rig today; more needs a wider analyzer tap), flash the build under test, load the binary-clock effect and a stateful twin (ph = fract(ph + fixed(dt) * speed) shown as the bit clock) on alternate LEDs.

  2. Align once through the bridge’s hub (not the harness’s own sync), then let the discipline run.

  3. Every 5 minutes for ≥ 3 hours, one analyzer capture of all taps: per DUT the wire-time minus effect-time offset (time_base_skew_core), for both the time clock and the integrated one; record time_sync_status alongside.

  4. Pass: p99 pairwise skew of both clocks < 10 ms + one frame period at every capture, no growth over the run, every reported skew_bound_ms ≥ the measured skew. Mid-run, reset one DUT and check it is back within budget after one interval while the others’ captures don’t move.

  5. Control run with the discipline off (drift: false): the skew grows ~ppm × t.

Cross-rig DUTs share no analyzer clock, so a ten-player run needs either one rig with a wider tap or a common reference (a photodiode or GPIO pulse captured on both analyzers).
